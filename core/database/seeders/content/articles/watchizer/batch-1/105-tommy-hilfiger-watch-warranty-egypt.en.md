## What the warranty covers

The warranty covers manufacturing defects in the materials and workmanship of the movement and its parts, under normal use. In practice that means the mechanism that keeps time and drives the hands: if the watch stops with a good battery, loses or gains time far beyond normal, or a function such as the date or the chronograph stops working because of a fault, that is what the warranty is for.

For a chronograph such as the TH85, the stopwatch mechanism is part of the movement, so a pusher that no longer starts or resets the chronograph because of an internal fault is covered too. The same is true of a date that no longer changes at midnight on a three-hand Kane or Daniel.

What decides whether a problem is covered is its cause. A defect that comes from how the watch was made is covered; damage that comes from what happened to the watch afterwards is not. When you bring a watch in, the cause is assessed, and we tell you the result before any work starts.

Some things that look like faults are not. A seconds hand that jumps in four-second steps is the watch's low-battery signal, not a broken movement. A chronograph hand that does not return exactly to zero after a battery change usually needs resetting rather than repairing. A date that seems to change slowly is normal: on many quartz watches the date wheel starts to move a little before midnight and settles a little after it. If you are unsure whether something is a fault, ask us on WhatsApp before you bring the watch in; a short video often answers the question.

## What the warranty does not cover

Several parts and situations are outside the warranty, and knowing them in advance avoids disappointment. The battery itself is not covered, although we replace it free of charge within the warranty. The strap or bracelet, the glass and the case with its plating are not covered either, because they are exposed to daily wear.

Water damage is not covered when the watch's water-resistance rating, 50 m on most models, and its care instructions were not followed. Damage from accidents, misuse or lack of care, such as a cracked glass after a fall or a dented case, is not covered, and nor is normal wear such as scratches on the case or bracelet. Finally, a watch that has been opened or repaired by anyone other than the brand's approved service loses its warranty.

None of this means that a damaged watch cannot be repaired. Work outside the warranty is done for a fee, and we always quote it before we start, so you decide whether to go ahead.

## Water resistance and the warranty

Most Tommy Hilfiger watches are rated to 50 metres, often written 5 ATM on the case back. That rating sounds deep, but it is a laboratory test, not a diving depth. In daily terms, 50 m allows a short pool swim, while 30 m (3 ATM) means splashes and rain only, and 100 m (10 ATM) or more is fine for the sea.

Because water damage outside the rating is not covered, a few habits protect both the watch and the warranty. Never press the chronograph pushers or pull out the crown under water. Rinse the watch in fresh water after the sea or a chlorinated pool. Avoid hot showers, saunas and steam rooms, where heat and steam can pass seals that resist cold water. And if you ever see condensation inside the glass, bring the watch in at once rather than waiting.

## Keeping the warranty valid

Keeping a warranty valid for its full term, 3 years on most models, comes down to three habits. Keep the papers: the warranty card and the invoice, ideally with a photo of each on your phone in case the originals are lost. Let only us, or the brand's approved service, open or repair the watch. And follow the care basics for its water rating and its strap.

It also helps to act early. A watch that starts losing time, a date that sticks or a pusher that feels different is easier to diagnose while the fault is small. Bringing it in quickly, rather than after months of wear, makes it simpler to show that the problem comes from the movement and not from an accident.

Straps and bracelets deserve their own care, because they are outside the warranty. Keep leather straps away from water and perfume, rinse silicone and rubber straps after sport or the beach, and wipe steel bracelets with a soft, dry cloth after a hot day. If a strap wears out after a few years of daily use, a replacement can be arranged for a fee, quoted before any work starts, and the watch keeps its warranty on the movement for the rest of its term.
