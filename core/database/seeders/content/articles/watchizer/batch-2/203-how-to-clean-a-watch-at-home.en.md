You can clean almost any watch at home with a soft cloth, a soft toothbrush, lukewarm water and a drop of mild soap, as long as you match the method to the strap and respect the water rating. A steel bracelet can be washed; a leather strap must only be wiped; rubber and silicone can be rinsed; and the case, glass and crown need a light touch whatever the watch. Ten minutes once a month keeps a watch looking new and helps the seals last longer. This guide walks through each part step by step, lists the things never to do, and explains when a watch is better brought to one of our stores than cleaned at home. We currently carry {{count family=watch}} watches, and the advice below applies to all of them.

## Key points

- Check the water rating on the product page before letting any water near the watch.
- Steel bracelets: soft toothbrush, lukewarm soapy water, rinse, dry thoroughly.
- Leather straps: wipe with a barely damp cloth, never soak, dry away from heat.
- Rubber and silicone: rinse after sweat or the sea, wash with mild soap once a week.
- Never use alcohol, bleach, glass cleaner or an ultrasonic bath on a watch with leather or a fragile coating.

## What you need before you start

You do not need special products. Gather a soft microfibre cloth, the kind used for glasses, a second dry cloth or a paper towel, a soft toothbrush that you will keep only for the watch, a small bowl of lukewarm water, and a drop of mild washing-up liquid or a pH-neutral hand soap. For leather, add a leather conditioner if you have one; it is optional. For tight corners, a wooden toothpick with its tip wrapped in a thin layer of cloth is safer than anything metal.

Before anything touches the watch, make sure the crown is pushed fully in, or screwed down if your model has a screw-down crown. The crown is the main door for water, and a crown left pulled out after setting the time is the most common reason a watch fogs up after cleaning. If the watch has a chronograph, do not touch the pushers while it is wet.

Finally, look up the water rating. A watch rated 30 m, or 3 ATM, resists splashes and rain but should not go under the tap. A watch rated 50 m can be rinsed briefly. Watches rated 100 m and above can be washed under running water. The rating is on every product page at Watchizer and usually on the case back.

## Cleaning a stainless steel bracelet

Steel bracelets collect sweat, dust and skin between their links, which dulls the shine and can make the bracelet feel rough. If your watch is rated 50 m or more, the easiest method is to clean the bracelet attached to the watch, holding the case so it does not go under water. Dip the toothbrush in the soapy water, shake off the excess, and brush along the links and into the gaps, top and bottom, including the clasp.

Rinse with a damp cloth, or under a gentle stream of lukewarm water if the rating allows, then dry immediately with the microfibre cloth. Pay attention to the clasp and the gaps between links, where water hides; leave the watch on a towel, clasp open, for 30 minutes so everything dries completely. A bracelet that stays damp can develop a dull film over time.

If your watch has a lower rating, or you prefer to be careful, our stores can remove the bracelet and clean it separately in a few minutes. Many owners of steel watches, such as the [Hugo Boss](/brand/hugo-boss) and Tommy Hilfiger models on bracelets, find a monthly wash is enough, and a quick wipe with a dry cloth every evening keeps fingerprints away.

## Caring for a leather strap

Leather and water do not mix. Water makes leather swell, stiffen and lose colour, and sweat speeds up the process. Never rinse, soak or wash a leather strap. Instead, wipe it with a cloth that is barely damp, almost dry, to remove dust and sweat, then dry it at once with a dry cloth. Let it rest flat, away from the sun, a heater or a car dashboard, which crack the surface.

Every few months, a small amount of leather conditioner rubbed in with a soft cloth keeps the leather supple and slows the darkening around the holes and the buckle. Test it first on the inside of the strap. If the strap has a strong smell or has darkened badly from sweat, it is usually time for a new one; leather is a wearing part, like the soles of shoes.

In the Egyptian summer, a leather strap ages faster. Many customers keep their leather watch for the cooler months and switch to steel, rubber or silicone from June to September. Our stores can fit a new strap of the right width; most watches use 18, 20 or 22 mm, and the product page or our team can tell you which.

## Rubber and silicone straps

Rubber and silicone are the easiest straps to clean, which is why they suit sport and summer. After the gym or a hot day, rinse the strap with fresh water and dry it with a cloth. Once a week, wash it with a drop of mild soap and the toothbrush, especially along the inside where sweat and sun cream collect, then rinse and dry.

After the sea or a chlorinated pool, rinse the whole watch with fresh water as soon as you can, if its rating allows, because salt and chlorine attack seals and leave a white film on the strap. A white or light-coloured silicone strap can pick up dye from new jeans; a little soap and patience usually removes it, but avoid harsh solvents, which damage the material.

Do not use oils or silicone sprays to make a rubber strap shine; they attract dust and can make the strap sticky. A strap that has hardened, cracked or lost its colour after a few summers simply needs replacing, and our stores fit new rubber straps for many models, including the Michael Kors and sport chronographs we sell.

## The case, the glass and the crown

The case collects dirt in three places: around the bezel, between the lugs, and around the crown. Use the toothbrush, barely damp with soapy water, in small circles for about 1 minute, then wipe with a damp cloth and dry. The cloth-wrapped toothpick reaches the gap between the case and the bracelet or strap. Never push anything sharp into the gap around the crown or the pushers.

For the glass, a dry microfibre cloth is usually enough. If there is a smudge, breathe on the glass and wipe, as you would with spectacles. Avoid glass cleaners and household sprays, because their chemicals can damage the anti-reflective coating some watches carry and can seep into the seals. A scratch on mineral glass cannot be polished away at home; our team can tell you whether the glass is worth replacing.

Finish by checking that the crown is still pushed in. Then wear the watch, or leave it on a dry towel for half an hour, so any trace of moisture evaporates from the edges before you put it in a box.

## What never to do

A few habits damage watches more than years of wear. Never use alcohol, acetone, bleach, vinegar or glass cleaner on a watch, not even 1 time: they dissolve the coating on plated cases, dry out leather and attack the rubber seals. Never use a hair dryer or put a watch on a heater to dry it, since heat expands the seals and lets moisture in. Never open the case back yourself to clean inside.

Be careful with ultrasonic cleaners sold for jewellery. They can work for a steel bracelet removed from the watch, but they can loosen parts, damage leather and push water into a case. And do not clean a watch that has fog under the glass: fog means water is already inside, and the watch needs to be dried and resealed professionally, the sooner the better.

Gold-tone and rose gold plated cases deserve extra gentleness, and so do watches with crystal-set bezels, where a stiff brush can loosen a stone. The plating is thin; scrubbing or abrasive cloths wear it away at the edges. A soft cloth and a little soapy water are all a plated watch needs.

## How often to clean

For a watch worn every day, a quick wipe with a dry cloth in the evening removes sweat and fingerprints and takes ten seconds. A proper clean of the bracelet or strap, the case and the glass, as described above, once a month is enough for most people. Put a reminder on your phone for the first Friday of each month; it takes 10 minutes, and after a few months it becomes a habit as natural as polishing shoes before an important day. In summer, or after the beach or sport, rinse rubber and steel sooner, the same day if possible.

A watch you wear only on special occasions still benefits from a wipe before you store it, because dried sweat and perfume work slowly on the surface. Store it in its box, dial up, away from sunlight and damp bathrooms, and never in a drawer next to a phone or a speaker, whose magnets can disturb an automatic movement. Perfume, in particular, should go on before the watch and be allowed to dry first.

Cleaning is also a good moment to check the watch, with the same soft light you used to see the dirt: is the bracelet loose, is a pin working its way out, does the crown turn smoothly, is the strap cracked? Catching these small things early saves a lost pin or a broken strap later.

## Cleaning after the beach, the pool and travel

The Egyptian summer is the hardest season for a watch. Salt water, chlorine, sand and sun cream all attack the strap, the seals and the finish, and the heat speeds everything up. The rule is simple: rinse the same day. If your watch is rated 100 m or more, hold it under a gentle stream of fresh water for 30 seconds, crown pushed in, then dry it with a cloth, paying attention to the bezel and the clasp, where sand hides.

If the rating is lower, wipe the watch with a cloth dampened in fresh water instead, then dry it. Sand trapped under a rotating bezel can be loosened by turning the bezel while rinsing, on watches made to be rinsed. Never press the pushers of a chronograph until it is completely dry. And do not leave the watch on a sun lounger or a car dashboard; at 50 degrees and more, strap colours fade and seals age.

When you travel, keep the watch in a soft pouch rather than loose in a bag with keys and coins, which scratch steel and glass. After a long trip, give it the full monthly clean described above; it is the easiest way to remove a week of sun cream, dust and sweat at once.

## Frequently asked questions

### Can I wash my watch under the tap?

Only if its water rating is 50 m or more, and only with lukewarm water, the crown pushed in and the pushers untouched. Watches rated 30 m should be wiped with a damp cloth instead. Leather straps should never go under the tap, whatever the rating; wipe them with a barely damp cloth and dry them at once.

### How do I remove the smell from a leather strap?

Wipe the strap with a barely damp cloth and a tiny amount of mild soap, then dry it and let it air flat away from heat for a day. A leather conditioner can help afterwards. If the smell comes back quickly, the leather is saturated with sweat, and replacing the strap is the lasting solution.

### Is toothpaste good for cleaning a watch?

No. Toothpaste is mildly abrasive and can scratch polished steel, wear away plating on gold-tone cases and leave residue in the gaps between links. A soft toothbrush with lukewarm water and a drop of mild soap cleans just as well without the risk. Keep a separate toothbrush only for the watch.

### Why is there fog inside my watch after cleaning?

Fog means moisture got inside, usually through a crown that was not pushed fully in or through ageing seals. Do not try to dry it with heat. Bring the watch to one of our stores as soon as possible, where it can be dried, checked and resealed before the moisture affects the movement.

### How often should I clean my watch?

Wipe it with a dry cloth every evening if you wear it daily, and give the bracelet or strap, the case and the glass a proper clean about once a month. Rinse rubber and steel the same day after the sea, the pool or sport, if the water rating allows, to remove salt, chlorine and sweat.
