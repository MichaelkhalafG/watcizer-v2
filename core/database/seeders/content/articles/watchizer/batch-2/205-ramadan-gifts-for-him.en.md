## Why a watch makes a good Ramadan gift

A watch is worn every day and seen by everyone at the iftar table, so it carries the giver's thought long after the month ends. Unlike clothes, it needs no exact size beyond a bracelet adjustment that takes a few minutes in store, and unlike perfume, it does not depend on a scent preference that is hard to guess. A well-chosen watch also lasts years, which suits the spirit of a gift given during a month of reflection and family.

There is a practical side too. Ramadan changes the rhythm of the day: suhoor before dawn, the fast, iftar at sunset, and evenings that run late. A clear, reliable watch helps keep track of all of it without reaching for a phone, especially at the mosque or during a family gathering. Many men find themselves checking the time more often in Ramadan than at any other time of year, counting the minutes to the maghrib call or making sure they leave the office in time to reach the family table. A watch on the wrist makes those small checks quicker and calmer, and it keeps the phone in the pocket during prayers and family conversations.

Finally, a watch is easy to match to a relationship. A classic steel watch suits a father; a sporty chronograph suits a brother; a slim dress watch suits a husband who wears suits to work. The next sections explain how to choose for each.

## For a father: classic and readable

For a father, think about comfort and clarity. A watch with a white, silver or blue dial and clear hands is easy to read without glasses; a steel bracelet or a soft leather strap is comfortable all day. A case of 40 to 42 mm suits most men's wrists and looks dignified without being flashy. A date window is a small but welcome detail for a father who still signs papers or keeps appointments, and luminous hands help him read the time at suhoor without turning on the light.

Quartz movements make life easy: they keep accurate time, need only a battery every 2 years or so, and do not stop if the watch is left in a drawer for a few days. If your father enjoys mechanical things, an automatic watch with a display back that shows the movement can be a wonderful surprise; our [automatic watches page](/subtypes/automatic) gathers those models.

Brands with a calm, classic style, such as the steel three-hand models from Tommy Hilfiger and Hugo Boss, are safe choices. If he already owns a watch he loves, choose a different style rather than a similar one: a leather strap if his watch is on steel, or a blue dial if his is black.

## For a husband: smart and versatile

A husband's gift is often worn to work, to family visits and to evenings out, so versatility matters most. A steel watch with a blue or black dial on a steel bracelet works with a suit, a shirt and jeans, or the white galabeya many men wear for Eid prayers. If he wears suits, a slim dress watch on leather is the elegant choice.

Look at what he already wears. If his belt buckle and cufflinks are silver, choose a steel case; if he wears gold-tone accessories, a gold-tone or two-tone watch completes the look. If he is sporty, a chronograph on steel or rubber is a better match than a dress watch, and it is easy to wear on weekends. Think also about his work: a man who spends his day at a desk can wear a slim watch on leather, while one who drives or works on site will be happier with steel, which shrugs off knocks and heat.

A watch from the same brand as his favourite wallet or belt creates a coordinated set, which makes the gift feel planned. Our [Hugo Boss page](/brand/hugo-boss) and [Tommy Hilfiger page](/brand/tommy-hilfiger) show both watches and accessories, so you can build a pair from 1 brand.

## For a brother or a son: sporty and bold

Younger men usually prefer sportier watches: chronographs, bold dials, rubber or silicone straps, and cases of 42 to 46 mm. Colour is welcome; blue, green and black dials with red or orange accents are popular, and an all-black watch is a favourite with men in their twenties who like a modern, understated look. A chronograph with a tachymeter suits a car lover, and a light watch on silicone suits someone who goes to the gym every evening after iftar.

Budget-friendly bold designs from Naviforce and Mini Focus are favourites with students, while Tommy Hilfiger and Guess offer sporty styles with recognised names. For a son starting his first job, a steel watch that works with a shirt and a suit is a gift he will keep for many years. For a teenager, a digital or analogue-digital watch with an alarm and a stopwatch is practical for school, exams and football, and it survives the bumps of a busy day.

If you are not sure of his wrist size, choose a model on a steel bracelet, which can be shortened in our stores, or on silicone, which adjusts easily. Our [sport watches page](/subtypes/sport) gathers the tough, sporty side of the range.

## Wallet and belt sets: the safe choice

When you do not know someone well, or you need several gifts for colleagues or relatives, a wallet and belt set is the safest choice. Each set pairs a leather wallet with a matching belt in the same colour and logo family, presented in a gift box ready to give. There is no style to guess and almost no size to get wrong, since most belts can be shortened.

Calvin Klein sets are minimal, mostly black with the CK monogram, which suits men who prefer a quiet style. Tommy Hilfiger sets add the brand's grid or stripe and come in black, brown and navy. Black is the safest colour for any man; brown suits someone who wears casual clothes and brown shoes. Check the belt width as well: most sets carry a belt of about 3.5 cm, which fits standard trouser loops, and a reversible belt gives 2 colours in 1.

A single wallet is a good alternative for a younger man or a student, and it is easy to tuck inside an Eid card: a slim card holder for a front pocket, or a bifold with space for cash. ## How to choose a watch without knowing his taste

Look for clues in what he already wears. The colour of his belt and shoes tells you whether leather should be black or brown. The metal of his ring, cufflinks or glasses frame tells you whether steel or gold-tone suits him. The watch he already owns, if any, tells you his preferred size; aim for something within 2 mm of it.

If you have no clues at all, choose the most versatile option: a steel watch of 40 to 42 mm with a blue or black dial on a steel bracelet. It suits almost every man, every outfit and every occasion. Avoid very large, very small or very colourful watches unless you know he likes them. You can also ask someone close to him, his wife, a sister or a friend, for a quick photo of his wrist or his current watch; it removes most of the guesswork in a minute.

Choose something he would not buy for himself: a watch in a different style from the one he wears, an automatic watch with a visible movement, or a premium wallet and belt set. A personal card explaining why you chose it makes even a simple accessory feel special.

Yes, a watch is one of the most meaningful gifts for a father. Choose a classic, readable design with a white, silver or blue dial, a comfortable steel bracelet or leather strap, and a case of 40 to 42 mm. A quartz movement keeps it simple, with only a battery change every 2 years or so.
