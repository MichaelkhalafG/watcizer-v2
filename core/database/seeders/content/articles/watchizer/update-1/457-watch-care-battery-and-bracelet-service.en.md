## Key points

- Wipe your watch with a soft, dry cloth after a hot day and put perfume on before you put the watch on.
- Change a quartz battery as soon as the seconds hand starts jumping in 4-second steps, so a weak battery never leaks.
- Clean steel bracelets with a soft brush; size them by removing links, free within the warranty at our 2 stores.

## Everyday habits

Most of what keeps a watch looking new takes seconds. After a hot day, wipe the case, the glass and the bracelet or strap with a soft, dry cloth, which removes sweat and dust before they settle into the links or the stitching. A microfibre cloth of the kind used for glasses is ideal and costs almost nothing.

Put perfume, hand cream and sunscreen on first, and the watch on last. These products contain alcohol and oils that dull plating, stain leather and leave a film on the glass. If they do reach the watch, wipe it as soon as you can. Take the watch off before heavy work with your hands, such as moving furniture or gardening, where a knock or a scratch is likely.

Wear the watch where it is comfortable: a little above the wrist bone, with enough room to slide a finger under the bracelet. A bracelet that is too tight marks the skin and traps sweat; one that is too loose lets the watch slide and knock against things.

Five minutes a week catch small problems before they become repairs.

## The battery

Almost every fashion watch is quartz and runs on a small battery that usually lasts 2 to 3 years, a little less in a chronograph whose stopwatch is used often. Many quartz watches warn you when the battery is low: the seconds hand starts jumping in 4-second steps instead of one. That is your signal to have the battery changed soon.

Do not leave a dead or weak battery in a watch for months. An old battery can leak and damage the movement, which turns a quick, inexpensive battery change into a repair. If you put a watch away for a long time, it is worth having the battery removed or changed before you store it.

Have the battery changed by us or by a competent watchmaker who will check the seal of the case back afterwards, so the watch keeps its water resistance. Within the warranty we replace batteries free of charge at our store at Arkadia Mall and our branch on El-Thawra Street in Heliopolis, usually while you wait. After a battery change, a chronograph's stopwatch hands sometimes need resetting to zero, which takes a minute.

Keep the date on quartz watches in mind too. Many fashion watches show a date that does not know how many days a month has, so it needs correcting after a 30-day month and after February. Pull the crown to the first position and turn it until the right date appears, avoiding the hours around midnight, when the date mechanism is working.

## Steel bracelets

Stainless steel bracelets are durable and easy to keep, but dust and sweat collect between the links over time. Every few weeks, brush the bracelet gently with a soft toothbrush. If the watch is rated to at least 50 m (5 ATM), you can use a little lukewarm water with a drop of mild soap, then rinse and dry it with a soft cloth; with a lower rating, use a slightly damp cloth instead and keep water away from the case.

A steel bracelet is sized by removing or adding links, which are held by small pins or screws. Within the warranty we size bracelets free of charge at both our stores, usually in a few minutes. It is worth having a link added back if your wrist changes with the season: a bracelet that fits in January may feel tight in August, when wrists swell slightly in the heat.

Polished steel shows fine scratches with time; brushed steel hides them better. Both are normal signs of wear and do not affect the watch. If the clasp starts opening by itself, bring the watch in: a loose clasp is easy to adjust and much better fixed than a watch lost on the street.

## Mesh bracelets

A mesh bracelet, the fine woven band found on many women's watches such as the Michael Kors Runway Slim and some men's watches, is light and comfortable, and most adjust with a sliding clasp that you can set at home. Its fine weave can trap dust and skin cream, so brush it gently now and then with a soft toothbrush, and rinse it only if the watch's water rating allows.

To adjust a mesh bracelet, lift the small lever on the clasp, slide it to the position you want and press it down firmly until it clicks. Do not force the lever with a sharp tool, which can scratch the mesh; a fingernail or the edge of a coin is enough. If the clasp no longer holds, bring the watch in and we will check it.

Mesh is also gentle on clothes, because it has no edges to catch on knitwear or silk, which makes it a good choice with long sleeves. On the other hand, it can pinch fine hair on the wrist if it is worn very tight, so leave a little room. Gold-tone and rose gold mesh keep their colour best when wiped after wear, before perfume and cream can settle in the weave.

## Leather straps

Leather is warm, comfortable and elegant, but it does not like water, sweat or perfume. Keep leather straps dry: take the watch off before washing your hands thoroughly, swimming or the shower, and avoid wearing leather during sport or on the hottest days of summer. If a leather strap gets wet, let it dry naturally at room temperature, never on a radiator or in direct sun.

Rotate leather straps if you can, giving each one 1 day of rest, which lets it dry and keep its shape. Fasten the buckle in the same hole each time, so the leather does not stretch in several places. With daily use, a leather strap is a part that wears out, and replacing it after a while is normal care, not a fault; the warranty covers the movement, not the strap.

A leather strap that has darkened or cracked can be replaced, and the right replacement keeps the watch looking as it did on the first day. Bring the watch in and we will tell you what fits; work outside the warranty is quoted before it starts. A second strap for summer, in rubber or silicone, spares the leather during the hottest months.

## Rubber and silicone straps

Rubber and silicone straps are the practical choice for heat, sport and water. They do not absorb sweat, they keep their shape and they rinse clean. After the pool or the sea, rinse the strap and the case in fresh water, especially around the buckle and the lugs, where salt and chlorine collect, then dry it with a soft towel.

Light-coloured silicone straps can pick up colour from dark denim or new clothes; a little mild soap and water usually removes it. Keep rubber away from solvents and strong cleaning products, which can harden it, and away from long hours of direct sun when you are not wearing it.

## Water and heat

Water resistance is a rating, not a promise. A watch rated to 30 m (3 ATM) is for splashes and rain only; 50 m (5 ATM) allows a short pool swim; 100 m (10 ATM) or more is fine for the sea. Whatever the rating, never pull out the crown or press a chronograph's pushers under water, and make sure the crown is pushed in fully before you wash your hands.

Heat is the hidden enemy of seals. Hot showers, saunas, steam rooms and hot tubs expand the gaskets and let steam pass where cold water would not. Take the watch off before any of them. And if you ever see condensation inside the glass, bring the watch in at once rather than waiting; moisture inside a case can damage the movement within days.

Summer in Egypt is hard on watches in another way: a watch left on a car dashboard or in direct sun can become very hot, which ages seals and fades dials and straps. Keep it in a bag or a pocket rather than on a sunny surface, and never leave it in a closed car in the afternoon. A sudden change from heat to air conditioning can also cause a faint mist on the glass of an older watch, another sign that its seals need checking.

Seals age too. If you swim with your watch often, have its water resistance checked every year or two, and always after a battery change has opened the case. We can tell you whether your model's case can be tested.

## Automatic watches

An automatic watch needs the same everyday care as any watch, plus two habits of its own. Wear it regularly, or wind it through the crown if the model allows, so it stays running; an automatic left off the wrist for longer than its power reserve, often a day or two, stops and needs setting. And have it serviced every few years, when a watchmaker cleans, oils and adjusts the movement.

Automatics are also more sensitive to magnets and knocks than quartz watches, so they deserve the most care in those two areas. Models such as the Fossil skeleton automatics or the Tissot PRX Powermatic 80 show their movement and reward that care with years of reliable running.

## Magnets, knocks and storage

Strong magnets can disturb a watch's timekeeping. Mechanical watches are the most sensitive, because a magnetised balance spring makes them run fast, but quartz watches can be affected too. Keep watches away from speakers, tablet covers, phone cases with magnetic clasps and magnetic bag closures, especially when you put the watch down for the night.

Knocks are the most common cause of damage. A fall on a hard floor can crack the glass or disturb the movement, and door frames and desks are the usual culprits for dents. When you travel, keep the watch in a soft pouch inside your bag rather than loose, 1 watch per pouch, so it never rubs against keys, coins or other jewellery.

At home, store watches in a box or a drawer lined with soft fabric, away from direct sun, which fades leather and coloured dials. If you own an automatic, wear it regularly or wind it before you put it away for a long time; if you store a quartz watch for months, consider having the battery removed.

Keep the box and papers somewhere safe, even if you do not use the box for storage. The invoice and the warranty card are what you need for a warranty claim, a free battery change or bracelet sizing within the warranty, and they are useful if you ever sell or give the watch away. A photo of each on your phone is a sensible backup.

## Frequently asked questions

### How often should I change my watch battery?

Usually every 2 to 3 years, a little sooner for a chronograph whose stopwatch is used often. Change it as soon as the seconds hand starts jumping in 4-second steps, so a weak battery never leaks inside the case. Within the warranty we replace batteries free of charge at our two Cairo stores, usually while you wait.

### How do I clean a steel watch bracelet?

Brush it gently every few weeks with a soft toothbrush. If the watch is rated to at least 50 m, you can use lukewarm water with a drop of mild soap, then rinse and dry it with a soft cloth. With a lower rating, use a slightly damp cloth and keep water away from the case.

### Can I wear a leather strap in the shower?

No. Leather does not like water, sweat or soap, and hot water and steam also stress the case's seals. Take the watch off before showering, swimming or washing your hands thoroughly. If a leather strap gets wet, let it dry naturally at room temperature, away from heaters and direct sun.

### Do you size watch bracelets?

Yes. Within the warranty we size steel bracelets free of charge at our store at Arkadia Mall and our branch on El-Thawra Street in Heliopolis, usually in a few minutes while you wait. Bring the watch with its invoice. Mesh bracelets usually adjust with a sliding clasp that you can set at home.

### What should I do if my watch has condensation inside?

Bring it in at once, rather than waiting for it to clear. Condensation means moisture has entered the case, often through a seal that has aged or been stressed by heat, and it can damage the movement within days. Do not open the watch yourself; we will check it and tell you what is needed.
