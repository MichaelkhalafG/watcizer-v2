To tell whether a watch is original, check it the way a watchmaker would: confirm that its model number exists and matches the watch, look closely at the finish of the case back, the dial and the clasp, test every function, and judge the seller's paperwork and price. Each brand has its own numbering and details, but the method is the same whatever the name on the dial. We sell {{count family=watch}} watches at Watchizer, and this guide explains the checks that work for every brand we carry, with the details that differ between them.

## Key points

- Every major fashion brand uses a model number in a fixed format; it should exist, match the watch and appear on the case back or the paperwork.
- A clean, even finish on the case back, dial, hands and clasp is one of the strongest signs of an original.
- Test the functions: the crown, the date and any chronograph should work exactly as the model's description says.
- Paperwork and the seller matter more than the box: an invoice, a stated warranty and a return period.
- A price far below the usual range for the model is the clearest warning sign of all.

## Why the method matters more than the brand

Copies of popular watches are made to pass a quick glance, so a quick glance is not enough to judge them. What they rarely survive is a slower, systematic check: the model number, the finish in the places nobody photographs, the functions, the paperwork and the price, taken together. One weak detail can have an innocent explanation; several weak details together almost never do.

The good news is that the method is the same for every brand. Whether the dial says Tommy Hilfiger, Michael Kors, Hugo Boss, Emporio Armani, Guess, Fossil or Diesel, the same questions apply, and only the details of the model number and the finish change. Once you have checked one watch carefully, the next one takes a few minutes.

It helps to do the checks in a fixed order, so you do not skip one when a watch looks convincing. Start with the seller and the price, because they can save you the trouble of checking at all. Then the model number, then the case back, the dial, the functions and the bracelet, and finally the paperwork. Write the model number down before the watch arrives, so you can compare it at the door without searching on your phone.

## The model number: every brand has a format

Fashion watch brands identify each model with a reference number, and each brand uses its own format. Tommy Hilfiger uses seven digits, usually beginning with 17, such as 1710401. Hugo Boss also uses seven digits, beginning with 151, such as 1513762. Emporio Armani uses the letters AR followed by digits, such as AR2448. Guess uses GW followed by digits and letters, such as GW0500G1. Michael Kors uses MK followed by four digits, and Fossil and Diesel use two letters followed by four digits.

The number should do three things. It should exist: searching it online should show the same model on the brand's site or on reputable retailers. It should match: the dial colour, the case and the strap of the watch in front of you should be the ones that number describes. And it should appear where the brand puts it, most often engraved on the case back, and on the seller's listing or invoice.

A copy typically fails one of these tests. It may carry no number at all, a number belonging to a different model, or the same number on watches of different colours. If the number does not exist, or does not match the watch, stop there.

Be aware that some sellers write a correct model number in their listing and then send a different watch. That is why the number on the listing is only half of the check: the other half is the number on the case back of the watch you receive, and the two must be the same. A seller who cannot photograph the case back for you before you order has usually got a reason.

## The case back

Turn the watch over. Original fashion watches have a stainless steel case back with clean, evenly spaced engraving: usually the brand name, the model number, the material and the water resistance, often written as 3 ATM, 5 ATM or 50 M. The letters should have the same depth all the way round, and nothing should be printed on a sticker or smudged.

Look at how the case back is fitted. Screws, if there are any, should sit flush and evenly; a press-fit back should sit tight without gaps. The edge between the case and the back should be clean. Scratches or tool marks around the edge of a new watch suggest it has been opened, which is worth a question even on a genuine watch, because an unapproved opening can affect the warranty.

## The dial and the hands

The dial is where poor copies show themselves most clearly, because fine printing is expensive. On an original, the brand name and logo are sharp, perfectly centred and evenly spaced from the markers. Applied markers sit straight and are firmly fixed; printed ones have crisp edges. The hands reach the markers they point to and move without touching each other or the glass.

Look at the date window, if there is one, as on a Tommy Hilfiger Kane 1710401: the number should be centred and straight in its window, and it should change around midnight. On a chronograph, every sub-dial should have a job. A sub-dial whose hand never moves, whatever you press, is one of the most common signs of a fake chronograph. Compare the dial colour with the brand's photos of the same model number; a navy dial that is plainly black, or a silver one that is plainly white, is not the model it claims to be.

Look at the glass too. A clean, clear glass that sits flush in the case is normal; a glass with distortion when you tilt the watch, or a visible gap at the edge, is not. Many fashion watches use mineral glass and some use sapphire; the description says which, and a scratch on a brand-new sapphire glass would be very unusual.

## The movement and the functions

Almost every fashion watch is quartz, powered by a battery. On a quartz watch the seconds hand moves in one step every second; that tick is normal. A smoothly sweeping seconds hand on a watch sold as quartz means it has a different movement from the one the model uses. Automatics, which are mechanical, do sweep, and their descriptions say so.

Test every function. Pull the crown out to set the time and push it back; the watch should restart at once. On a chronograph such as the Tommy Hilfiger TH85, the stopwatch hand should return exactly to zero. If the model has a date or a day, change it. On a chronograph, start, stop and reset the stopwatch with the pushers, and check that the minute counter moves after a full minute. A function that does not work, or works differently from the description, is a reason to return the watch.

Keep an eye on the first week too. A good quartz watch should gain or lose only a few seconds in that time, roughly 15 seconds a month at most. If it is minutes out after a few days, or stops with a new battery, the movement is not what it should be, and the seller should take the watch back or repair it under the warranty.

## The bracelet, the strap and the weight

An original steel bracelet has solid links that fit tightly without rattling, smooth edges and a clasp that closes with a firm click and stays closed. The clasp usually carries the brand name or logo, cleanly engraved or embossed. Rubber and silicone straps should feel dense and flexible, with a cleanly moulded logo; leather straps should have even stitching and finished edges.

Weight is a hint rather than proof. A stainless steel case and bracelet feel solid in the hand; a watch that feels surprisingly light for its size may use a cheaper alloy. Compare it with a steel watch you already own if you are unsure. ## Box, papers and warranty

The box is the least reliable sign. Boxes, booklets and bags are easy to copy, and original watches are sometimes sold without their outer box, for instance after being used for display. Treat the box as a pleasant extra rather than as proof in either direction.

What matters is the paperwork. An invoice that names the seller, the date and the model number proves where the watch came from. A warranty with a stated length and provider proves that someone stands behind it. ## The price and the seller

Price is the clearest signal. Fashion watch prices in Egypt are shaped by each brand's distribution, so established sellers sit in a broadly similar range for the same model. A watch offered at a small fraction of that range, with a story about a stock clearance or a relative abroad, is very rarely original.

The seller is the other half of the picture. An established seller has an address you can visit, a phone number that answers, written terms for returns and warranty, and an invoice for every sale. A seller who exists only on a social media page, who asks for the full amount by transfer before you see the watch, or who cannot tell you the model number, leaves you nothing to fall back on.

Online, the safest way to pay is on delivery after inspecting the watch, because it lets you check everything in this guide before any money changes hands. Card payment through a proper checkout is also reasonable. Paying the full amount by transfer to an individual before you have seen the watch is the one method that leaves you with no protection at all.

## Frequently asked questions

### What is the most reliable way to tell if a watch is original?

Check several things together rather than one: a model number that exists and matches the watch, a clean finish on the case back, dial and clasp, functions that work as described, an invoice and a stated warranty, and a price in the usual range. One weak detail can be innocent; several together almost never are.

### Where is the model number on a watch?

On most fashion watches it is engraved on the stainless steel case back, together with the brand name, the material and the water resistance. Each brand has its own format: Tommy Hilfiger and Hugo Boss use seven digits, Emporio Armani uses AR followed by digits, and Guess uses GW followed by digits and letters.

### Does a watch without a box mean it is fake?

No. Boxes are easy to copy, and original watches are sometimes sold without their outer box, for example after being on display. The box proves little either way. Focus on the model number, the finish, the functions, the paperwork and the seller instead.

### Should the seconds hand tick or sweep?

On a quartz watch, which is almost every fashion watch, the seconds hand ticks once per second; that is normal. On an automatic, which is mechanical, it moves in many small steps and appears to sweep. A sweeping hand on a watch sold as quartz means the movement is not what the model uses.
