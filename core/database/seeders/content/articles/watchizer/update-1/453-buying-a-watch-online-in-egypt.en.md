## Key points

- Read the whole product page: case size, movement, water resistance, strap material, warranty and real photos.
- Measure your wrist before you order; the case size matters more than the photos suggest.
- Check who gives the warranty and for how long; it should be written on the page.

## Step 1: read the product page properly

A good product page answers your questions before you ask them. Before you look at the price, look for the basic specification: the case size in millimetres, the movement (quartz or automatic), the water resistance in metres or ATM, the case and strap materials, and the warranty. If any of these is missing, ask the shop before you order rather than after the watch arrives.

Then look at the photos. Real photos of the watch itself, from several angles, tell you far more than a single polished image borrowed from the brand. Look at the dial from the front, the side of the case to judge its thickness, the clasp and, where the shop shows it, the case back. A watch whose photos look different from one another, or whose photos appear on many other sites at very different prices, deserves a second look.

Read the description as well as the specification table. A good description tells you what makes the watch different from its neighbours: a sunray dial, a mesh bracelet, a crystal bezel, a sapphire glass or a skeleton dial. If two models look almost the same in the photos, the description is usually where you find the reason for a difference in price, and it helps you decide whether that difference matters to you.

## Step 2: get the size right before you order

Size is the detail most often regretted when a watch is bought unseen. A men's watch of 44 mm looks very different from one of 40 mm, and a women's watch of 39 mm from one of 33 mm, yet in a photo on a screen they can look almost the same. The case size on the product page is the number that matters.

![Tommy Hilfiger TH85 chronograph with a white dial on a silver stainless steel bracelet](/Uploads_Images/Product_image/1785173919_g0_6a67979fd36ea.webp)
*A Tommy Hilfiger TH85 chronograph: a busy dial makes a watch look larger than its case size suggests.*

Measure your wrist with a strip of paper just below the wrist bone, then measure the strip with a ruler. As a rough guide, a men's wrist of 15 to 17 cm suits a case of 40 to 43 mm, and a larger wrist suits 44 mm and above; a women's wrist below about 15 cm suits a case of 33 to 36 mm, and an average wrist 36 to 39 mm. Remember that a chronograph, with its sub-dials, looks larger than a three-hand watch of the same diameter.

The easiest test of all is to compare with a watch you already own. Look at its case back or its original listing for the size, and decide whether you want the new one larger, smaller or the same.

Thickness and strap width matter too, although product pages mention them less often. A thick chronograph sits higher on the wrist and is harder to slide under a shirt cuff; a slim three-hand watch disappears under any sleeve. If you wear fitted shirts every day, or long sleeves, favour a slimmer case and a bracelet or strap that sits flat.

## Step 3: understand the movement and the water rating

The movement decides how the watch is powered and cared for. A quartz watch runs on a battery, keeps very accurate time and needs a battery change every two to three years. An automatic watch is mechanical, winds itself as you move your wrist and stops after a day or two off the wrist. Most fashion watches are quartz; the product page should say which one you are buying.

The water rating decides where you can wear the watch. A rating of 30 m (3 ATM) means splashes and rain only; 50 m (5 ATM) allows a short pool swim; 100 m (10 ATM) or more is fine for the sea. The number is a laboratory test, not a diving depth, so treat it as a guide to daily life rather than a promise. If you plan to swim with the watch, choose 50 m at least, and never press a chronograph's pushers under water.

The strap affects where you can wear the watch as much as the rating does. A steel bracelet or a rubber strap copes with water and sweat; a leather strap should stay dry, whatever the case is rated for. If you want one watch for everything, including the beach in summer, choose a steel bracelet or rubber with at least 50 m, and keep leather for the office and evenings.

## Step 5: choose a safe way to pay

The way you pay is your protection if something goes wrong. Cash on delivery, with the right to open the parcel and inspect the watch in front of the courier before you pay, is the safest way of all: if the watch is not what you ordered, you simply refuse it. Card payment through a proper checkout page is also reasonable, because the shop has a merchant account and a returns process. Either way, a refund for a returned watch should come back by the method you paid with; ours is made within 24 hours of the watch reaching us.

The one way to avoid is transferring the full amount by bank or mobile wallet to an individual before you have seen anything. It leaves you with no protection at all, and it is the method most often used by sellers who cannot be found again. At Watchizer you can pay by card online or in cash when the watch arrives, and with cash on delivery you pay no delivery fee if you refuse the watch.

If you pay by card, keep the order confirmation and the card receipt with the invoice. If you pay cash, keep the delivery receipt. Either way, these papers are what prove when and where you bought the watch, and they are what make a return, an exchange or a warranty claim simple later on, whether that is in 14 days or in two years.

## Step 7: inspect the watch at the door

When the parcel arrives, open it in front of the courier and check five things before you pay. First, the model: the brand, the dial colour and the strap should match the product page. Second, the case back: where the brand engraves a model number, it should match the listing. Third, the condition: no scratches on the glass, the case or the bracelet.

![Tommy Hilfiger Decker with a navy dial on a navy silicone strap](/Uploads_Images/Product_image/1785254167_g0_6a68d117a9ea5.webp)
*A Tommy Hilfiger Decker on silicone: check the strap, the buckle and the crown before you pay.*

Fourth, the functions: pull the crown out and set the time, then push it back; on a chronograph, press the pushers to start, stop and reset the stopwatch. Fifth, the fit of the clasp or buckle: it should close firmly. If everything is right, pay and keep the delivery receipt. If anything is wrong, refuse the watch; with cash on delivery you pay no delivery fee.

Take your time at the door: five minutes is enough for every step above. Do not remove the protective film or the tags until you have decided to keep the watch, because an unused watch with its film and tags is what makes a return possible later.

## Step 8: after delivery, returns and service

The first week is your test drive. A quartz watch should keep time within a few seconds; an automatic within several seconds a day. If the watch stops, gains or loses far more, or a function fails, contact the shop at once. Keep the invoice, the delivery receipt and the warranty card together, ideally photographed on your phone.

## Frequently asked questions

### Is it safe to buy a watch online in Egypt?

Yes, when the shop gives you a full product page, a written warranty and return period, and a way to inspect the watch before you pay. Cash on delivery with the right to open the parcel in front of the courier is the safest method. Avoid transferring the full amount to an individual before you have seen the watch.

### How do I choose the right watch size online?

Measure your wrist with a strip of paper just below the wrist bone and compare it with the case size on the product page. A men's wrist of 15 to 17 cm suits 40 to 43 mm; a larger wrist 44 mm and above. A women's wrist below about 15 cm suits 33 to 36 mm.

### What should I check when the watch arrives?

Before you pay, check that the model, dial colour and strap match the product page, that any model number on the case back matches the listing, that the glass and case are unscratched, that the crown sets the time and the chronograph works, and that the clasp closes firmly. Refuse the watch if anything is wrong.
