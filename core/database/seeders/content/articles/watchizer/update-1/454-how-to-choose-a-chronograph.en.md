## What a chronograph is

A chronograph is a watch with a stopwatch built in. Alongside the normal hours, minutes and seconds, it can measure an interval, such as a lap, a cooking time or a parking meter, and show it on its own hands. The word comes from the Greek for "time writer", and the function has been part of watchmaking for two centuries.

Most chronographs you will see in a fashion watch shop are quartz. A quartz chronograph uses a battery-powered movement with small motors for the stopwatch hands, which makes it accurate, reliable and affordable. Mechanical chronographs exist too, mostly in higher price ranges, and they work on the same principle with springs and levers instead of motors.

The function is useful more often than people expect: timing a run or a workout, a parking meter, the minutes left on a phone call, the boiling of an egg, a presentation at work. Even if you never use it, the stopwatch gives a chronograph its busy, technical look, so you may choose one simply for its style. Both reasons are good ones.

Chronographs also differ in how they count. Most quartz fashion chronographs time up to 30 or 60 minutes, and some up to 12 hours, with the central hand counting seconds and sometimes fractions of a second. If you need to time long events, such as a football match or a long drive, check the description for the longest interval the model can measure.

## The pushers and the crown

A chronograph has two pushers, one on each side of the crown. On almost every model, the upper pusher at 2 o'clock starts and stops the stopwatch, and the lower pusher at 4 o'clock resets it to zero. Press the top pusher once to start, again to stop, and the bottom one to reset; on most quartz chronographs you can also stop and restart to add up several intervals before resetting.

The crown in the middle works as on any watch: pull it out to set the time, and on models with a date, pull it to the first position to change the date. When you choose a chronograph, try the pushers if you can. They should press with a firm, even click and spring back cleanly; a mushy or uneven pusher is a sign of a cheaper mechanism.

Some chronographs have screw-down pushers or a crown that screws in to improve water resistance; if yours does, unscrew them before use and screw them back afterwards. Most fashion chronographs have simple push-pieces that need no special care, other than not pressing them under water.

## Reading the sub-dials

The small dials inside the main dial are called sub-dials or registers, and they are where chronographs differ most. A classic layout has three: one counts the stopwatch's minutes, usually up to 30 or 60; one counts its hours, on longer timers; and one shows the running seconds of the normal time, so you can see that the watch is working. The large central seconds hand belongs to the stopwatch and stays at zero until you start it.

Not every sub-dial is part of the stopwatch. On many fashion chronographs, one sub-dial shows the day of the week, another a 24-hour scale, so you can tell 9 a.m. from 9 p.m. The product photos and descriptions tell you which layout a model uses. If you plan to use the stopwatch, choose a model whose minute counter is easy to read; if you want the look more than the function, any layout will do.

The position of the sub-dials changes the look too. Three sub-dials arranged in a triangle, at 12, 6 and 9 o'clock or at 6, 9 and 3, give a classic, balanced dial. Two sub-dials side by side, sometimes called a panda layout when they contrast with the dial, look sporty and retro. A layout with a date window squeezed between sub-dials can look busy, so check the photos if you like a clean dial.

## The tachymeter

Many chronographs carry a scale around the edge of the dial or on the bezel, marked with numbers from about 60 to 500. That is a tachymeter, and it turns the time it takes to cover a known distance into a speed. Start the stopwatch as you pass one marker, stop it as you pass the next one a kilometre later, and the central hand points to your average speed in kilometres per hour on the scale.

In daily life the tachymeter is more of a design feature than a tool, but it gives a chronograph its racing character. If you like the look of a motorsport watch, choose a model with a tachymeter bezel; if you prefer a cleaner dial, choose one with plain hour markers around the edge. Either way, the stopwatch works exactly the same.

Some chronographs carry other scales instead, such as a pulsometer for measuring a heart rate or a telemeter for estimating distance from the time between seeing lightning and hearing thunder. These are rare on fashion watches and, like the tachymeter, mainly part of the design. If a scale on a model you like is unfamiliar, the product description or the brand's site explains it.

## Size and how a chronograph wears

Chronographs look larger than three-hand watches of the same diameter, because the sub-dials fill the dial and the pushers widen the case. A 44 mm chronograph can therefore look like a 46 mm three-hand watch on the wrist, and it is usually a little thicker. Most men's chronographs in our shop are 42 to 45 mm.

As a rough guide, a wrist of 15 to 17 cm suits a chronograph of 40 to 43 mm, and a larger wrist suits 44 mm and above. If you wear fitted shirts every day, a slimmer, smaller chronograph slides under the cuff more easily. Measure your wrist with a strip of paper below the wrist bone, compare it with the case size on the product page, and remember that you can try every size in our two Cairo stores.

## Choosing by occasion

Think about where you will wear the chronograph most. For the office, a steel or two-tone model of 42 to 44 mm on a bracelet, with a blue, black or white dial, looks smart with a shirt and a suit. For weekends and sport, a model on rubber or silicone, with a bold dial and a contrasting stopwatch hand, is comfortable and practical. For evenings and weddings, a gold-tone chronograph on a steel bracelet, or a black dial on a leather strap, looks dressy.

If you want one chronograph for everything, choose steel on a steel bracelet with a blue or black dial, in a size that suits your wrist. It works at the office, at dinner and at the weekend, and it copes with heat and sweat better than leather. A second chronograph on rubber can then cover the beach and the gym.

## Dial colours and readability

Readability is the most important quality of a chronograph, and it depends on contrast. White or silver hands on a black or blue dial, or black hands on a white dial, are the easiest to read; gold hands on a gold dial look rich but are harder to read at a glance. Look at the stopwatch's central hand in particular: a contrasting colour, often red or orange, makes it easy to follow.

Blue dials are the most versatile, pairing with navy, grey and denim; black is the most discreet; white and silver look fresh and sporty. Green dials appear on many current models, and they look rich with beige, olive and brown clothes. Choose the colour you will enjoy looking at every day, and check the photos for how the sub-dials contrast with the main dial.

Lume, the luminous paint on the hands and markers, is worth checking if you need to read the time in the dark, at the cinema or when you wake at night. Not every fashion chronograph has it, and where it does, it may be only on the hands. The product description usually mentions it; if it matters to you, ask us before you order.

## Steel, leather or rubber

The strap or bracelet changes a chronograph's character more than any other detail. A steel bracelet makes it sporty and smart at once, suitable for the office and weekends. A leather strap dresses it up and suits suits and evenings, but should stay dry. A rubber or silicone strap makes it a sports watch, comfortable in the heat and easy to rinse after the gym or the beach.

Chronographs in gold tone or two-tone on a steel bracelet, such as the Michael Kors Lexington, are the dressiest choice, while black steel and gunmetal finishes look modern and understated. Within the warranty we size steel bracelets free of charge at our stores, usually while you wait.

A rubber strap is worth a closer look if you live in a hot city. It does not absorb sweat, it rinses clean under the tap, and it is light on the wrist, which makes it comfortable from April to October. A steel bracelet is just as practical but warmer in the sun; a leather strap is the least practical in summer and the most elegant in winter. Many chronographs use standard strap widths, so a watchmaker can often fit a second strap for a change of mood.

## Water, battery and care

A chronograph is no more fragile than a three-hand watch, but two habits protect it. Never press the pushers under water, even on a model rated to 50 m or more, because pressing them can let water past the seals. And check the water rating on the product page: 30 m means splashes only, 50 m a short pool swim and 100 m or more the sea.

A quartz chronograph uses a little more battery than a three-hand watch, especially if you leave the stopwatch running for long periods. When the seconds hand starts jumping in four-second steps, the battery is low; within the warranty we replace it free of charge at our two Cairo stores. After a battery change, the stopwatch hands sometimes need resetting to zero, which takes a minute.

Look after the glass as well as the movement. A chronograph's larger case can knock against door frames and desks more easily than a slim watch, so take care in tight spaces and keep the watch in a soft pouch when you travel. Wipe the case and bracelet with a soft, dry cloth after a hot day, and clean between the links now and then with a soft brush to keep the bracelet moving freely.

## Frequently asked questions

### What is a chronograph watch?

A chronograph is a watch with a built-in stopwatch. Alongside the normal time, it can measure an interval on its own hands, started and stopped with the upper pusher beside the crown and reset to zero with the lower one. Its small sub-dials count the stopwatch's minutes and hours, or show the running seconds, the day or a 24-hour scale.

### How do I use the pushers on a chronograph?

On almost every model, press the upper pusher at 2 o'clock to start the stopwatch, press it again to stop, and press the lower pusher at 4 o'clock to reset it to zero. On most quartz chronographs you can stop and restart several times to add up intervals before resetting. Never press the pushers under water.

### What is the tachymeter on a watch?

A tachymeter is a scale around the edge of the dial or on the bezel that turns the time taken to cover a known distance into an average speed. Start the stopwatch at one marker and stop it one kilometre later; the central hand then points to your speed in kilometres per hour.

### What size chronograph should I choose?

Chronographs look larger than three-hand watches of the same size. On a wrist of 15 to 17 cm, choose 40 to 43 mm; on a larger wrist, 44 mm and above. If you wear fitted shirts every day, a slimmer, smaller model slides under the cuff more easily. Try sizes in our stores if you can.

### Does a chronograph need more battery?

A little more than a three-hand watch, especially if you leave the stopwatch running. A quartz chronograph battery usually lasts two to three years in normal use. Within the warranty we replace it free of charge at our two Cairo stores, and the stopwatch hands may need resetting to zero afterwards.
