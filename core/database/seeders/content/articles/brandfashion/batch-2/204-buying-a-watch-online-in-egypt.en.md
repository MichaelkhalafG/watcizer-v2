Buying a watch online in Egypt is safe and convenient when you check a few things in the right order: the product page, the seller, the price, the payment method, the delivery terms, the parcel at your door and the exchange policy afterwards. Most problems people run into, a watch that looks different from the photo, a size that does not fit, a seller who stops answering, can be avoided with a quick look at each step before you order. This guide turns those steps into a practical checklist, explains what each detail on a product page means, and shows what you should expect from a serious online shop. At Brand Fashion we carry {{count family=watch}} watches, and every point below is something we do ourselves or encourage you to ask of any seller.

## Key points

- Read the specifications, not just the photos: case size, movement, water rating, strap and warranty.
- Check that the seller has a real address, a phone number that answers and a written returns policy.
- Prefer cash on delivery with inspection before payment, especially with a new seller.
- Open the parcel in front of the courier and check the watch against the product page.
- Keep the invoice and the box: they are your warranty and your exchange within 14 days.

## Start with the product page

A good product page tells you almost everything you need. Look for the case diameter in millimetres, the movement type, quartz or automatic, the water rating in metres or ATM, the strap material, the dial colour and the warranty. If any of these 6 details is missing, ask before you order; a serious seller answers quickly. A case of 40 mm and a case of 44 mm look alike in a photo, but they wear very differently, so the diameter alone is worth the question.

Read the title carefully too. It usually includes the brand, the collection, the model code and the main colours. The model code matters most: it lets you compare the same watch between sellers and check it against the brand's own catalogue. A page that hides or blurs the model code makes comparison impossible. On our pages the code appears in the title and in the specifications, and it is printed on the case back or the box of the watch you receive.

Finally, read the description and any notes about stock. Some pages list a watch that is not currently in stock; the button may say it is unavailable or offer to notify you when it returns. Ordering an unavailable model only delays you, so look for a clear "in stock" indication before you plan around a date. On Brand Fashion, unavailable models stay listed so you can follow them, but they cannot be added to the basket until they return.

## Photos: what they show and what they hide

Photos sell watches, but they can mislead. Close-up studio shots make every watch look large; dark dials look lighter under strong lights; gold-tone can look yellow or pink depending on the camera. Use photos to judge the design, and the specifications to judge the size and the colour. Screens differ as well: the same photo can look warmer on a phone and cooler on a laptop, which matters most with gold-tone, rose gold and green dials.

Look for photos from several angles: the dial straight on, the side to see thickness, the case back, the clasp and the strap. Real photos taken by the seller, sometimes on a wrist, are more useful than catalogue images, because they show the actual watch in natural light. If a page shows only one catalogue image, ask for more.

A wrist shot is the best size guide of all, especially if the wrist is close to yours in size. Even if the wrist is not yours, it shows how the watch sits and how large it looks. If you are unsure, send the seller your wrist size and ask how the watch would look on it; at Brand Fashion we often send an extra photo on WhatsApp for exactly this reason.

## Check the seller before the watch

The seller matters as much as the watch, sometimes more. Before ordering, look for a physical address, a phone number or WhatsApp that answers during working hours, and written pages for shipping, returns and warranty. A seller with stores you can visit, like our two in Cairo, is easier to reach if something goes wrong.

Read recent reviews on the shop itself and on independent places such as maps listings, preferably from the last few months. Look for patterns rather than single comments: delivery times, how problems were solved, whether exchanges were honoured. A handful of complaints is normal for any busy shop; complaints that nobody answered are a warning.

Be careful with pages that exist only on social media, with no website, no address and no returns policy. A quick test is to send a question and see how the answer comes: a clear reply with the model code and a photo is a good sign, a reply that only repeats the price is not. Many are honest small sellers, but you have no way to check, and no recourse if the watch is not what you expected. If you buy there, insist on cash on delivery with inspection.

## Price: compare like with like

Prices for the same watch vary between sellers, but within a reasonable range. When you compare, make sure you compare the same model code, the same strap and the same condition, new in its box. A watch that looks identical but has a different code may be a different size, movement or finish.

Look at what the price includes: delivery, the original box and papers, the warranty, sizing of the bracelet, the right to inspect before paying, and the exchange period. A slightly lower price without any of these is often worth less than a higher one with all of them. A 14-day exchange alone can save you the full price of a watch that turns out to be the wrong size.

Be wary of prices far below what established sellers charge. A large discount on a branded watch is possible during a sale, but a permanent price at a small fraction of the market is very rarely a genuine new watch. If something seems too good to be true, ask the seller for details, the model code and a photo of the case back before you order.

## Payment: cash on delivery and cards

In Egypt, cash on delivery is the most common way to pay for a watch online, and for good reason. You pay only after the parcel arrives, and with a good seller you can open it and check the watch before handing over the money. If the watch is not what you ordered, you refuse it and pay nothing.

Card payments online are convenient and secure on a proper checkout page with the padlock in the browser bar and the shop's own address in the link. They make sense with a seller you already trust, or when you want to avoid handling cash. Check that the page belongs to the shop, not a link sent in a message, and that you receive an order confirmation by e-mail or SMS. Never share the 1-time code your bank sends you with anyone who calls about an order; a real shop will never ask for it.

Avoid sending money in advance by transfer or wallet to a seller you do not know. It removes your ability to inspect and refuse, and it is the method most often used in scams. A serious seller will offer cash on delivery or a secure card payment; one who insists on a transfer first is asking you to carry all the risk.

## Delivery: times, fees and tracking

Before ordering, read the shipping page. It should tell you how long delivery takes to your governorate, what it costs and whether you can choose a time. At Brand Fashion delivery takes 24 hours to every governorate, and our [shipping page](/shipping) explains the details.

After ordering, a serious shop confirms the order with you by phone or WhatsApp, often to check the address and the model. This call is a good sign: it means someone is checking before the watch leaves. Keep your phone nearby on the delivery day so the courier can reach you. If you will be out, tell the shop in advance; most couriers can deliver to a work address or call before arriving.

If you need the watch for a specific date, a birthday, an engagement or Eid, order a few days ahead. Delivery is fast, but a day in reserve leaves time for a bracelet adjustment or an exchange if the size or colour is not quite right.

## Checking the parcel at your door

When the courier arrives, check the outside of the parcel first: it should be sealed and undamaged. Then, if the seller allows inspection, as we do, open it in front of the courier. Look for the brand box, the watch inside with its protective film, the warranty card or booklet and the tags. The check takes 2 or 3 minutes, and a good courier will wait while you do it.

Compare the watch with the product page: the brand and model code on the case back or the box, the dial colour, the strap material and the size. Check that the watch runs; a quartz watch's seconds hand should move steadily, and an automatic may need a gentle shake to start. Make sure the glass is not scratched and the bracelet is complete.

If anything is wrong, a different model, damage or a missing item, refuse the parcel there and then and contact the seller. With cash on delivery from Brand Fashion, a refused parcel costs you nothing. If everything is right, pay and keep the invoice with the box. Take a quick photo of the invoice on your phone too; if the paper gets lost, the photo still shows the date and the order number.

## After delivery: sizing, warranty and exchange

Most steel bracelets arrive at full length and need to be shortened. Do not try it with a pin and a hammer; a bracelet sized badly can be scratched or lose a pin. Our two Cairo stores size bracelets in a few minutes, free of charge within the warranty, and any good watch shop can do the same.

Keep the box, the warranty card and the invoice together. They prove the date and place of purchase, and you will need them for any warranty service or exchange. The [warranty page](/warranty) explains what each watch's cover includes.

## Common mistakes and how to avoid them

The most common mistake is choosing by photo without reading the size. A 44 mm watch can look elegant in a picture and overwhelming on a slim wrist. Measure your wrist with a strip of paper and compare the case diameter before ordering; our guide to case sizes explains the rules.

The second is ignoring the water rating. Many fashion watches are rated 30 m, which means splashes only. If you plan to swim, choose 100 m or more. The third is forgetting the strap: leather suffers in an Egyptian summer, while steel and silicone cope easily. A fourth, less obvious, is ordering two similar watches at once to decide at home; it is simpler to ask for extra photos first and order the one you prefer.

The last is giving up the right to inspect, which is the 1 protection you should never trade for a small discount. Paying in advance to an unknown seller to save a small amount, or accepting a parcel without opening it, removes your protection. A few minutes at the door with the courier is the best insurance you have.

## Frequently asked questions

### Is it safe to buy a watch online in Egypt?

Yes, with a seller that has a real address, a written returns policy and cash on delivery with inspection. Check the product page, compare the model code and price, open the parcel in front of the courier, and keep the invoice. These steps remove almost all the risk of buying online.

### Can I open the parcel before paying?

With Brand Fashion, yes. With cash on delivery you can open the parcel in front of the courier, check the watch against the product page and pay only if everything is right. If the watch is not what you ordered, you refuse it and pay nothing, including the delivery fee.

### What should I check when the watch arrives?

Check that the parcel is sealed, then look for the brand box, the watch with its protective film, the warranty card and the tags. Compare the model code, the dial colour, the strap and the size with the product page, make sure the watch runs, and check the glass and the bracelet for damage.

### How long do I have to exchange a watch?

At Brand Fashion, an unworn watch in its original packaging, with its tags and protective film, can be returned or exchanged within 14 days of delivery, and we pay the return shipping. Keep the invoice and the box, and send your order number on WhatsApp to arrange it.

### Who sizes the bracelet after I buy online?

Our two Cairo stores size steel bracelets in a few minutes, free of charge within the warranty, whether you bought online or in store. Any good watch shop can also do it. Avoid sizing it yourself without the right tool, because a slipped pin can scratch the bracelet or get lost.
