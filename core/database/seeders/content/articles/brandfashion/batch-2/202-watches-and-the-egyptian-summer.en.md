An Egyptian summer is hard on a watch. Temperatures above 35 degrees, sweat that is slightly salty and acidic, strong sun, sea water on the North Coast and in the Red Sea, chlorinated pools and fine sand all work on the strap, the seals and the finish at the same time. The good news is that a few choices and a simple routine protect almost any watch: pick a strap that copes with heat, respect the water rating, rinse after the sea or the pool, and keep the watch out of a hot car. This guide explains what each summer enemy does, which materials resist best, and how to care for your watch from June to September. We carry {{count family=watch}} watches at Brand Fashion, and the advice below applies to all of them.

## Key points

- Steel, silicone and mesh straps handle heat and sweat far better than leather.
- Check the water rating before the beach: 30 m means splashes only, 100 m means swimming.
- Rinse with fresh water the same day after the sea or a pool, then dry thoroughly.
- Never leave a watch on a car dashboard or in direct sun; heat ages seals, batteries and straps.
- Plated gold and rose gold cases need extra gentleness against sweat and sand.

## Why summer is hard on watches

Heat changes materials. Metal expands slightly, rubber seals soften and age faster, leather dries and cracks, and the glue that holds some parts together weakens at high temperatures. A watch left on a car dashboard in August can reach 60 degrees or more, which is enough to shorten the life of a battery and dry out the gaskets that keep water out.

Sweat is the second enemy. It contains salt and acids that attack leather, dull plating and leave a film between bracelet links. In summer we sweat more, and a watch worn all day can sit in moisture for hours. Over a season, this is what darkens leather straps, discolours the inside of silicone and wears plating at the edges of the case.

Then come the holiday enemies: sea water, chlorine, sun cream and sand. Salt and chlorine attack seals and leave deposits; sun cream stains straps and makes glass greasy; sand scratches polished surfaces and can jam a rotating bezel or a crown. None of these is dangerous on its own, but together, day after day, they age a watch faster than any other season of the year.

## Choosing a strap for the heat

Steel bracelets are the most practical summer choice. They do not absorb sweat, they can be rinsed if the watch allows, and they keep their look for years. On very hot days steel can feel warm in direct sun, but it cools quickly and never smells. Brushed finishes hide small scratches better than polished ones, and a bracelet with a fold-over clasp stays secure even when your wrist is wet with sweat.

Silicone and rubber straps are the most comfortable. They are light, soft and waterproof, they dry in minutes, and they come in summer colours such as white, navy, green and orange. They are the natural choice for the beach, the gym and travel. A quick rinse after sweat or the sea keeps them fresh; a wash with mild soap once a week removes sun cream and dust.

Mesh bracelets are a good middle ground: light, airy and easy to adjust with a sliding clasp when your wrist swells in the heat. Leather is the one material to avoid in high summer. It absorbs sweat, darkens and smells, and it does not like water at all. Many customers keep their leather watch for the cooler months, from November to March, and switch to steel or silicone for the summer.

## Water resistance and the beach

Every watch has a water rating, shown on the product page and usually on the case back. It describes resistance to water pressure in laboratory conditions, not a depth you can dive to. In everyday terms: 30 m, or 3 ATM, resists splashes and rain but should not be swum with; 50 m tolerates a short swim in calm water; 100 m is suitable for swimming and snorkelling; 200 m is built for diving.

Most fashion watches are rated 30 or 50 m, which means they are fine for washing your hands but not for the sea. Sport watches from brands such as G-Shock, Naviforce and many chronographs from Hugo Boss and Maserati reach 50 to 100 m. If you plan to swim, choose a watch rated 100 m or more, or leave your watch in a safe place on the beach.

Two habits matter as much as the rating. Never press the buttons of a chronograph or pull out the crown while the watch is wet or under water, because that opens a path for water. And remember that seals age: a watch that was water resistant when new may not be after a few years without a seal check. A good habit is to have the seals checked every time the battery is changed, about every 2 years, and before any summer in which you plan to swim with the watch.

## Salt, chlorine and the same-day rinse

After the sea or a chlorinated pool, rinse the watch with fresh water as soon as you can, if its rating allows. Salt crystals dry inside the gaps of the bracelet and around the bezel, and chlorine slowly attacks the rubber seals. A 30-second rinse under a gentle stream of lukewarm water, crown pushed in, removes most of it.

Dry the watch with a soft cloth, paying attention to the clasp, the gaps between links and the space under the bezel. Leave it on a towel for 30 minutes with the clasp open so the last traces of water evaporate. If the watch is rated 30 m, wipe it with a cloth dampened in fresh water instead of rinsing it.

Silicone straps deserve a little extra attention after the beach. Sand and sun cream collect on the inside, where the strap touches the skin; a soft toothbrush with a drop of mild soap cleans them in a minute. White and light-coloured straps stay bright much longer when they are washed after every beach day.

## Sand, dust and scratches

Sand is fine, hard and everywhere in summer, from the beach to the desert road. It scratches polished steel, gets into the gaps of the bracelet and can stick under a rotating bezel or around the crown. The rule is simple: do not wipe sand off with a dry cloth, which rubs it into the surface like sandpaper. Rinse it off first, then dry.

If the watch has a rotating bezel and is made to be rinsed, turn the bezel gently while holding it under fresh water; the sand comes out of the gap. Do the same with the clasp by opening and closing it under water. Never force a crown or a pusher that feels gritty; bring the watch to one of our two Cairo stores instead, where it can be cleaned properly without damaging the seals.

Dust from the road is less harsh but builds up, especially on the khamaseen days of spring and early summer, when fine dust reaches every corner of a bracelet. A soft brush once a week, especially around the lugs and the clasp, keeps the watch clean. Mineral glass resists most scratches from daily life but not sand rubbed across it, so a soft pouch in your beach bag is worth more than any polish.

## Sun, heat and the car dashboard

Direct sun fades colours. Silicone straps, coloured dials and printed details can lose their brightness after a few summers in strong sunlight, and leather fades and dries. Keep the watch in a drawer or a box when you are not wearing it, never on a sunny windowsill. On the beach, put it in the shade inside a bag rather than on a towel in the sun, where the case can become too hot to touch within 20 minutes.

The car is the worst place of all. In July, the inside of a parked car in Cairo can exceed 60 degrees, and a dashboard in the sun can be even hotter. At those temperatures seals soften, batteries drain faster, liquid crystal displays on digital watches can darken temporarily, and leather can warp. Never leave a watch in a parked car, not even in the glove box.

Sudden changes are also a risk. Going from 45 degrees outside to an air-conditioned room at 18 degrees can cause condensation inside an older watch whose seals are no longer perfect. If you see fog under the glass, the seals need checking; bring the watch in as soon as possible rather than letting moisture reach the movement.

## Sweat, plating and skin

Gold-tone and rose gold watches are usually plated: a thin layer of colour over steel. Plating is durable, but sweat, perfume and friction wear it away at the edges, starting with the clasp and the corners of the case. In summer, wipe a plated watch with a dry cloth every evening, and avoid scrubbing it or using any abrasive product.

Perfume and body lotion are part of summer for many people. Apply them before putting on the watch and let them dry first; the alcohol and oils in them dull plating and stain straps. The same applies to sun cream, which leaves a white film on silicone and a greasy layer on the glass.

Some people find that a steel or leather strap irritates the skin in summer, especially after long hours of sweating. A looser fit, a silicone strap or a few hours without the watch usually solves it. If irritation continues, a strap in a different material is the easiest fix, and our stores can fit one in minutes. Keeping the skin under the watch clean and dry matters too; a quick wipe of the wrist and the case back at the end of a hot day helps both.

## A simple summer routine

A few habits keep a watch in good shape through the whole season. Every evening, wipe it with a dry cloth to remove sweat and sun cream. Once a week, wash a steel or silicone strap with a soft toothbrush, lukewarm water and a drop of mild soap, if the rating allows, and dry it carefully.

After every beach or pool day, rinse with fresh water the same day. Keep the watch out of the car and out of the sun when you are not wearing it. On very hot days, loosen the bracelet by one notch or move the mesh clasp a little so the skin can breathe.

At the end of the summer, look at the watch closely. Is the strap cracked, is the crown smooth, is the seconds hand still moving in regular steps? A battery change before winter is a good moment to check the seals too, so the watch keeps its water resistance for the next summer. ## Frequently asked questions

### Can I swim with my watch in the sea?

Only if it is rated 100 m or more, and only with the crown pushed in and the pushers untouched. Watches rated 30 m resist splashes only, and 50 m tolerates a brief swim in calm water. Rinse the watch with fresh water after the sea, the same day, and dry it thoroughly.

### Is a leather strap a bad idea in summer?

Leather is not ideal from June to September. It absorbs sweat, darkens, smells and can crack, and it must never get wet. If you love leather, wear it in the evening when it is cooler, wipe it after use and let it air. Steel, mesh and silicone are much more comfortable in the heat.

### Why is there fog inside my watch after the beach?

Fog means moisture got inside, usually through ageing seals, a crown that was not pushed in, or a sudden change from heat to air conditioning. Do not dry it with a hair dryer or in the sun. Bring the watch to one of our stores as soon as possible so it can be dried and resealed.

### Does heat damage the battery?

High heat makes batteries drain faster and can shorten their life, especially if the watch is left in a parked car or in direct sun. A normal summer on the wrist is fine. If the seconds hand starts jumping in steps of several seconds, the battery is low and should be replaced soon.

### How do I clean sun cream off a silicone strap?

Wash the strap with lukewarm water, a drop of mild soap and a soft toothbrush, especially on the inside where it touches the skin, then rinse and dry it. Avoid alcohol, solvents and oils, which damage silicone or make it sticky. Washing after every beach day keeps light colours bright.
