An original bag is made to last for years, but how many years depends mostly on how it is treated. In Egypt, bags face conditions that most care guides written elsewhere do not mention: summer heat of 35 to 40 degrees, cars that become ovens in the sun, fine dust that settles everywhere, crowded transport and long days out of the house. The good news is that a few simple habits protect a bag from almost all of it. This guide covers the habits that matter every day, how to clean each common material, what to do about stains, and how to look after the handles, straps and hardware that wear first. We carry {{count family=bag}} bags at Brand Fashion; this advice applies to all of them.

## Key points

- Know the material first: smooth leather, pebbled leather, coated canvas and nylon each need different care.
- Do not overfill: a bag carrying 3 to 5 kg every day stretches its handles and loses its shape.
- Keep bags out of hot cars and direct sun; heat dries leather, fades colours and softens glued edges.
- Treat stains within minutes, test every product on a hidden area, and never rub hard.
- Wipe the bag once a week in dusty months, and condition smooth leather every 3 to 6 months.

## Know what your bag is made of

Every care decision starts with the material, so check it before you clean anything. The product page or the label inside the bag usually tells you. Most bags we sell fall into 4 groups: smooth leather, pebbled or textured leather, coated canvas, often printed with a monogram, and nylon or other fabrics. Many bags combine two, for example coated canvas with leather trims and handles.

Smooth leather is elegant and soft but shows scratches and water marks. Pebbled and textured leathers, such as saffiano-style finishes, hide small marks and resist scratches better. Coated canvas, used on many Coach, Michael Kors and Guess signature bags, is a fabric sealed with a protective coating, which makes it light, resistant to water and easy to wipe clean. Nylon is the lightest and the most forgiving, but it can stain with oil.

When a bag mixes materials, care for each part as what it is: wipe the canvas body, condition the leather handles, and keep products for one away from the other. Leather conditioner on coated canvas, for example, can leave a greasy film, and a damp cloth that is fine for canvas can leave a mark on untreated leather.

## Everyday habits that matter most

Most damage comes from ordinary habits rather than accidents. Overfilling is the first: a bag carrying a laptop, a water bottle and everything else can weigh 3 to 5 kg, and carried that way every day it stretches the handles, pulls the seams and bends the base out of shape. Carry only what you need, and move heavy items such as a laptop to a second bag on the days you carry them.

Where you put the bag down matters too. Floors, café chairs and car floors are covered in dust and grit, which scratch leather and mark light colours. Hang the bag on a hook or the back of a chair, or put it on a clean seat. Hand cream, sunscreen and perfume transfer to handles and leave dark marks over time, so let them dry before you pick up a leather bag.

Colour transfer is the third common problem. Dark, new jeans can rub blue dye onto the back of a light bag, especially a crossbody that sits against the hip. If you wear new denim, choose a darker bag that day, or carry the bag on the other side.

## Cleaning smooth and pebbled leather

For routine cleaning, once a month or whenever the bag looks dull, empty it, shake out the dust and wipe the outside with a soft, dry microfibre cloth. For light dirt, slightly dampen the cloth with clean water, wring it out until it is almost dry, wipe gently and let the bag dry naturally at room temperature. Never soak leather, and never put it on a heater, under a hair dryer or in the sun to dry.

Every 3 to 6 months, smooth leather benefits from a leather conditioner, which replaces the natural oils that heat and dry air remove. Apply a small amount with a soft cloth, in circles, let it absorb and buff off the excess. Test it first on a hidden area, such as the underside of a flap or the base, because some conditioners darken leather slightly. Pebbled leather needs less conditioning, once or twice a year.

Avoid household cleaners, baby wipes, alcohol and make-up removers on leather. They can strip the finish and leave pale patches that cannot be reversed. If you are not sure whether a product is safe, it is better to use nothing more than a dry cloth.

## Cleaning coated canvas and monogram bags

Coated canvas is the easiest material to keep clean. Wipe it with a soft cloth slightly dampened with water and, for more stubborn dirt, a drop of mild, unscented soap; then wipe again with a cloth dampened in clean water and dry it with a soft towel. Do not scrub with a brush or the rough side of a sponge, which can dull the coating and wear the printed pattern.

Pay attention to the leather trims, handles and piping on canvas bags, which are often untreated or lightly finished leather. Clean them as leather, with a dry or barely damp cloth, and keep water and soap off them. Corners and edges are where canvas bags wear first; if the edge paint starts to crack after years of use, a leather repair shop can usually repaint it.

## Nylon, fabric and suede

Nylon and fabric bags can usually be spot-cleaned with a soft cloth, lukewarm water at around 30 degrees, never hot, and a little mild soap. Dab rather than rub, rinse the area with a clean damp cloth and let the bag dry open, with its shape supported by tissue paper. Unless the care label says so, do not put a nylon bag with leather parts in a washing machine: the leather trims, the hardware and the stiffeners inside will not survive it.

Suede and nubuck are the most delicate leathers. Keep them away from water, brush them gently with a soft suede brush in one direction to lift the nap, and use a suede eraser on small marks. A water spot on suede often fades if you brush it once it is completely dry. In Cairo, suede bags are best kept for dry days and evenings, and for days you drive rather than walk.

## Dealing with stains

The first rule of stains is speed: a stain treated within minutes is much easier to remove than one left for a day. The second is gentleness: blot, do not rub, because rubbing pushes the stain deeper and spreads it. The third is caution: test every product on a hidden area first, and if a stain does not come out with gentle treatment, stop and ask a professional rather than trying stronger products.

For oil and grease on leather, blot immediately with a dry tissue, then sprinkle a little cornflour or talc on the spot and leave it for several hours or overnight to draw the oil out, before brushing it away. For water marks on smooth leather, dampen the whole panel evenly with a barely damp cloth so it dries in one even tone. For ink, do not use water or alcohol on leather; take the bag to a leather specialist, because ink sets quickly and home remedies often make it worse.

Make-up and food stains on coated canvas usually come off with a damp cloth and a drop of mild soap. On nylon, a little soap and lukewarm water dabbed with a cloth work for most marks. Whatever the material, let the bag dry naturally afterwards, away from the sun.

## Egypt's heat, sun and dust

Heat is the biggest risk for bags in Egypt. In summer, the inside of a parked car can reach 60 degrees or more within an hour, which dries and cracks leather, softens the glue that holds edges and linings, and can even warp plastic parts and coated finishes. Take your bag with you rather than leaving it in the car, or at least keep it out of direct sun on the floor behind the seats, covered.

Direct sun fades colours, especially bright and light leathers, and the side of a bag that always faces a window will fade faster than the other. Do not store bags on a sunny shelf or near a window. Dust is the third enemy: in the dusty months, and after a khamasin day, wipe your bag with a dry microfibre cloth once a week so dust does not settle into the grain or the stitching.

Humidity matters on the coast. In Alexandria and the North Coast, a bag left closed in a damp place can develop mould spots, especially on leather and fabric linings. Let bags air after a humid day, keep a sachet of silica gel inside those you use less often, and never store a bag while it is still damp.

Winter brings its own risk. On the few rainy days between December and February, a sudden shower can leave water spots on smooth leather and suede. Keep a small foldable umbrella or a cloth bag in the car for those days, and if a leather bag gets wet, blot it at once with a dry towel and let it dry slowly at room temperature, stuffed with tissue paper to hold its shape.

## Handles, straps, hardware and lining

Handles and straps carry all the weight and take all the hand contact, so they wear first. Switch shoulders and hands from time to time, use the long strap when the bag is heavy, and wipe the handles with a dry cloth every week to remove the oils from your hands. A strap that starts to crack or a handle whose stitching loosens can usually be repaired by a leather shop if you act early.

Metal hardware, such as zips, clasps, chains and feet, can tarnish or scratch. Wipe it with a soft dry cloth, keep perfume and hand cream off it, and avoid metal polish, which can damage the leather around it. If a zip becomes stiff, run a graphite pencil or a little candle wax along the teeth rather than forcing it. Inside, empty the bag every week; pens without caps, open make-up and loose coins are the most common causes of stained and torn linings.

Once every 2 or 3 months, look closely at the points that carry the load: the stitching where the handles join the body, the rings and clips that hold the strap, and the corners of the base. Pull gently on each handle and look for loose threads or a gap opening in the seam. These checks take a minute, and they find most problems while a repair is still small and cheap.

## When a bag needs a professional

Some problems are beyond home care: deep ink stains, large water stains on untreated leather, broken stitching, torn handles, cracked edges and broken zips. A good leather repair shop can treat most of them, and an early repair is cheaper and more invisible than a late one. Take the bag as soon as you notice the problem, and tell the shop what the material is and what you have already tried.

Prevention is still the best repair. With the habits in this guide, a weekly wipe, a monthly clean, conditioner every 3 to 6 months for smooth leather, and no hot cars, an original bag can look good for 5 years or more of regular use.

## Frequently asked questions

### How often should I clean my bag?

Wipe it with a dry microfibre cloth once a week, especially in dusty months, and give it a fuller clean once a month or whenever it looks dull. Smooth leather needs a conditioner every 3 to 6 months, pebbled leather once or twice a year. Coated canvas only needs wiping.

### Can I use baby wipes on a leather bag?

It is better not to. Baby wipes, household cleaners, alcohol and make-up removers can strip the finish of leather and leave pale patches that cannot be reversed. Use a soft cloth, dry or barely damp with clean water, and a leather conditioner tested first on a hidden area.

### How do I remove an oil stain from leather?

Blot it immediately with a dry tissue without rubbing, then cover the spot with a little cornflour or talc and leave it for several hours or overnight to draw the oil out. Brush the powder away gently. If a mark remains, take the bag to a leather specialist rather than using stronger products.

### Is it safe to leave my bag in the car?

Not in summer. The inside of a parked car in Egypt can reach 60 degrees or more within an hour, which dries leather, softens glued edges and fades colours. Take the bag with you, or at least keep it covered and out of direct sun on the floor behind the seats.

### How do I clean a coated canvas bag?

Wipe it with a soft cloth slightly dampened with water, and use a drop of mild, unscented soap for stubborn dirt. Wipe again with a clean damp cloth and dry it with a soft towel. Keep water and soap off the leather trims and handles, and never scrub the canvas with a brush.
