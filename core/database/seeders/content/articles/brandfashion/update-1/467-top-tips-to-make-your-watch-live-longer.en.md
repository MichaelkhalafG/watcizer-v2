A watch rarely fails all at once. It wears out slowly, through small habits repeated every day: perfume sprayed on the wrist, a hot shower with the watch still on, a dead battery left inside for months, a leather strap that never dries. The good news is that each of those habits has a simple alternative, and most of them take less than a minute. This guide goes through the eight things that shorten a watch's life, why each one does harm, and what to do instead, then ends with a simple care calendar you can follow. We carry {{count family=watch}} watches at Brand Fashion, and the advice below applies to almost all of them.

## Key points

- Sudden heat and cold are harder on a watch than water: take it off before a sauna, a hot shower or a jump into a cold pool.
- Perfume, lotion and chlorine wear gold-tone and rose gold plating; let them dry before you put the watch on.
- Replace a dead battery within a few weeks, because an old battery can leak and damage the movement.
- Check the crown, the strap and the clasp every month; they are the first parts to wear.

## Keep it away from sudden heat and cold

The seals that keep water and dust out of a watch are small rubber rings, called gaskets, at the case back, around the crystal and inside the crown. Rubber expands with heat and shrinks with cold, and a sudden change of temperature can open a tiny gap for long enough to let steam or water in. That is why a sauna, a steam room or a very hot shower is harder on a watch than an ordinary swim: the heat and the steam arrive together.

The same is true in the other direction. Jumping from a hot beach into a cold pool, or walking from 40 degrees outside into a cold air-conditioned room with a wet watch, makes the air inside the case contract and pull moisture in. If you see a light mist on the inside of the crystal after a change of temperature, take the watch off, keep it in a warm, dry place with the crown closed, and bring it to us if the mist does not clear within a day.

The rule is simple: take the watch off before the sauna, the steam room or a hot bath, and let it reach room temperature before it goes into cold water. A car dashboard in the Egyptian summer is the other place to avoid, because the temperature behind a windscreen can reach 60 or 70 degrees.

## Protect the plating from perfume and cosmetics

Most gold-tone, rose gold and black watches from fashion brands such as Michael Kors, Guess and Tommy Hilfiger are made of stainless steel with a thin coloured layer on top, applied by plating or by a process called PVD. That layer is durable, but it is thin, and it wears faster when it is exposed again and again to the alcohol in perfume, the oils in lotion, the chlorine in swimming pools and the salt in sweat and sea water.

![Michael Kors Portia gold-tone stainless steel watch with crystal accents](/Uploads_Images/Product_image/1794248434_2026-11-09_6af20ef27d724.webp)

The habit that protects it is simple: put on perfume, lotion and hand cream first, wait a minute for them to dry, and only then put on the watch. If you spray perfume, aim at the neck rather than the wrist that wears the watch. After a swim or a hot day, wipe the case and the bracelet with a soft, dry cloth. Avoid polishing cloths and jewellery cleaners on plated watches, because they are abrasive and can remove the colour they are meant to brighten.

Plating wears first where the watch rubs: the edges of the clasp, the back of the bracelet links and the corners of the case. A little wear there after 2 or 3 years of daily use is normal; wear after a few months usually points to perfume, chlorine or a desk that the bracelet rubs against while you type.

## Avoid knocks and drops

A quartz movement is robust, but its parts are small and precise, and a hard knock can bend a hand, loosen a sub-dial or crack the crystal. Mechanical and automatic watches are more sensitive still, because the balance wheel that keeps time swings several times a second on a hair-thin spring. The most common accidents are ordinary ones: a watch dropped on a tiled floor while being taken off, a wrist hitting a door frame, a watch worn during a game of football or a session at the gym.

Take the watch off over a bed, a sofa or a towel rather than over a tiled floor, and keep it off for contact sports and heavy weights. If you want a watch for sport, choose one built for it: a G-Shock, for example, is designed to absorb knocks that would damage an ordinary dress watch.

## Look after the crystal

The crystal is the transparent cover over the dial, and on most fashion watches it is mineral glass: hard, clear and affordable, but less scratch-resistant than the sapphire crystal used on more expensive watches. Mineral glass resists everyday rubbing well, but it can be scratched by harder materials such as sand, stone, concrete and other metals. On the Mohs scale of hardness, mineral glass sits at about 5 to 6, the quartz in sand at 7 and sapphire at 9, which is why sand scratches mineral glass and not sapphire.

The habits that protect it are easy. Do not put the watch face down on a table or a bathroom counter, where grit can scratch it. Do not keep it in a pocket or a bag next to keys and coins. Be careful with metal bracelets and rings worn on the same hand, which can rub against the crystal. And on a beach trip, keep the watch away from sand, which is mostly quartz and harder than mineral glass. Light hairline scratches are part of a watch's life; a deep scratch or a chip should be checked, because it can weaken the crystal and let water in.

## Keep water out: crown, pushers and seals

Water resistance is printed on the case back or the dial as metres or as ATM. A watch marked 30 m or 3 ATM resists splashes and rain; 50 m or 5 ATM resists a swim in shallow water; 100 m or 10 ATM resists swimming and snorkelling. These figures come from laboratory tests at rest, not from real depths, and they apply only when the crown is pushed in or screwed down.

That makes the crown the most important part of water care. Check that it is fully pushed in before you wash your hands, and if it screws down, that it is screwed down. Never pull out the crown or press the chronograph pushers under water, because that opens a path past the seals. Rinse the watch in fresh water after the sea or a pool, so that salt and chlorine do not dry on the seals, and dry it with a soft cloth.

Seals also age. Rubber gaskets harden and shrink over the years, especially with heat, sweat and sun cream, so a watch that was water resistant when new may not stay that way forever. A watch shop can test water resistance with a pressure tester, which is worth doing before you swim with a watch that is several years old; until then, treat an older watch more carefully around water.

## Do not leave a dead battery inside

A quartz battery usually lasts between 1 and 3 years, depending on the movement and its functions: a chronograph that is run often, a backlight or an alarm all use more power. When the battery runs low, many quartz watches give a warning by making the second hand jump in steps of 4 or 5 seconds instead of one. That is the moment to replace it.

What shortens a watch's life is leaving a dead battery inside for months. An old battery can leak, and the leaking chemicals corrode the contacts and the circuit of the movement, turning a simple battery change into a repair. If you put a quartz watch away for a long time, have the battery removed or replaced first, and on many quartz watches pulling the crown out to its last position stops the hands and reduces the power used.

Have the battery changed by a watch shop rather than at home. Opening the case back without the right tool can scratch the case, damage the gasket or let dust in, and the seal should be checked when the case is closed again. Within the warranty, we change batteries free of charge at our stores.

## Strap and clasp checks

Straps and clasps wear faster than any other part of a watch, and they are the parts that decide whether the watch stays on your wrist. A leather strap absorbs sweat and water; let it dry naturally after a hot day, never on a heater or in the sun, and avoid wearing it in the shower or the sea. Rotating between two straps, or between a leather and a steel watch, lets each one rest and lasts longer than wearing one every day.

![Fossil Neutra chronograph with a grey dial and a brown leather strap](/Uploads_Images/Product_image/1794247002_2026-11-09_6af2095a118af.webp)
*A Fossil Neutra on brown leather: a leather strap lasts longer when it dries naturally and rests between wears.*

Steel bracelets collect dirt between the links. Once a month, if the watch is water resistant to at least 50 m, clean the bracelet with a soft toothbrush and a little mild soapy water, rinse it and dry it well; if it is rated below 50 m, use a dry toothbrush and a soft cloth instead. Silicone and rubber straps only need a rinse in fresh water after sweat, the sea or a pool.

Check the clasp and the spring bars, the small pins that hold the strap to the case, every month. A clasp that opens too easily or a strap that moves at the lug is a warning: a worn spring bar costs very little to replace, while a watch dropped on the floor can cost much more.

## Storage, travel and magnets

When the watch is off, keep it in its box, a soft pouch or a watch tray, away from jewellery and keys that can scratch it. Keep it away from humidity, such as a bathroom shelf, and away from direct sun, which fades leather and some dials over time. For travel, a pouch in a hand bag is safer than a suitcase, where it can be crushed.

Magnets matter more than most people think. A mechanical or automatic watch exposed to a strong magnet can start to run very fast, because the hairspring sticks to itself. Quartz watches are more resistant, but strong magnets can still stop them for a moment. Watches sold as anti-magnetic meet the ISO 764 standard, which asks them to keep time after exposure to a field of 4,800 A/m; most fashion watches do not claim it. In daily life, the magnets to watch are speakers, tablet covers, laptop lids and the magnetic clasps of many bags and wallets. Do not store the watch on a speaker or a tablet, and do not leave it for hours inside a bag pocket next to a magnetic closure.

## A simple care calendar

Daily: put perfume and lotion on first, check the crown is pushed in, and take the watch off before the sauna, a hot shower or contact sport. Weekly: wipe the case and the bracelet with a soft dry cloth, and let a leather strap rest for a day. Monthly: clean a steel bracelet, check the clasp and the spring bars, and look at the crystal for chips.

Every 1 to 3 years: replace the quartz battery as soon as the second hand starts to jump, and have the water resistance tested before you swim with it again. Every 4 to 6 years: an automatic or mechanical watch benefits from a full service, in which the movement is cleaned and lubricated. Those few habits, spread over the year, are what make a watch last a decade or more.

## Frequently asked questions

### How long does a watch battery last?

A quartz battery usually lasts between 1 and 3 years, depending on the movement and its functions. A chronograph that is run often, a backlight or an alarm use more power. When the second hand starts to jump in steps of 4 or 5 seconds, replace the battery within a few weeks.

### Can I wear my watch in the shower?

It is better not to. Even a water-resistant watch is exposed to hot water, steam and soap in the shower, and the heat can open the seals for a moment. Take the watch off before a hot shower, a bath or a sauna, and keep it on only for splashes and hand washing.

### Why is the plating on my watch fading?

Gold-tone, rose gold and black plating is a thin layer, and it wears faster with perfume, lotion, chlorine, salt and sweat. Put perfume and lotion on first and let them dry, wipe the watch after a swim or a hot day, and avoid abrasive cloths and jewellery cleaners.

### What should I do if there is mist inside the crystal?

Take the watch off, keep it in a warm, dry place with the crown pushed in, and do not open it yourself. If the mist does not clear within a day, bring the watch to us: moisture inside the case can damage the dial and the movement if it stays.
