A watch is the one accessory most men wear every day, and it is often the only piece of metal they wear at all apart from a belt buckle and a ring. That makes it a strong signal: the right watch makes an ordinary outfit look considered, and the wrong one makes a good suit look unfinished. The good news is that matching a watch with your clothes follows a few simple rules, and once you know them you can dress any outfit, from a wedding suit to a gym kit, in a minute. We carry {{count family=watch gender=men}} men's watches at Brand Fashion; this guide shows how to wear them well.

## Key points

- Match the metal of the watch to your belt buckle, cufflinks and ring: steel with silver, gold with gold.
- Match a leather strap to your shoes and belt: black with black, brown with brown.
- The more formal the outfit, the simpler and thinner the watch should be; the more casual, the more freedom you have.
- A case of 40 to 44 mm suits most men's wrists; a dress watch should slide under the shirt cuff.
- Two or three watches, a steel everyday watch, a dress watch on leather and a sports watch, cover almost every occasion.

## Metal and leather: the two matching rules

The first rule is about metal. Look at the other metal you wear: the buckle of your belt, your cufflinks, your wedding band, the frame of your glasses. A steel or silver-tone watch belongs with silver buckles and white gold or platinum rings; a gold-tone watch belongs with a gold buckle and a yellow gold ring; a rose gold watch is warmer and works best with brown leather and earth colours. Two-tone watches, steel with gold, are the most flexible, because they bridge both metals.

The second rule is about leather. If your watch is on a leather strap, its colour should follow your shoes and your belt. Black shoes and a black belt call for a black strap; brown, tan or cognac shoes call for a brown strap of a similar shade. You do not need an exact match, but the family should be the same: a black strap with brown shoes looks like a mistake, while a dark brown strap with mid-brown shoes looks deliberate.

Steel bracelets escape the second rule, which is one reason they are so versatile: a steel watch on a steel bracelet goes with black and brown leather equally well. If you own only one watch, a steel watch on a steel bracelet is the safest choice.

A practical test takes 30 seconds. Before you leave, look at your wrist, your belt buckle and your hands together in a mirror. If you see 2 different metals side by side, a gold buckle next to a steel watch for example, change one of them. Glasses frames and phone cases matter much less, because they sit far from the wrist; what people notice is the line from the cuff to the belt. A reversible belt with a silver buckle, black on one side and brown on the other, makes the test easy to pass with a steel watch.

## Suits and formal occasions

For a wedding, an evening event or a formal dinner, the classic rule is simplicity. Choose a thin watch with a clean dial in white, silver, black or deep blue, with two or three hands and few or no sub-dials, on a black leather strap or a fine steel bracelet. A case of 38 to 42 mm looks elegant, and a case thinner than about 10 mm slides under the shirt cuff, which is where a dress watch belongs.

Avoid large chronographs, rubber straps and bright colours with a dark suit: they draw attention away from the outfit. If the event is black tie, a black dial on a black leather strap or a silver dial on a polished bracelet is the traditional choice. And match the metal to your cufflinks: silver cufflinks with a steel watch, gold cufflinks with a gold-tone one.

Seasons matter too. At an outdoor wedding in the Cairo summer, when the jacket comes off after an hour, the watch becomes the most visible accessory you wear. A steel bracelet is cooler than leather at 35 degrees and does not mark with sweat, so on hot evenings a slim steel watch can be a better choice than a leather strap, as long as its dial stays simple.

## The office

Office dress in Cairo ranges from a full suit in a bank to a shirt and chinos in a design studio, and the watch can follow. For a suit and tie, the dress-watch rules apply, though a simple chronograph with a clean dial is acceptable if it fits under the cuff. For business casual, a shirt without a tie and smart trousers, a steel watch on a steel bracelet is the most versatile choice: it looks professional and survives the commute.

Blue and black dials are the safest for the office, and a date window is genuinely useful at work. Brands such as Hugo Boss, Tommy Hilfiger and Emporio Armani make many models designed exactly for this: steel, 41 to 44 mm, with a sober dial that looks good with a white or light blue shirt.

## Smart casual

Smart casual, a polo or an open-collar shirt with chinos or dark jeans, is the outfit most men wear most often, and it gives you the most freedom. A chronograph on a steel bracelet, a watch on a brown leather strap, a mesh bracelet or a two-tone watch all work. This is the place for a coloured dial: navy, green or grey dials look good with the neutral colours of smart casual clothing.

Brown leather straps are especially at home here, because they match the brown shoes, loafers and desert boots that go with chinos. A Fossil chronograph on brown leather, or a Tommy Hilfiger watch with a blue dial on a steel bracelet, is the kind of watch that looks right at a lunch, a café or a family visit. Keep the metal of your belt buckle consistent with the watch, and you are done.

## Weekends and casual

For jeans and a T-shirt, shorts on holiday or a hoodie on a cold evening, almost any watch works, as long as it suits the mood. Larger cases of 44 to 46 mm, chronographs, silicone straps, bright dials and analog-digital watches all belong here. A watch on a silicone or rubber strap is practical in summer, because it does not absorb sweat like leather.

Casual outfits are also the place to match the watch to a colour in your clothes. A green dial with an olive jacket, a blue dial with a navy T-shirt, an orange detail on the dial with an orange stripe on your trainers: small echoes like these make a casual outfit look intentional. The one thing to avoid is a formal dress watch with a gym outfit, which looks out of place and puts a delicate watch at risk.

Evenings out sit between casual and smart. For jeans with a blazer or a dark shirt, a watch with a black dial on a black steel bracelet, or a steel chronograph with a dark dial, bridges the two moods. A watch that has 1 strong detail, a coloured seconds hand or a textured dial, is enough to make an evening outfit feel finished without looking like you tried too hard.

## Sport and the gym

For running, football, the gym or the beach, choose a watch built for it: a light case, a silicone or rubber strap, a resistant crystal and water resistance of at least 100 m or 10 ATM if you swim. Analog-digital watches with a stopwatch and a backlight, such as many G-Shock models, are practical for training, and they are designed to absorb knocks.

Leave leather straps and plated dress watches at home on these days: sweat wears leather, and impacts and chlorine wear plating. After the gym or a swim, rinse a sports watch in fresh water and let it dry. A sports watch is also the second watch most men need after an everyday steel one, because it protects the better watch from the hardest days.

## Dial colour and your clothes

The dial is the largest area of colour on a watch, and it should sit easily with what you wear. Black dials are the most formal and go with everything. White and silver dials are bright and classic, at their best with a suit or a white shirt. Blue dials are the most versatile colour after black: they work with navy suits, grey trousers, denim and white shirts. Green dials are a modern choice for smart casual, and they pair well with beige, brown, olive and white.

Brown, champagne and gold dials are warmer and suit earth tones and brown leather. Grey and gunmetal dials are modern and calm, good with grey, black and blue clothing. A practical approach is to choose the dial colour you wear most around: if most of your shirts and suits are blue and grey, a blue or grey dial will match more often than any other.

## Case size, thickness and the cuff

Size matters as much as style. For most men's wrists, a case of 40 to 44 mm looks balanced; wrists under about 17 cm suit 38 to 42 mm, and wrists over 19 cm can carry 44 to 46 mm. The lugs, the parts that hold the strap, should not extend past the edges of the wrist. A watch that is too large looks heavy with a suit, and one that is too small can look lost on a broad wrist.

Thickness decides whether a watch fits under a shirt cuff. Dress watches are often 7 to 10 mm thick; chronographs are usually 11 to 14 mm, and sports watches can be more. If you wear long-sleeved shirts often, try the watch with a shirt before you buy it, or ask our team to measure the thickness.

To find your wrist size at home, wrap a strip of paper around the wrist just below the bone, mark where it overlaps and measure it with a ruler. Most men measure between 16 and 20 cm. Then compare the lug-to-lug length of the watch, the distance from one end of the case to the other, with the width of the top of your wrist: if the case is wider than your wrist, choose a smaller size. A steel bracelet should fit with room for a finger between the bracelet and the wrist; within the warranty, we size steel bracelets free of charge at our stores.

## A small watch wardrobe

You do not need many watches to be well dressed; you need the right two or three. The first is an everyday steel watch on a steel bracelet, with a blue, black or grey dial, 40 to 44 mm: it covers the office, smart casual and most evenings. The second is a dress watch on a black leather strap, thin and simple, for weddings and formal events. The third, if you train or travel, is a sports or analog-digital watch on a silicone strap.

Build the wardrobe in that order, starting with the watch you will wear most often, and let each new watch fill a gap rather than repeat what you already have. A second strap, for example a brown leather strap for a steel watch, can also change the character of a watch you already own.

Rotating also helps the watches themselves. A watch worn every day takes all the knocks, sweat and sun; two or three watches worn in turn share that wear and each one lasts longer. A simple habit is to choose the watch the night before, at the same time as the shirt and the shoes, so the matching rules become automatic. With three watches and two straps, you will have a watch that suits every outfit in your wardrobe.

## Frequently asked questions

### Should my watch match my belt?

The metal of the watch should match the buckle of your belt, silver with steel and gold with gold. If the watch is on a leather strap, the strap colour should be in the same family as the belt and shoes: black with black, brown with brown. A steel bracelet goes with both black and brown leather.

### Can I wear a sports watch with a suit?

It is better not to for formal occasions, because a large sports watch or a rubber strap draws attention away from the suit and does not fit under the cuff. For the office, a simple steel chronograph with a clean dial is acceptable. Keep the sports watch for weekends, travel and training.

### What watch size suits a man?

A case of 40 to 44 mm suits most men's wrists. If your wrist is under about 17 cm, choose 38 to 42 mm; if it is over 19 cm, 44 to 46 mm can look balanced. The lugs should not go past the edges of your wrist, and a dress watch should slide under your shirt cuff.

### Can I wear a gold watch with silver jewellery?

The classic rule is to keep one metal, so a gold-tone watch with a gold ring and buckle, and a steel watch with silver ones. If you wear both metals, choose a two-tone watch, which combines steel and gold and bridges the two. A wedding band is the exception most people accept.
