Wallet fashions come and go, but the styles that last are the ones that fit the way people actually pay. In Egypt that still means a mix: bank cards and a phone for most purchases, cash for taxis, tips and small shops, and often a national ID and a driving licence. The right wallet holds exactly that mix, flat and in order, without making a bulge in a pocket or taking half the space in a bag. This guide explains each wallet style for men and women, what it holds, who it suits and what to look for, together with materials, RFID protection, colours and care. ## Key points

- Choose the style from what you carry: the number of cards, how much cash and whether you carry coins.
- Men: a bifold suits most people, a card holder suits those who travel light, a trifold suits those who carry many cards.
- Women: a zip-around wallet suits a tote or a large shoulder bag; a compact or snap wallet suits a small crossbody.
- A bank card measures 85.6 by 54 mm; every wallet is built around that size, so check the slots, not just the outside.
- Do not overfill a wallet: 6 to 8 cards and a few notes keep it flat and the leather in shape.

## Start with what you carry

Before you look at wallets, look at what is inside your current one. Count the cards you really use: bank cards, an ID, a driving licence, a metro or club card, and perhaps two or three loyalty cards. Most people use 4 to 8 regularly and carry several more out of habit. Then think about cash: do you carry a few notes for the day, or a larger amount for the week? And coins: do you carry them, or do they end up in a car or a drawer?

The answers point to a style. Few cards and little cash suit a card holder. Six to eight cards and some notes suit a bifold. Many cards, receipts and a lot of cash suit a trifold or a larger wallet. If you carry coins every day, look for a wallet with a zipped or snap coin pocket. For women, the size of the usual bag matters as much as the contents: a long wallet in a large tote, a compact one in a small crossbody.

Every card in the world's payment and ID systems is built to the same ID-1 size of 85.6 by 54 mm, defined by an international ISO standard. That is why wallets look similar from outside: what differs is how many slots they have, how the notes sit and how thick the wallet becomes when it is full.

Banknotes deserve a thought of their own. Egyptian notes are longer than a card, so a wallet with a full-length note section keeps them flat, while a card holder only takes them folded. The newer polymer notes are smoother than paper and can slide out of a loose section; a note compartment with a snug fit, a divider or a snap keeps them in place.

## Bifold wallets

The bifold is the classic men's wallet: a rectangle that folds once in the middle, with card slots on each side and a full-length section for notes. It holds 6 to 10 cards, a few notes flat and often an ID window, and it fits a front or back trouser pocket or the inside pocket of a jacket. For most men, it is the right starting point.

Some bifolds add a removable card case or a coin pocket, which is useful if you sometimes leave the house with only a card and a key.

## Trifolds and larger wallets

A trifold folds twice, into three panels, which gives more card slots and more space for receipts in a compact outline. It is thicker than a bifold when full, so it suits a jacket pocket, a bag or a back pocket better than a front pocket of slim trousers. Men who carry 10 or more cards, business cards and receipts often prefer it, because nothing needs to be left at home.

Larger men's wallets, sometimes called travel or long wallets, add a section for a passport or a phone and work like a small organiser. They are practical for trips and for people who carry everything in a bag or a backpack rather than in their pockets. If you choose one, check that it closes flat with a zip or a snap, so cards do not slide out when it is upside down in a bag.

## Card holders and card cases

The card holder is the smallest wallet: a flat case with 3 to 6 slots on the outside and a middle pocket for a few folded notes. It slides into a front trouser pocket or a shirt pocket without a bulge, and it is a good match for men and women who pay mostly by card or phone. It also works as a second wallet, kept in a work bag or a car for cards that are not needed every day.

Many people who pay with their phone combine a card holder with a phone case that holds one or two cards, and carry nothing else. If that is your routine, choose the card holder for the days you need an ID, a driving licence and a little cash, and keep it in the same pocket every day so it becomes automatic.

## Zip-around and long wallets

The long zip-around wallet is the classic women's wallet: about 19 to 20 cm long, with a zip on three sides, 8 to 12 card slots, two or three note compartments and a zipped coin section in the middle. It holds everything, including receipts and a phone in some models, and nothing falls out in the bag. It suits a tote, a shoulder bag or a work bag, where there is room for its length.

Medium zip-around wallets, about 14 to 16 cm, keep most of the organisation in a smaller size and fit more bags. Brands such as Guess, Coach and Calvin Klein make zip-around wallets in more than one size, in leather, quilted finishes and monogram canvas. Choose the size from your bag first: measure the inside of your usual bag and leave room for a phone and keys next to the wallet.

## Compact, snap and wristlet wallets

Compact wallets, about 10 to 12 cm, fold or snap closed and hold 4 to 8 cards, a few notes and coins. They fit small crossbody bags, evening bags and even a coat pocket, and they are the right choice for women who carry a small bag most days.

They are also the wallet to choose when you change bags often. A compact wallet moves from a work tote to an evening clutch in a second, and it keeps the essentials together, so nothing is left behind in yesterday's bag. A snap closure is quick to open at a till; a zip keeps coins in better.

Wristlets are zip wallets with a small wrist strap, large enough for cards, notes and a phone. They work as a wallet inside a big bag and as a tiny bag on their own for a quick errand or an evening out. Phone wallets go one step further, with a section shaped for a phone and a strap to wear on the shoulder or across the body. They are useful for travel and for days when a full bag is too much.

## Materials and RFID

Smooth leather is classic, ages well and develops a soft shine with use, but it shows scratches. Pebbled and textured leathers hide marks and keep their colour. Coated canvas with a monogram, as on many Coach and Guess wallets, is light, resistant to water and easy to wipe clean. Quilted finishes look dressy and suit evening bags. Synthetic materials are lighter and cheaper but usually do not last as long as leather in daily use.

RFID protection is a lining that blocks the radio signals used by contactless cards. It stops someone from reading a contactless card through the wallet with a scanner at close range, which matters most when you travel or use crowded transport. It adds almost no thickness, and if you carry several contactless cards, it is a sensible feature to look for.

Look at the stitching and the edges as well. Even, tight stitches and smooth, painted or burnished edges are signs of a wallet that will last; loose threads and rough edges wear quickly. Open and close the wallet a few times, and check that the card slots hold a card firmly without being so tight that cards are hard to pull out.

## Colours, monograms and matching

Black and brown remain the most versatile wallet colours for men: black with black shoes and belts, brown with brown. Navy, grey and dark green are quieter alternatives. For women, wallets are a chance for colour, because a bright wallet inside a black bag is easy to find and adds a touch of personality; pink, red, beige and white are all common choices, alongside black.

Monogram wallets, with a repeating logo pattern, are recognisable and practical, because the pattern hides small marks. If you prefer a quieter look, choose a plain leather wallet with a small logo. Matching a wallet to the bag, same brand, colour or material, looks deliberate, and a wallet and belt set, such as those from Tommy Hilfiger and Calvin Klein, makes matching easy for men.

Think also about where the wallet will be seen. A man's wallet comes out at a till or a taxi 2 or 3 times a day, often in front of colleagues or clients, so a clean, plain wallet in good leather says more than a bright one. A woman's wallet is seen mostly when she opens her bag, so it can carry more colour or a print, and it is a simple way to add one cheerful accent to a bag that is black or neutral.

## Caring for a wallet

The worst thing for a wallet is overfilling it. Too many cards stretch the slots until they no longer hold a card, and a thick wallet in a back pocket bends the leather and is uncomfortable to sit on. Clear out receipts every week, keep only the cards you use, and move the others to a card holder at home or in a work bag.

Carrying the wallet in a front pocket rather than a back one helps the wallet and you: the leather bends less, the wallet is harder to steal in crowded places, and you do not sit on it. When the card slots no longer hold a card or the edges start to fray, it is time for a new wallet; repairing a worn slot rarely lasts.

Keep a leather wallet dry, and if it gets wet, let it dry naturally at room temperature, not on a heater or in the sun. Wipe coated canvas with a soft, slightly damp cloth. Once or twice a year, a little leather conditioner keeps smooth leather supple; test it first on a hidden area. With these habits, a good leather wallet can last 5 to 10 years of daily use.

## Frequently asked questions

### Which wallet style suits most men?

A bifold with 6 to 10 card slots and a full-length note section suits most men, because it holds cards and cash flat and fits a trouser or jacket pocket. If you pay mostly by card or phone, a card holder with 3 to 6 slots is slimmer. If you carry many cards and receipts, choose a trifold.

### Which wallet suits a small bag?

A compact or snap wallet of about 10 to 12 cm, or a zip card case, fits a small crossbody or an evening bag and still holds 4 to 8 cards, a few notes and coins. Long zip-around wallets of 19 to 20 cm are better for totes, shoulder bags and work bags.

### Is RFID protection worth it?

If you carry contactless cards and travel or use crowded transport, yes. An RFID lining blocks the radio signals that contactless cards use, so a scanner held close to the wallet cannot read them. It adds almost no thickness or weight, so there is little reason not to choose it when it is available.

### How many cards should a wallet hold?

Most people use 4 to 8 cards regularly. Choose a wallet with slots for those, plus one or two spare, and avoid filling it beyond that: overfilled slots stretch and stop holding cards, and the wallet becomes thick and uncomfortable. Keep rarely used cards in a second card holder at home.
