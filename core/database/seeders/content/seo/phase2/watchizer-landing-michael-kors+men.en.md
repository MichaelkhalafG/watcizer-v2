A man's outfit has two places where metal shows: the wrist and the waist. When the watch and the belt buckle agree, the whole look appears planned; when they clash, even good clothes look accidental. Michael Kors makes both, so this page treats them as a pair: which belt goes with which watch, for the office, the weekend, sport and evenings, and how to choose one as a gift.

## What is on this page

There are 19 **watches** and 3 belts. The watches are mostly chronographs with three sub-dials, from 42 to 48 mm, on stainless steel bracelets or silicone and rubber straps, with a few three-hand steel models with champagne, turquoise or crystal-set dials. The belts are black MK monogram straps with metal buckles. Every piece here is in stock.

## The three belts

The first belt pairs a black and brown **MK monogram** strap with a silver-tone round logo buckle. The second is a black monogram belt with a silver-tone metal buckle. The third is black with a **gunmetal** MK logo buckle, a darker grey metal that looks more discreet than polished silver. All three let the monogram show without being loud.

## Rule one: match the metals

The simplest rule is to keep one metal tone from wrist to waist. A **silver** steel watch, such as the blue-dial or the black steel chronographs, goes with the silver-tone buckles. A gunmetal watch like the Bayville goes with the gunmetal buckle. With a gold or two-tone watch, keep the belt simple and let the watch lead.

## Rule two: match the leathers and colours

A belt is part of the shoe-and-belt pair as much as the watch-and-belt pair. The black monogram belts go with black shoes, and the black and brown belt bridges black and **brown** footwear for smart-casual outfits. If your watch is on a black strap or bracelet, a black belt finishes the line from wrist to shoes.

## At the office

For work, choose a steel chronograph on a **steel bracelet**, with a blue or black dial, and the black monogram belt with the silver-tone buckle. The bracelet looks crisp with a shirt cuff, and the monogram belt adds texture without breaking a sober outfit. Keep the watch under the cuff when you are not checking the time.

## At the weekend

With jeans, chinos and trainers, a **silicone** strap chronograph is the relaxed choice: the Dylan with an orange or black strap, or a model in navy or with a red strap. Pair it with the black and brown monogram belt, which is the most casual of the three. Bright straps look best with plain clothes in white, grey or navy.

## For sport and the gym

Rubber and silicone straps cope with sweat better than leather, and a large dial is easy to read in movement. For training, a black **rubber** strap Dylan or a silicone model is the practical choice. Check the water rating on the product page: 50 m (5 ATM) allows a short pool swim, while 30 m means splashes only and 100 m or more is fine for the sea.

## In the evening

For dinners, engagements and weddings, a gold-tone watch is the dressier option: the **Lexington** with a black dial on a gold steel bracelet, the champagne gold model or the gold crystal multi-dial watch. With these, wear a plain black belt with a discreet buckle so that the watch is the only bright piece on show.

## Sizes from 42 to 48 mm

Most men's models are between 42 and 45 mm, with a few at 48 mm. On a slim wrist, below about 17 cm, a **42** to 44 mm watch looks balanced; on a larger wrist, 45 to 48 mm sits well. A big chronograph also looks larger under a fitted shirt cuff, so if you mostly wear shirts, consider the smaller sizes.

## Colours to build around

**Blue** dials are the most versatile here: they work with navy, grey and denim, and look good with both silver and gunmetal belts. Black dials are the most discreet. Turquoise, orange and red details are for men who like one bold accent, best with an outfit in plain colours.

## Fitting the belt

A belt fits well when it closes on the **middle** hole, with a tail reaching the first belt loop. Choose about one size above your trouser waist. To check, measure a belt you already wear from the buckle's end to the hole you use, and compare it with the length on the product page before you order.

## Caring for the watch and the belt

Wipe a steel bracelet with a soft, dry **cloth** after a hot day, and rinse a silicone strap under the tap after sport, then dry it. Keep the belt away from perfume, store it rolled loosely or hung by the buckle, and wipe the buckle after wear so the metal keeps its finish.

## Questions and answers

### Which belt goes with a silver Michael Kors watch?

One of the black MK monogram belts with a silver-tone buckle: the round logo buckle on the black and brown strap, or the metal buckle on the black one. Keeping one metal tone from wrist to waist makes the outfit look planned. For a gunmetal watch, choose the belt with the gunmetal MK buckle.

### Are the men's watches waterproof?

They are water resistant, not waterproof. Check the rating on each product page: 50 m (5 ATM) allows a short pool swim, 30 m (3 ATM) means splashes and rain only, and 100 m (10 ATM) or more is fine for the sea. Never press the buttons of a chronograph under water.

### Which size should I choose?

Most models are 42 to 45 mm, with a few at 48 mm. On a wrist below about 17 cm, a 42 to 44 mm watch looks balanced; on a larger wrist, 45 to 48 mm suits well. If you mostly wear fitted shirts, the smaller sizes slide under the cuff more easily.

### How do I find my belt size?

Choose about one size above your trouser waist, so the buckle closes on the middle hole. The most reliable way is to measure a belt you already wear, from the end of the buckle to the hole you use, and compare it with the length shown on the product page.
