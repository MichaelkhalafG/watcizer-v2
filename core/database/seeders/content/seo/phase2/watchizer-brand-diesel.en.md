Diesel was founded in Italy in 1978 by Renzo Rosso and became famous for its denim and its provocative, self-confident style. Its watches carry that attitude: large, industrial-looking cases, exposed screws, thick crowns and dials crowded with sub-dials. They are made under licence by a large watch group and run on quartz movements, which keeps them accurate and easy to live with. If you want a quiet dress watch, Diesel is not it; if you want a statement, it is one of the first brands to look at.

## The sizes: why 51 and 57 mm

Most of our Diesel watches come in two sizes: 51 millimetres and 57 millimetres, with a few at 55, 56 and 58 mm. For comparison, a typical men's watch measures 40 to 44 mm, so a 57 mm Diesel is about a third wider. That size is the design: it makes the dial easy to read, gives room for several sub-dials and turns the watch into the centre of an outfit.

On the wrist, the 51 mm models are the more wearable of the two, especially on leather, which sits lower than steel. The 57 mm models suit a broad wrist — above about 18 centimetres — or someone who wants the most extreme look. Before you order, measure your wrist with a tape and compare it with the case size on the product page.

## Multi-dial faces and chronographs

Many Diesel dials show more than the time. Some are chronographs, with a stopwatch started and stopped by the pushers beside the crown. Others show **several time zones** at once, each on its own small dial, which is useful if you work with people in other countries or travel often. If you only need one second time zone, a model with a single extra dial is easier to read than one with three or four, and the face is not crowded with numbers you do not need. The product page names the functions of each model, so read it if you want a specific one. All the Diesel watches we list are quartz, so they keep accurate time without winding.

## Steel, leather or denim

About half of our Diesel watches come on a steel bracelet, often in black or gunmetal plating that matches the case. Many others use thick leather straps, and a few use **denim**, a nod to the brand's jeans heritage that you will not find on most other watches. Leather and denim soften with wear and look more casual; steel is heavier, more durable and needs sizing, which we do for free in our stores while the watch is under warranty.

A large watch is heavy, especially on steel, so if you are not used to it, a leather strap is the more comfortable way to start. The [Diesel page](/brand/diesel) lets you filter by strap material.

## Finishes and colours

Black dominates the Diesel range: about 2 in 3 of the Diesel watches we carry are black, in the case, the dial or the strap, followed by gunmetal grey, silver and gold-tone details. A black-on-black Diesel is the most versatile style, because it lets the size and shape speak. Gold-tone and rose-gold accents suit evening wear and a dark shirt. Plated cases look striking but can show wear at the edges and the clasp over time, especially if the watch is worn every day.

## Wearing a big watch well

A large watch looks best when the rest of the outfit gives it room. Short sleeves, a rolled-up shirt or a jacket with a slightly loose cuff let a 51 or 57 mm case sit freely; a tight shirt cuff fights it. Wear it a little higher on the wrist than a small watch, just above the wrist bone, so the case does not press on the back of your hand when you bend it. Match the metal to your belt buckle or ring if you can: a gunmetal case with a black belt, a gold-tone one with brown leather. And because a Diesel is the statement of the outfit, keep the other accessories simple. One option is a Diesel for evenings and weekends and a smaller, quieter watch for the office, which is a sensible way to enjoy both.

If this is your first large watch, give yourself a week to get used to its weight and size; most people who hesitate at first find after a few days that they no longer notice it, while everyone around them does.

## Water and everyday care

The Diesel watches we list are rated for 50 metres of water resistance, which covers rain, washing your hands and a short swim in a pool, but not diving or the sea. Do not press the pushers under water. With large cases, knocks are the main risk: a door frame or a car door catches a 57 mm watch more easily than a small one, so take care at first, until you are used to its size. Keep leather and denim straps away from water and perfume, and let them dry naturally if they get wet.

## Questions and answers

### Is a 57 mm Diesel too big to wear every day?

For many men it is a weekend or evening watch rather than an office one, but it depends on your wrist and your style. On a wrist above about 18 centimetres, it looks bold but balanced. On a slimmer wrist, the 51 mm models are the better daily choice. Trying both in our stores in Cairo is the fastest way to decide.

### What does a multi-time-zone Diesel show?

Each small dial shows the time in a different place, set independently with its own crown or pusher. You might keep Cairo on the main dial and Dubai, London or New York on the small ones. The product page lists how many time zones a model shows. Setting them takes a minute, and the instructions come in the box.

### Will a denim strap fade?

A little, over time, like a pair of jeans. Denim is treated to resist wear, but sun, sweat and water lighten it gradually, which many owners like because it gives the watch character. Keep it dry when you can, and if it gets wet, let it dry naturally rather than in the sun or with a hair dryer.

### What warranty comes with a Diesel watch?

Each product page states the warranty length and who provides it, as does the card in the box. It covers manufacturing defects in the movement. Keep the card with your invoice. Damage from knocks, a cracked glass and water above the rating are not covered. The [warranty page](/warranty) explains how the warranty works.
