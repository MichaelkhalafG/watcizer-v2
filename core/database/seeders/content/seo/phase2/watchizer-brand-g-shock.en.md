A G-Shock is the watch people buy when they are tired of being careful with their watch. This page explains what makes the design tough, the difference between the analog-digital and digital models we carry, and how to choose and use one.

## Built to take a knock

The idea behind G-Shock is protection. The movement sits inside a resin case that cushions it, the buttons are shielded so they are not pressed by accident, and the glass is set back from the edge of the bezel so that a fall is taken by the case rather than the crystal. It is the reason these watches survive drops from about 1 m, bumps and sport that would mark an ordinary watch.

## Analog-digital or fully digital

3 of the 5 models are analog-digital: hands for a quick look at the time, plus a digital screen for the stopwatch, alarms and date. The white and silver model is fully digital, with large numbers that are easy to read at a glance. Choose analog-digital for a more classic face, digital for maximum clarity.

## The black and gold models

The black and gold analog-digital models are the dressiest G-Shocks on this page. Gold hands and markers on a black case look sporty without being loud, so they work with a polo shirt, a casual jacket or an evening out. They are the safe choice if you want 1 G-Shock for everything.

## The white and silver digital

The white and silver digital model is light and bright, with a clean screen. White stays 2 or 3 degrees cooler in the sun than black and looks fresh with summer clothes. It suits students, runners and anyone who prefers numbers to hands.

## Functions you will use

Depending on the model: a stopwatch, a countdown timer, daily alarms, an hourly signal, a second time zone, a calendar and an LED backlight for the dark. The product description lists the functions of each watch, and the leaflet in the box explains the buttons. Once set, most of these work with 1 press.

## Water and sport

G-Shock watches are generally rated for water resistance to 200 m, enough for swimming and snorkelling; check the rating on each product page. Do not press the buttons under water, and rinse the watch with fresh water after the sea or a chlorinated pool. Resin and rubber dry quickly and do not mind sweat.

## Size and comfort

G-Shocks look big because of the protective bezel, but resin makes them light. Most sit comfortably on medium and large wrists. If your wrist is slim, try the watch first in our stores in Arkadia Mall or Heliopolis, or check the case dimensions on the product page.

## Battery and care

The battery lasts about 2 to 3 years depending on how often you use the backlight and alarms. When the screen fades, bring the watch to one of our stores for a change and a seal check. Clean the case and the strap with water and a soft brush; a little soap removes sweat and sun cream.

## Questions and answers

### Can I swim with a G-Shock?

G-Shock watches are generally rated 200 m, which covers swimming and snorkelling. Check the exact rating on the product page before you swim, never press the buttons under water, and rinse the watch with fresh water after the sea or the pool to remove salt and chlorine.

### Is a G-Shock heavy?

No. Despite its size, a G-Shock is light because the case and strap are made of resin rather than steel. Most people forget they are wearing it during sport. If you have a slim wrist, try one in our stores to check the size before you buy.

### How do I turn on the backlight?

Press the light button, usually marked LIGHT or found at the front or the top left of the case. The screen or the dial lights up for a few seconds. Some models can be set to light automatically when you tilt your wrist; the leaflet in the box explains how.
