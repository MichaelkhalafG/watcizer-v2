Choosing a phone holder is mostly about your car: where the vents are, what the dashboard is made of, and how your phone sits in its case. This guide works through those questions, then covers the bike mount, installation and safe use.

## Start with your car's layout

Look at your dashboard before choosing among the 11 models. If the air vents are horizontal, firm and close to your line of sight, a vent holder is the simplest choice. If the vents are round or fragile, use a dashboard or windshield mount. If the dashboard is curved or soft, a long-arm holder that sits in the cup holder avoids adhesion problems entirely.

## Vent holders

A vent holder clips onto 1 blade of the air vent and holds the phone with a mechanical grip. It installs in seconds, leaves no mark and keeps the phone at the level of the steering wheel. In summer the air conditioning also cools the phone while it navigates and charges. Choose a blade that does not move when you push it lightly.

## Dashboard and suction mounts

Dashboard holders use an adhesive or suction base on a flat surface. The suction mounts on this page rotate through 360 degrees, so the phone can sit upright for calls or sideways for a wide map. Press the cup for about 10 seconds on a clean, dry surface and lock the lever; it should hold firmly over bumps.

## Long-arm and windshield holders

A long arm of 20 cm or more lets you place the phone exactly where you want it, closer to the driver or higher on the glass. One version fits a cup holder, another attaches to the windshield or the dashboard. Keep a windshield mount low in a corner, never in the middle of your view of the road.

## The bike mount

The handlebar mount holds a phone securely on a bicycle for navigation, delivery apps or tracking a ride. Fit it on a straight section of the bar, tighten it until it does not rotate, and check the grip on a short test ride of 5 minutes before a long one. In strong sun, a phone on a handlebar heats up, so take breaks in the shade.

## Fitting your phone and case

The holders suit most phones of about 4.7 to 6.9 inches, including large models such as the iPhone 17 Pro Max. A thick case, a ring on the back or a wallet case can stop the arms closing fully; test the fit at home first. Phones heavier than about 200 g sit best in a mechanical grip rather than a light clip.

## Charging while mounted

Navigation and music drain a battery quickly. Combine the holder with a car charger and a 1.2 m cable, routed so it does not pull on the phone or hang near the gear stick. A 20 W or faster charger keeps the battery rising even with the screen on. The [chargers page](/subtypes/chargers) has a 45 W car charger with 2 ports.

## Driving safely with a phone

Set the destination before you move off and use voice commands for calls. Look at the map only for a glance of 1 or 2 seconds. Never mount the phone in front of an airbag or over the instrument panel. A holder is for navigation, not for watching videos or typing messages while driving.

## Heat and the summer

On a parked car in summer the dashboard can pass 60 degrees. Do not leave the phone in the holder when you park, and remove suction mounts on very hot days if they start to slip. Vent holders are kinder to the phone in summer because of the cool air flowing behind it.

## Cleaning and care

Wipe the grip arms and pads with a damp cloth every 2 or 3 weeks, because dust reduces the hold. If a suction cup loses its grip, rinse it in warm water, let it dry and press it again. Check the vent clip now and then; plastic vent blades can loosen over years of use.

## Which holder for which driver

A daily commuter in the city: a vent holder. A ride-hailing or delivery driver: a 360 degree suction or long-arm holder that keeps the phone close and visible. A driver of an older car with round vents: a dashboard or cup-holder mount. A cyclist or a delivery rider: the bike mount. Each of these 4 profiles has a model on this page.

## Questions and answers

### Will a vent holder damage my air vent?

Not if it is fitted correctly on a firm horizontal blade. Push the clip fully onto the blade without forcing it, and do not hang a very heavy phone and case on a fragile vent. If your vents are round or loose, choose a dashboard or cup-holder mount instead.

### Does the 360 degree mount hold in summer?

On a clean, smooth surface, yes. Heat softens suction cups, so check the grip on very hot days and press the cup again if needed. Rinse the cup in warm water if it loses its hold. Do not leave the phone on the mount when the car is parked in the sun.

### Can I use the bike mount on a motorcycle?

The bike mount is designed for bicycle handlebars and the vibration of cycling. Motorcycles vibrate more strongly, which can loosen the grip and is not good for a phone's camera. For a motorcycle, a mount designed specifically for it is the safer choice.
