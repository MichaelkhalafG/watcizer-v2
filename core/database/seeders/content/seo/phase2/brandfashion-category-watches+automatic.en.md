Owning an automatic watch means wearing a small engine. It needs no battery, it moves with you, and with care it can last a lifetime. This guide compares the brands on this page, explains what to expect day to day, and helps you choose your first or your next mechanical watch.

## The mechanism in brief

An automatic movement contains a mainspring that stores energy, a rotor that winds it as your wrist moves, and a balance wheel that releases the energy in regular beats. Most of the movements here beat 6 or 8 times per second, which is why the seconds hand sweeps smoothly. A full winding keeps the watch running for about 1 to 2 days when you take it off.

## Size and thickness

Cases on this page range from 34 to 50 mm, with most between 40 and 46 mm. Automatic movements are thicker than quartz ones, so expect 12 to 15 mm of height. A steel bracelet balances the extra weight on the wrist, while a leather strap feels lighter. If your wrist is slim, stay at 40 to 42 mm.

## Daily accuracy

A mechanical watch gains or loses a few seconds each day, depending on how you wear it and how warm it is. That is normal and part of its nature. Set it against your phone once every 7 days. If you leave the watch flat at night, dial up, it often runs slightly differently than on its side, which you can use to correct small drift.

## Getting started

If the watch has stopped, wind it with about 20 to 30 turns of the crown, set the time and wear it. Avoid changing the date between about 9 pm and 3 am. If you own more than 1 watch and wear this one only some days, a watch winder keeps it running and saves setting it each time.

## Water and magnets

Divers such as the Sea Explorer are made for water, usually rated 100 m or more; other automatics on this page usually manage splashes and rain. Check the rating on each product page. Keep automatics away from strong magnets, such as speakers and phone cases with magnetic clasps, which can make a watch run fast until it is demagnetised.

## Servicing

Plan a service every 4 to 5 years. A watchmaker cleans the movement, oils it, regulates it and replaces the seals. This keeps the watch accurate and water resistant for decades. Our [warranty page](/warranty) explains the cover, up to 3 years depending on the watch, and what it includes.

## Questions and answers

### Does an automatic watch need a battery?

No. An automatic watch is powered by a mainspring that winds itself as your wrist moves, so it never needs a battery. If you do not wear it for a day or two it stops; wind it with the crown, set the time and wear it again, and it will keep running.

### Which automatic brand is the most reliable?

All the brands here use proven movements, but Seiko is especially known for durable in-house automatic calibres, and Tissot for Swiss precision. Emporio Armani, Fossil, Maserati and Hugo Boss focus more on design. Whatever the brand, regular wear and a service every 4 to 5 years keep the watch reliable.

### Can I swim with an automatic watch?

Only if the product page states a rating of 100 m or more, as on many Sea Explorer divers. Make sure the crown is pushed in or screwed down, avoid hot showers and saunas, and rinse the watch with fresh water after the sea. Other automatic models are best kept away from water.

### Why is my automatic watch running fast?

The most common cause is magnetism from speakers, laptops or magnetic phone cases. A watchmaker can demagnetise it in a few minutes. Other causes are knocks or a movement that is due for a service. Our stores can check the watch and tell you what it needs.
