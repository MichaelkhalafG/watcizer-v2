A watch spends the whole week with you, from meetings to the gym to a dinner out, so it helps to choose it with your real week in mind. This guide walks through seven situations most men meet every week and the Hugo Boss watches that suit each, then explains how to cover them all with one or two watches, which size fits your wrist and how to keep a steel chronograph looking new.

## What is on this page

There are 111 men's watches, 104 of them in stock. Most are 44 mm, with others from 41 to 46 mm. About 64 sit on **stainless steel** or mesh bracelets, 27 on leather and 11 on rubber or silicone. Nearly all are quartz chronographs or three-hand watches, with a handful of automatics. Most are rated 50 m (5 ATM).

## At the office

For the working week, a steel bracelet and a calm dial are the safest pair. A **Grand Prix** or an Avery with a black or blue dial looks crisp next to a shirt cuff and goes with grey, navy and black suits. If you wear fitted shirts every day, a 41 to 43 mm model, such as the 41 mm Candor, slides under the cuff more easily.

## At an interview or an important meeting

When first impressions matter, choose restraint: a dark dial, a steel or black leather strap and nothing that flashes. The **Skytraveller** with a black dial on brown leather or a black Grand Prix on leather looks serious without being plain. Leave bright colours and rose gold for later in the week.

## At the gym or a football match

Rubber is the material for sport: it copes with sweat, rinses clean and does not stretch. The **Admiral** models on black, white or green rubber suit this best, and the Candor Chrono comes on a blue silicone strap. A big, high-contrast dial is easy to read on the move, which is why the black and white Admiral works well here.

## At dinner or a family occasion

In the evening, a little warmth in the metal looks good. Gold and **rose gold** accents, as on the Skytraveller with a gold case, the rose gold Avery or the Gregor in grey and gold, dress up a dark shirt or a suit. A two-tone bracelet, silver with gold, is the bridge if you wear both metals in your rings or cufflinks.

## For a man who loves mechanics

The **Principle** automatics show their movement through a skeleton dial: grey on brown leather, silver on a two-tone bracelet, or green on steel. An automatic winds itself as you move, so it needs no battery, but it will stop after a day or two off the wrist. It is the watch to wear when you want something to talk about.

## On a trip

Travelling calls for a watch that is comfortable for long hours and easy to read at a glance. A steel **mesh** bracelet, as on the blue Skytraveller or the black Gregor, is light, airy in the heat and adjusts precisely. Wear the watch on the plane rather than packing it, so it is never lost in a suitcase.

## At the beach or the pool

Check the rating on the product page before you swim. 50 m (**5 ATM**), the most common rating here, allows a short pool swim; 30 m (3 ATM) means splashes and rain only; 100 m (10 ATM) or more is fine for the sea. Rinse the watch in fresh water after the pool, and never press the chronograph buttons under water.

## One watch for the whole week

If you want a single watch, choose a 44 mm steel chronograph with a **black** or blue dial on a steel bracelet, such as a Grand Prix. It handles the office, dinners and weekends, and it survives the gym if you are careful. A green dial, as on the Strike or the green Grand Prix, is the alternative for a man who wants more colour.

## Two watches that cover everything

With two watches, combine opposites: a steel chronograph for work and evenings, and a **rubber** Admiral for sport and the beach. Or pair a steel watch with a leather-strap Driver or Dapper for a warmer look at weekends. Two contrasting watches will cover more situations than two similar ones.

## The right size for your wrist

Most models are **44** mm, which suits an average to large wrist. On a wrist below about 17 cm, a 41 to 43 mm watch looks better balanced; on a large wrist, 45 and 46 mm sit comfortably. A chronograph also looks larger than a three-hand watch of the same size, because its busy dial fills the case.

## Bracelet, mesh, leather or rubber

Steel link bracelets are the most durable, and we size them free of charge in our two stores in Cairo while the watch is under warranty. Mesh adjusts with its clasp. **Leather** is the warmest and most formal but should stay dry. Rubber is for sport and water. Choose the strap by where you will wear the watch most.

## Keeping a steel chronograph new

Wipe the case and bracelet with a soft, dry **cloth** after a hot day, and clean between the links with a soft brush now and then. Keep perfume away from leather straps. Avoid knocking the watch against desks and door frames, and have the battery of a quartz watch changed as soon as it starts losing time.

## Questions and answers

### Which Hugo Boss watch suits the office?

A steel bracelet with a black or blue dial, such as a Grand Prix or an Avery, looks crisp with a shirt and a suit. If your shirts are fitted, choose a 41 to 43 mm case, such as the 41 mm Candor, which slides under the cuff more easily than a 44 mm chronograph.

### Which Hugo Boss watch is best for sport?

A model on rubber, such as the Admiral on black, white or green rubber, or the Candor Chrono on a blue silicone strap. Rubber copes with sweat and rinses clean. Check the water rating before swimming: 50 m allows a short pool swim, and 100 m or more suits the sea.

### Do the automatic watches need a battery?

No. The Principle automatics wind themselves with the movement of your wrist and show the mechanism through a skeleton dial. If you leave the watch off for a day or two it will stop; set the time, give it a few gentle shakes and it starts again.

### What size should I choose?

Most models are 44 mm, which suits an average to large wrist. On a wrist below about 17 cm, choose 41 to 43 mm; on a large wrist, 45 or 46 mm. A chronograph looks larger than a three-hand watch of the same diameter, so measure your wrist before you decide.
