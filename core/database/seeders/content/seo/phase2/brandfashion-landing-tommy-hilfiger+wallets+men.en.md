## The anatomy of a wallet

A men's wallet is a small set of compartments, and knowing them makes comparison easy. **Card slots** hold bank cards and IDs; most bifolds have 6 to 8. A **note section**, the long pocket along the top, keeps banknotes flat. A **coin pocket**, zipped or with a snap, holds change. Some wallets add an **ID window** for a national ID or a driving licence, and hidden slip pockets behind the card slots for receipts. The product photos show the inside of most wallets, so you can count the slots before you order.

## Bifold wallets

The **bifold** is the most common wallet on this page: it folds once in the middle, holds cards on both sides and notes along the top, and fits a trouser or jacket pocket. It suits most men, from students to managers. Choose a slimmer bifold if you carry it in a front pocket, and one with extra slots if you carry many cards and still use cash.

## Coin-and-card wallets and card cases

A **coin-and-card wallet** adds a pocket for change, useful in Egypt, where coins and small notes are still part of daily life. A **card case** is the slimmest option: a few card slots and no note section, ideal for a front pocket or a small crossbody bag, and for anyone who mostly pays by card or phone. Many men carry a card case for daily errands and a full bifold for travel.

## Genuine leather and how it ages

Many wallets on this page are made of genuine leather, and the title says so when they are. Leather softens and darkens slightly with use, and the creases that form where it folds are part of its character. Smooth leather looks the most formal; patterned and textured leathers, such as the **H-patterned** finishes, hide small scratches better. A good leather wallet looks better after a year than on the first day.

## Black or brown

**Black** is the most versatile and formal choice and goes with black shoes, belts and watch straps. **Brown**, from tan to dark chocolate, looks warmer and pairs with brown shoes and leather watch straps. If your belts and shoes are mostly one colour, match your wallet to them; if you wear both, a black wallet is the safer single choice.

## How many cards do you carry

Empty your current wallet and count. Up to 4 or 5 cards: a card case keeps your pocket flat. Between 6 and 10 cards and some cash: a standard bifold. More than 10 cards, or receipts and photos too: a bifold with extra slots or a coin-and-card wallet. Overfilling a slim wallet is the fastest way to stretch it out of shape, so choose a size with a little room to spare.

## Caring for a leather wallet

Do not carry a wallet in a back pocket all day in summer; heat, sweat and pressure wear leather fastest, and sitting on a thick wallet strains your back too. Clear out old receipts every 1 or 2 weeks so it keeps its shape. If it gets wet, let it dry naturally at room temperature, never on a heater or in the sun. Avoid perfume and creams on the leather.

## Questions and answers

### How do I know a wallet is genuine leather?

The product title or description says "genuine leather" when it is. Genuine leather has a natural, slightly irregular grain and a soft, warm feel, and it develops a patina with use. If the material matters to you, check the product page or ask our customer service on WhatsApp before you order.

### Which wallet fits a front pocket?

A card case or a slim bifold. They are thin enough to sit flat in a front trouser pocket without a bulge, which is also more secure in crowded places. Keep to the cards you really use, and move the rest to a second wallet at home.

### Can I shorten the belt in a set?

Usually, yes. A belt with an automatic buckle can be trimmed at the tip and then adjusts in small steps; a classic belt can be shortened at the buckle end by a leather shop. Measure a belt you already wear and compare it with the length on the product page first.
