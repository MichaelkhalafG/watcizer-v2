An analog-digital watch, sometimes called ana-digi, combines two displays in one case. The hands tell the time the traditional way, while a small LCD window shows numbers and runs extra functions. This guide explains how to use one and why Naviforce models are popular with younger buyers.

## Two displays in one watch

The hands and the digital window run from the same quartz movement but can show different things. Many owners set the digital screen to a second time zone, which is handy for anyone with family in the Gulf, 1 or 2 hours ahead of Cairo. Others keep it on the date and day, or switch it to the stopwatch when they need it.

## What the digital screen can do

On the Naviforce models here the screen typically offers the date and day, a daily alarm, an hourly chime, a stopwatch to a hundredth of a second, a countdown timer and a 12 or 24-hour format. A small button lights the screen and sometimes the dial for 3 seconds, so you can read it in the dark. The functions are listed in each product description.

## Setting the time

Each display is set separately. The hands are adjusted with the crown, like any watch. The digital part uses the 4 buttons: usually one button changes the mode, one holds to enter setting mode, and the others move between digits. The instruction leaflet in the box shows the exact sequence, and our store team can set it for you when you collect the watch.

## Sizes and fit

All digital watches on this page measure 44 to 46 mm across. That size gives the digital screen room to be readable, but it also means the watch sits large on the wrist. It suits men with average or larger wrists best. If your wrist is under 17 cm, try the watch in our store first or check the lug-to-lug length on the product page.

## Steel and mesh bracelets

The models here come on stainless steel link bracelets or fine mesh bracelets. A link bracelet is shortened by removing links, which our stores do for you. A mesh bracelet, usually 22 mm wide, adjusts with a sliding clasp, so you can change the fit in seconds at home. Both handle water and sweat better than leather and suit daily wear.

## Dial colours

Black is the most common dial, followed by burgundy, blue, white and navy. Burgundy, found on 3 of the 8 models, is the most distinctive option on this page and pairs well with silver steel. Black is the most versatile. Blue and navy look sporty but still neat enough for a shirt. The digital window is easiest to read on lighter dials and with the backlight on at night.

## Battery life

An analog-digital watch uses a single battery for both displays. Using the backlight and the alarm often shortens its life, so expect 1 to 2 years depending on use. When the screen starts to fade or the backlight dims, it is time for a replacement. Our stores change batteries and test the seals at the same time.

## Water resistance

Most analog-digital watches resist splashes and hand washing, and some are rated 30 to 50 m. Check the rating on the product page before showering or swimming, and never press the buttons under water, because that is how water reaches the electronics. Dry the watch if it gets wet and keep it away from very hot water.

## Questions and answers

### What is an analog-digital watch?

It is a watch with traditional hands and a small digital screen in the same dial. The hands show the time, and the screen shows the date, a second time zone, alarms, a stopwatch or a timer. Both run from one quartz movement and one battery, so it is as easy to own as an ordinary quartz watch.

### How do I set the digital time?

Use the buttons on the side: one changes the mode, another enters setting mode when held, and the others move between digits. The leaflet in the box shows the exact steps for each model. If you prefer, our store team will set the hands and the screen for you when you buy or collect the watch.

### Why does the screen look faint?

A faint digital screen usually means the battery is getting low, especially if the backlight is also dim. Using the light and the alarm often drains the battery faster. Bring the watch to one of our stores for a battery change; the team will also check the seals so the water resistance stays as rated.

### Can I swim with these watches?

Only if the product page states a rating of 50 m or more, and even then avoid pressing the buttons under water. Most analog-digital models are designed for splashes and daily wear rather than swimming. For the pool or the sea, a sports watch with 100 m or 200 m resistance is the better choice.
