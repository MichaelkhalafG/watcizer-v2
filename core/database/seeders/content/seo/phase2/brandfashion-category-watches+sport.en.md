A sport watch is chosen for what you do, not only for how it looks. Training, swimming, driving or a long day outdoors all ask different things of a watch. This guide matches the models on this page to those activities and explains what each feature is for.

## Match the watch to the activity

For the gym, choose a light case on rubber or silicone, so it does not slide or rub during lifts. For swimming, choose a rating of at least 100 m and a screw-down or tight crown. For football and running, a G-Shock or a resin case survives knocks. For driving and travel, a chronograph with a tachymeter is both useful and smart. Each product page lists these details.

## Straps for sport

Rubber, on about 25 models, is the best strap for sweat and water: it dries fast and stays comfortable in the heat. Steel, on about 23 models, is the most durable but heavier and warmer in the sun. Silicone is the softest and lightest. Resin, used by G-Shock, is tough and light. Leather looks good but is best kept away from water and sweat.

## Size and weight

Cases range from 42 to 57 mm. Larger dials are easier to read on the move, but weight matters during long sessions. A 46 mm watch on rubber weighs much less than a 42 mm watch on steel. For most wrists, 44 to 46 mm is the sweet spot. If your wrist is under 17 cm, stay at 42 to 44 mm.

## Reading the dial in action

Look for white or luminous hands on a dark dial, which stay readable in sun and at dusk. Black dials dominate this page, with blue and navy close behind. A rotating bezel lets you time up to 60 minutes at a glance, and digital displays add a stopwatch to a hundredth of a second.

## Water resistance in practice

At 30 m a watch is splash resistant only. At 50 m it survives a shower and a short swim. At 100 m it suits regular swimming, and at 200 m snorkelling. Never press buttons under water, and rinse the watch in fresh water after the sea or the pool. The product page states the rating of every model.

## Caring for a sport watch

Rinse rubber and silicone straps after a sweaty session and let them dry. Clean a steel bracelet with a soft brush and mild soap every month. Check the crown is pushed in before swimming. Replace a quartz battery every 2 to 3 years and have the seals tested at the same time.

## Questions and answers

### Which sport watch is best for the gym?

A light watch of 42 to 46 mm on a rubber or silicone strap is the most comfortable for lifting and cardio. G-Shock and Naviforce models on rubber are popular choices. Avoid heavy steel bracelets during training, and rinse the strap after each session so sweat does not build up.

### Can I wear a sport watch to the office?

Yes, especially a Hugo Boss or Tommy Hilfiger on steel or a dark rubber strap with a black or blue dial. These look neat with a shirt. For formal meetings with a suit, a slimmer dress watch is more elegant, but many men wear their sport watch every day.

### Is a G-Shock waterproof?

G-Shock models are usually rated 200 m, enough for swimming and snorkelling. Check the exact rating on the product page, avoid pressing the buttons under water, and rinse the watch after the sea. No watch is fully waterproof, but a 200 m rating covers almost any water sport.

### Do these watches have a stopwatch?

Many do. The Hugo Boss, Tommy Hilfiger and Guess Fleet models are chronographs, and Naviforce and G-Shock digital displays include a stopwatch, alarms and a backlight. The product title and description list the functions of each watch, so you can filter for exactly what you need.
