Tissot has been making watches in Le Locle, in the Jura mountains of Switzerland, since 1853, and today it belongs to the Swatch Group, the largest Swiss watch company. It is known for bringing Swiss watchmaking to a price many people can reach, and for technical firsts such as tactile-screen watches. If you want a watch that says "Swiss made" on the dial without moving into luxury prices, Tissot is usually the first name to consider. This guide explains the lines we carry.

## PRS 516 and T-Race: the chronographs

Most of our Tissot watches are chronographs, and two lines lead the range. **PRS 516** takes its style from the racing cars of the 1960s: a round steel case, a clean chronograph dial and often a leather strap with perforations like a driving glove. **T-Race** is the bolder motorsport line, inspired by motorcycle racing, with a larger case, a sculpted bezel and a crown and pushers styled after motorcycle parts; some T-Race models are limited editions linked to racing riders.

Both lines use Swiss quartz chronograph movements: the stopwatch is started and stopped with the two pushers, and the small hands on the dial count the minutes and seconds. The [men's Tissot range](/brand/tissot) shows both lines together, with the case size of each model on its page.

## T-Touch and the solar models

**T-Touch** is the watch Tissot is most famous for in the technical world: you touch the glass to call up functions such as a compass, an altimeter, a thermometer and a chronograph. The solar versions recharge their battery from light, so they need almost no maintenance as long as they see daylight regularly. They suit someone who travels, hikes or simply likes a watch that does more than tell the time. If you buy one, keep it in a lit place when you are not wearing it, not in a closed drawer; a few hours of daylight are enough to keep it charged.

## Powermatic 80: the automatics

For mechanical watch lovers, Tissot's **Powermatic 80** movement is the reason to look at the brand. The name comes from its power reserve of up to 80 hours, which means you can take the watch off on a Friday evening and it will still be running on Monday morning. Most automatics stop after a day and a half; this one lasts more than three days. In our range the Powermatic models include the **PRX**, with its integrated steel bracelet and 1970s shape, which is one of the most talked-about watches of recent years.

## Size and straps

Tissot's men's watches in our range are large: the most common sizes are 45 and 42 millimetres, with some at 44 mm. The 42 mm models are the more versatile choice for an average wrist; the 45 mm T-Race and Touch models are made to be noticed. Leather is the most common strap in our Tissot range, followed by steel and rubber. Leather suits the PRS 516 and the office; rubber suits the T-Race and the heat; steel goes everywhere and lasts longest, and we size it free of charge in our stores while the watch is under warranty.

## The sapphire glass

Nearly every Tissot we list has a sapphire crystal, which is the hardest glass used in watches — 9 on the Mohs hardness scale, second only to diamond: keys, coins and a desk edge will not scratch it in daily use. It is one of the clearest signs of the quality you get for a Swiss watch at this level, and it means the dial stays as clear after years as on the first day. Sapphire can still crack under a hard knock, so treat it as scratch-proof, not unbreakable.

## Questions and answers

### Is a Tissot really Swiss made?

Yes. Tissot is a Swiss brand of the Swatch Group, and its watches carry "Swiss made" on the dial, which under Swiss rules means the movement and the final assembly and inspection are Swiss. That is a different level of origin from fashion brands, whose watches are made under licence. It is the main reason to move up to Tissot from a fashion watch.

### What does "Powermatic 80" mean?

It is the name of Tissot's automatic movement, and the 80 is its power reserve in hours: fully wound, it runs for up to 80 hours off the wrist, more than three days. That means a watch taken off on Friday is still running on Monday. Like every automatic, it is less precise than quartz, and a few seconds a day is normal.

### How do I look after a T-Touch solar watch?

Let it see light. The solar cell under the dial recharges the battery from daylight or a lamp, so a watch kept in a drawer for months can run down. If the display starts to flash or a function stops, leave it in daylight for a few hours. Clean the touch glass with a soft cloth; it reacts to your finger, so it should be dry.

### What warranty comes with a Tissot?

Each product page states the warranty length and who provides it, and so does the card in the box. It covers the movement against manufacturing defects. Keep the card and your invoice together, because you need both. A broken glass, damage from a fall and water that entered because the crown was open are not covered. The [warranty page](/warranty) explains the details.
