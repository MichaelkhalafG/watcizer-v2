Every man has a style, even if he never names it: some dress classic, some sporty, some keep everything minimal, some like a bold piece. A watch looks best when it agrees with that style. This guide sorts the men's Hugo Boss watches at Brand Fashion into six styles, names the lines and details that suit each, and ends with advice on size, straps and care.

## The classic man

A classic wardrobe of shirts, trousers and a good jacket wants a watch that looks right for years. Choose a steel bracelet, a **black** or blue dial and a clean chronograph layout: a Grand Prix, a Peak with a black dial on silver steel, or a Champion in blue on silver. Avoid bright accents; let the steel and the dial do the work.

## The sporty man

For a man who trains, plays football or spends his weekends outdoors, the strap matters most. **Rubber** handles sweat and water: the Admiral on black, white or green rubber, and the Volane with a grey dial on green rubber or in black with orange accents. These have bold, high-contrast dials that read easily on the move.

## The minimalist

A minimalist wants fewer details, not more. Look at the **three-hand** models and the calmer chronographs: a Peak with a plain black dial on stainless steel, or a Candor at 41 mm, which is smaller than most. Black dials on black or silver steel are the quietest combination in the range.

## The bold dresser

If you like your watch to be noticed, choose colour and contrast. **Green** dials, as on the Troper with a brown leather strap, the Top in olive green and gunmetal or the green Grand Prix, look rich and unusual. A rose gold dial on black, as on a Top chronograph, or a two-tone Champion adds warmth for evenings.

## The smart traveller

A man who travels for work wants comfort and a dial he can read at a glance in any light. A **mesh** bracelet, as on the Gregor with a black sunray dial, is light and airy in hot weather and adjusts precisely with its clasp. A Taper, in navy and grey or in black, is another easy companion for long days on the move.

## The man who loves mechanics

For a man who likes to know how things work, an **automatic** is the answer. The Principle shows its movement through a skeleton dial, and the Troper Automatic in black and silver is another self-winding model in a sportier case. An automatic needs no battery, but it stops after a day or two off the wrist; set it and wear it to restart.

## Matching the dial to your clothes

The dial is the first thing people see. A **blue** dial goes with navy, grey and denim and is the easiest to wear. Black goes with everything and suits evenings. Green pairs well with beige, olive, brown and white. If you wear mostly dark suits, black or blue; if you wear more casual colours, green is a fresh change.

## Steel, mesh, leather or rubber

Steel link bracelets are the most durable and look sharp with a shirt. Mesh is lighter and more refined. **Leather**, as on the Allure in black or black and beige, is warm and formal but should stay dry. Rubber is for sport and water. If you are unsure, steel is the safest all-round choice.

## The 44 mm standard

Most men's models here are **44** mm, which suits an average to large wrist. On a wrist below about 17 cm, choose 41 to 43 mm, such as the 41 mm Candor; on a large wrist, 45 to 46 mm sits comfortably. Remember that a chronograph's busy dial makes it look larger than a three-hand watch of the same size.

## Water ratings in plain words

Check the rating on each product page. 50 m (**5 ATM**), the most common here, allows a short pool swim; 30 m (3 ATM) means splashes and rain only; 100 m (10 ATM) or more is fine for the sea. Rinse the watch in fresh water after swimming, and never press a chronograph's buttons under water.

## Two watches, two styles

Many wardrobes need two watches rather than one. Pair a classic steel chronograph for work with a **rubber** Admiral or Volane for sport and the beach, or a steel watch with a leather Allure for evenings. Two watches of different styles cover far more occasions than two similar ones.

## Daily care

Wipe the case and bracelet with a soft, dry **cloth** after a hot day, and brush between the links now and then to remove dust. Keep perfume and creams off leather straps. Have a quartz watch's battery changed as soon as it starts losing time, so a weak battery never leaks inside the case.

## Questions and answers

### Which Hugo Boss watch suits a classic style?

A steel bracelet with a black or blue dial and a clean chronograph layout, such as a Grand Prix, a Peak with a black dial on silver steel, or a Champion in blue on silver. These look right with shirts, trousers and suits, and they stay in style for years.

### Which Hugo Boss watch suits sport?

A model on rubber: the Admiral on black, white or green rubber, or the Volane with a grey dial on green rubber or in black with orange accents. Rubber copes with sweat and water. Check the water rating on the product page before swimming: 100 m or more is the rating for the sea.

### What does automatic mean?

An automatic watch winds itself with the movement of your wrist, so it needs no battery. The Principle shows its movement through a skeleton dial, and the Troper Automatic is another self-winding model in a sportier case. If you take it off for a day or two it stops; set the time and wear it again.

### Is 44 mm too big for me?

It suits an average to large wrist. If your wrist is below about 17 cm, a 41 to 43 mm model such as the 41 mm Candor looks better balanced. Measure your wrist with a strip of paper and a ruler, and compare it with the case size on the product page before you order.
