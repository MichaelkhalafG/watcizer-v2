Hugo Boss began as a clothing company in Metzingen, in the south of Germany, in 1924, and it is still best known for its suits — sharp, dark and well cut. The watches carry the same idea onto the wrist: clean dials, strong cases and colours you can wear with a jacket. They are made under licence by a specialist watch company and run on quartz movements, with a handful of automatic models. This guide explains the range we carry and how to choose within it.

## The lines you will see in the titles

The name of the line is in almost every Hugo Boss title on our site, and it tells you the character of the watch before you look at the photos. **Grand Prix** is the most motorsport-inspired: a chronograph with a tachymeter scale and racing colours. **Candor** and **Troper** are everyday chronographs with clean, readable dials. **Admiral** and **Skytraveller** borrow from diving and pilot watches, with rotating bezels or a second time zone. **Gregor** and **Peak** are bold and sporty, while **Dapper**, **Avery** and **Principle** are the dressier, slimmer choices for a suit.

If you like one model but want a different dial colour, search for the line name: most lines come in black, blue and a green or grey version, and our [men's Hugo Boss watches page](/brand/hugo-boss/watches/men) shows them side by side.

## Size: why most are 44 mm

The single most common case size in our Hugo Boss range is 44 millimetres, followed by 41, 43 and 45 mm. A 44 mm chronograph is a statement watch: it fills the wrist and reads well across a desk, which is the look these lines are designed for. On a slim wrist, choose a 41 mm model or a watch with a leather strap, which sits lower and looks smaller than a steel bracelet of the same width.

Check the thickness too, which is on the product page. Chronographs with three sub-dials are thicker than three-hand watches, and that matters if you wear fitted shirt cuffs every day. The lug-to-lug length — how far the case reaches across the wrist — decides comfort as much as the diameter does.

## Bracelet, leather or rubber

Most Hugo Boss watches we list come on a stainless steel bracelet, roughly a quarter on leather and the rest on rubber or silicone. A steel bracelet is the most versatile and the most durable; it needs sizing, which we do for free in our stores while the watch is under warranty. Leather is the most formal, but in Egypt's summer it absorbs sweat and needs rest days to dry, so it suits an office watch better than a daily beach watch. Rubber is the practical choice for heat, the gym and travel, and it is the easiest to clean.

Several models come in two-tone or gold-tone steel, and a few in black or gunmetal plating. Plated cases look striking but show scratches sooner on the edges and clasp, so they suit someone who rotates two or three watches rather than wearing one every day.

## Automatic or quartz

Nearly all of our Hugo Boss watches are quartz, which means they keep accurate time with no winding and need a battery change every 2 years or so. 6 models are automatic, including the skeleton versions that show the movement through the dial. An automatic winds itself as you wear it and stops after a day or two in a drawer, so it suits someone who wears one watch daily, or who enjoys setting it. It is also less precise than quartz: a few seconds a day either way is normal for an automatic, and not a fault.

## Looking after a steel chronograph

Never press the chronograph buttons under water, whatever the rating on the product page, because that is how water gets past the seals. Rinse a steel bracelet with fresh water after the sea or a sweaty day and dry it with a soft cloth; salt and dust collect between the links. Keep the watch away from strong magnets such as speakers and phone cases with clasps, which can disturb an automatic movement. And if the second hand starts jumping in 4-second steps on a quartz model, that is the low-battery signal: bring it in before it stops.

## Questions and answers

### Which Hugo Boss watch is best for a suit?

Choose a slim three-hand or a simple chronograph on a leather strap or a fine link bracelet, in a 41 to 43 mm case. Dapper, Principle and Avery are made for this, and a black or blue dial goes with most suit colours. Big sports lines such as Grand Prix look out of place under a tight shirt cuff, and they are better kept for the weekend.

### Does the chronograph have to run all the time?

No. The chronograph is a stopwatch, and you start and stop it with the pushers beside the crown. Leaving it running all day uses the battery faster on a quartz model, so start it only when you want to time something. If the chronograph hand does not return exactly to twelve after a reset, it can be adjusted; bring it to our stores and we will reset it.

### How often does a quartz Hugo Boss need a new battery?

Usually every two to three years, depending on how much you use the chronograph. When the second hand starts jumping in four-second steps, the battery is low. Change it soon, because a dead battery left inside can leak and damage the movement. While the watch is under warranty, the battery change is free in our stores; bring the warranty card.

### Is an automatic watch less accurate than a quartz one?

Yes, by design. A quartz watch is accurate to a few seconds a month, while an automatic can gain or lose a few seconds a day. That is normal and is not a defect. An automatic also stops if you do not wear it for a day or two; wind it with the crown or set it again before you put it on.

### What does the warranty cover, and who provides it?

The product page states the warranty length and who provides it, and the card in the box plus your invoice are what you need to use it. It covers manufacturing defects in the movement. It does not cover a broken crystal, damage from a fall, or water that got in because the crown was open or a pusher was pressed under water. The [warranty page](/warranty) gives the full list.
