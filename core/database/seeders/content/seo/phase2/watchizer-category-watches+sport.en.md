A sport watch is the one you can forget about while you train, swim, drive or travel. It should be legible in a hurry, comfortable when you sweat and strong enough to survive a knock. This guide explains what to look for and how the models on this page compare.

## What makes a watch sporty

Three things: a robust case, usually 42 mm or larger; a strap that copes with water and sweat, such as rubber or silicone; and a dial you can read at a glance, with bold hands and luminous markers. Many add a chronograph or a rotating bezel. Some, like the 5 G-Shock models here, are built specifically to survive drops and shocks.

## Rubber, silicone or steel

Rubber is the most common strap on this page because it stays comfortable in heat, dries quickly and does not absorb sweat. Silicone is softer and lighter. Steel looks smarter and lasts longest but can get hot in the Egyptian summer sun. Resin, used by G-Shock, is the toughest and lightest. A 22 mm rubber strap can be fitted to many steel models if you want a change.

## Size and weight

Sport watches here run from 42 to 57 mm. Larger cases are easier to read during activity, but they also catch on sleeves and feel heavier. For most wrists, 44 to 46 mm is a good balance. If you plan to wear the watch while running or at the gym, a lighter case on rubber or silicone will feel better after an hour.

## Water resistance for sport

Check the product page before you swim. At 50 m a watch survives a shower or a gentle swim, 100 m suits regular swimming, and 200 m is for snorkelling and diving. Never press buttons or turn the crown under water. After swimming in the sea, rinse the watch in fresh water to remove salt from the bezel and the strap.

## Reading the time on the move

Look for high contrast: white hands on a black or blue dial are easiest to read. Luminous markers glow after exposure to light, useful at dawn and dusk. Digital displays on Naviforce and G-Shock models add backlights and alarms. A rotating bezel lets you time an exercise interval of up to 60 minutes without starting a stopwatch.

## Caring for a sports watch

Rinse rubber and silicone straps with water and mild soap after a sweaty session, then dry them. Check that the crown is fully pushed in or screwed down before swimming. Replace the battery of a quartz model every 2 to 3 years and have the seals tested at the same time, so the water resistance stays as rated.

## Questions and answers

### Which sports watch is best for swimming?

A model rated at least 100 m on a rubber, silicone or resin strap. G-Shock models are usually rated 200 m and are the safest choice for the sea. Whatever the rating, keep the buttons and crown untouched under water, and rinse the watch in fresh water after swimming in salt water.

### Can I wear a sports watch to work?

In most offices, yes. A sports watch on a steel bracelet or a dark rubber strap with a black or blue dial looks neat with a shirt. For formal meetings or events with a suit, a slimmer dress watch is more elegant, but many men wear their sports watch every day without any problem.

### Is a large sports watch heavy?

Large steel watches can be heavy, but most sports watches here use rubber, silicone or resin, which keeps them light. A 46 mm Naviforce on silicone weighs much less than a 42 mm steel watch on a bracelet. If weight matters, check the strap material on the product page before you choose.

### Do sports watches have a stopwatch?

Many do. Hugo Boss and Tommy Hilfiger sports models are often chronographs, and Naviforce and G-Shock digital displays include stopwatches, alarms and backlights. The product title and description list the functions of each watch, so you can find one with exactly the features you need.
