Most of our Emporio Armani watches belong to one of three families: a diver, a chronograph or a dress watch. Each family has its own character and its own place in a man's week, and the women's models add a fourth, smaller group. This guide introduces each family, compares them side by side, and shows how two or three of them make a complete watch wardrobe.

## The three families at a glance

The **diver** family is the Sea Explorer: automatic, sporty, with a bezel and bright dial colours. The chronograph family is Racer and Carlo: quartz, three sub-dials, on steel or leather. The dress family is Gino: a clean three-hand watch, often in gold tone on leather. Pick the family first; the colour comes second.

## The diver: Sea Explorer

**Sea Explorer** is the most rugged of the three, an automatic with a diver-style bezel. We have it with blue, green, yellow and black dials, on steel and in a two-tone version. The yellow dial is the boldest face in our whole Emporio Armani range, while the blue on silver steel is the easiest to wear with anything.

## The chronograph: Racer

**Racer** is the sports chronograph, with a stopwatch and three sub-dials. It comes in black, silver, green and blue on steel, as a silver dial on black leather and as a black dial on a silver and gold bracelet. The steel versions suit the office and weekends; the leather one is the dressier option.

## The chronograph: Carlo

**Carlo** is the second chronograph line, with blue, green or black dials, one of them on a black leather strap. The two chronograph lines differ in their dial designs, so look at the photos of both side by side. Choose the one whose dial you find easier to read at a glance.

## The dress watch: Gino

**Gino** is the most understated: three hands, no sub-dials, a clean face. Our versions have a mother-of-pearl dial on silver steel, a beige dial in a gold case on brown leather, and a black dial in a gold case on black leather. It is the watch for weddings, suits and evenings, and for a man who prefers a calm face.

## Two that stand apart

The **World Explorer** in two-tone, with a blue dial on silver and gold, suits a man who wears both metals. A grey model is titled with an Arabic dial; check its photos if you want Arabic numerals. A blue model on rubber completes the sporty side. These sit between the families and add variety.

## The women's models

The women's Emporio Armani watches in stock are dress watches: gold-tone **two-hand** models with an oval case and further refined designs. Two hands, for hours and minutes only, keep the dial as clean as a piece of jewellery. They suit a slim wrist and pair naturally with gold rings and bracelets.

## Diver, chronograph or dress: side by side

On the wrist, a diver with a bezel and a chronograph with sub-dials both look larger and sportier than a dress watch of the same size. The **dress** watch slips under a cuff most easily. The diver copes best with water if its rating allows. The chronograph sits between them, sporty but smart. Think about where you will wear it most.

## The 43 mm standard

Most men's models here are around **43** mm, which suits an average wrist. On a slim wrist, below about 17 cm, a dress watch like Gino looks more balanced than a diver or a chronograph of the same diameter. Women's models are much smaller, around 32 to 34 mm, for a slim wrist.

## Building a watch wardrobe

With two watches, combine a **Sea Explorer** for weekends and the sea with a Gino for work and occasions. With three, add a Racer or Carlo chronograph for everyday smart wear. Three watches from three families, all from one brand, cover every situation without ever looking repetitive.

## Choosing the metal

Silver steel goes with white shirts, grey suits and silver jewellery. **Gold** tone, as on the Gino and the women's models, looks warm with beige, brown and black and with gold rings. Two-tone, as on the World Explorer and one Racer, bridges both. Match the metal to the jewellery you wear every day.

## Water and wear

Check the rating on each product page. 50 m (**5 ATM**) allows a short pool swim; 30 m (3 ATM) means splashes and rain only; 100 m (10 ATM) or more is fine for the sea. Keep leather straps dry, and rinse a watch in fresh water after the pool or the beach.

## Living with an automatic

A Sea Explorer winds itself while you wear it. If it sits in a drawer for a day or two it stops: set the time and give it a few gentle movements to restart it. Keep it away from strong **magnets**, such as speakers or magnetic phone cases, and have it serviced every few years to keep it accurate.

## Questions and answers

### What is the difference between Sea Explorer, Racer and Gino?

Sea Explorer is an automatic diver-style watch with a bezel and bright dials. Racer is a quartz chronograph with a stopwatch and three sub-dials, on steel or leather. Gino is a clean three-hand dress watch, often in gold tone on leather. They cover sport, smart everyday wear and formal occasions.

### Which Emporio Armani watch suits a formal occasion?

Gino, with a mother-of-pearl dial on silver steel or a beige or black dial in a gold case on leather. Its clean three-hand face slips under a shirt cuff and suits suits and evening wear. For women, the gold-tone two-hand models give the same refined look.

### Do automatic watches need a battery?

No. The Sea Explorer automatics wind themselves with the movement of your wrist. If you leave one off for a day or two it stops; set the time and move it gently to restart. Quartz models such as Racer, Carlo and Gino run on a battery that is changed every few years.
