A men's Fossil is, above all, a leather watch. The brand's warm, vintage-inspired style is at its best on a brown or black strap, and that is where most of our range sits. This guide is organised around how you will wear the watch, with jeans, with a suit or on weekends, and it explains the lines, the sizes and the specifications along the way.

## A leather-first range

More than half of our men's Fossils are on **leather**. Brown leather is the classic Fossil look, warm and casual; black leather is dressier and suits formal shoes; blue leather is the fresh option, especially with a blue dial. A leather strap also wears lighter and a little smaller on the wrist than a steel bracelet of the same case size.

## The lines in brief

**Grant** brings Roman numerals and a traditional chronograph dial. **Neutra** is minimal and modern. **Townsman** is a dressier chronograph with slim hands. **Commuter** keeps things simple for everyday wear. **Nate** is the big, rugged one at 50 mm, and **Machine** has an industrial look on steel. The product title names the line of every watch.

## With jeans and a T-shirt

For casual days, a brown leather chronograph is the natural partner. A **Grant** or Townsman with a blue or cream dial on brown leather looks relaxed but finished, and it goes with denim, chinos, white trainers and brown boots. Match the strap roughly to your belt and shoes and the whole outfit comes together.

## With a suit

For the office and formal events, choose black leather or a steel bracelet with a black, blue or silver dial, and a size that slides under a cuff: 42 to 44 mm. The **Townsman** and the minimal Neutra are the most formal choices here. Keep the chronograph pushers clear of the cuff so they do not catch.

## At the weekend and outdoors

For weekends and outdoor days, a steel bracelet or a rugged design works better than fine leather. The **Nate** and the Machine on steel shrug off heat and sweat, and a watch that lists 10 ATM, or 100 metres, can go into the sea. Leave leather straps at home when you expect water.

## Automatic, skeleton and open heart

Our range includes **automatic** models, mechanical watches that wind with your wrist. A skeleton dial shows much of the movement; an open-heart dial reveals only the balance wheel through a small window. They need no battery, and if you leave them off for a day or two, a few turns of the crown start them again.

## Sizes from 42 to 50 mm

**44** mm is by far the most common size here and suits a wrist of about 17 to 18 centimetres. 42 mm is the choice for a slimmer wrist or a dressier look, 45 and 46 mm feel sportier, and 48 to 50 mm models such as the Nate are for a broad wrist of about 19 centimetres or more.

## Reading the water rating

Water resistance appears in **ATM** or metres. 3 ATM is 30 metres, for splashes only. 5 ATM is 50 metres, the most common rating here, for rain, washing your hands and a short swim in a pool. 10 ATM is 100 metres and allows swimming in the sea. Leather straps should stay dry, whatever the rating.

## Glass

Most of our men's Fossils list **mineral** glass, which is clear and handles everyday knocks well; some list sapphire, which resists scratches better. The specifications on each product page name the glass. Whichever your watch has, keep it away from walls, stone and metal tools, the main causes of deep scratches.

## How leather ages

A good leather strap softens and darkens slightly with wear, which many people like. Heat and sweat speed this up, so let the strap air after a hot day before putting the watch away, and keep perfume and creams off it. When the strap is tired, replace it with a new one of the same **width** and the watch looks new again.

## Steel bracelets

Steel bracelets are the low-maintenance choice: rinse-proof, durable and right for every season. They are sized by removing links, which any watch shop does in minutes. Black, gunmetal and grey steel, on models such as the **Machine**, look darker and more modern than polished silver.

## Questions and answers

### Is a leather Fossil watch good for everyday wear?

Yes, as long as you keep the strap dry. Leather is comfortable, light and looks better with age. Take the watch off before washing up, swimming or the shower, let the strap air after a hot day, and replace it when it wears out. For days with water, a steel bracelet is the better choice.

### Which men's Fossil line is the most formal?

The Townsman and the Neutra. The Townsman is a refined chronograph with slim hands, and the Neutra has a clean, minimal dial. On black leather in 42 or 44 mm, either looks right with a suit. The Grant, with its Roman numerals, is a classic middle ground between formal and casual.

### Does a Fossil automatic need a battery?

No. An automatic winds itself from the movement of your wrist while you wear it. If it stops after a day or two off the wrist, give the crown a few turns, set the time and wear it. Quartz Fossils do need a battery, which usually lasts 2 to 3 years.
