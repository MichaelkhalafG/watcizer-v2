G-Shock was born at Casio in 1983, when an engineer set out to build a watch that would not break if it was dropped. The result became one of the most recognisable watches in the world, worn by soldiers, athletes, builders and fashion fans alike. Unlike most watches in our range, a G-Shock is not designed to be delicate: it is designed to be forgotten on your wrist while you work, train or travel. This guide explains how it is built, what the displays do and how to choose one.

## How a G-Shock survives a fall

The original goal, set by its engineer at Casio, was a watch that would survive a fall of 10 metres. The secret is in the construction. The movement floats inside the case on shock-absorbing points, so an impact does not reach it directly, and the case itself is protected by a thick resin shell and a raised bezel that takes the hit before the glass does. The buttons are recessed or guarded so they cannot be knocked off. That is why a G-Shock can be dropped on concrete, worn on a building site or taken to the gym without a second thought. The resin used for the case and strap is light, does not get cold or hot like metal, and is comfortable in any season.

## Digital or analogue-digital

Our G-Shock range includes **digital** models, where everything is shown on a screen, and **analogue-digital** models, which combine hands for the time with small screens for the other functions. A digital G-Shock is the easiest to read in any light and offers the most functions on one display: the date, a stopwatch, a countdown timer, several alarms and a second time zone. An analogue-digital model looks more like a traditional watch from a distance and still gives you the tools. Most models also have a backlight, so the display is readable at night.

## Sizes: big by design

The G-Shock models we list measure about 49 to 57 millimetres. That sounds huge next to a 40 mm dress watch, but resin is much lighter than steel and the case shape is rounded, so a G-Shock wears smaller and lighter than its numbers suggest. On a slim wrist a 49 mm model is the most comfortable; the 53 and 57 mm models make a stronger statement. Because the strap has a classic buckle and many holes, it fits almost every wrist without any adjustment.

## Water resistance for real use

G-Shock watches are built for water as well as knocks, and the product page states the rating of each model; the strongest in our range is rated at 200 metres. At 200 metres a watch is suitable for swimming, snorkelling and the sea. Rinse it with fresh water after salt water or chlorine, and press the buttons only when the watch is dry. Because the strap is resin, getting wet is never a problem for it, unlike leather.

## Who a G-Shock suits

A G-Shock suits anyone whose day is hard on a watch. Engineers and builders wear one because a knock against a wall or a tool will not mark it. Students and young people like it because it is light, colourful and takes a beating in a backpack. Athletes and people who go to the gym use the stopwatch and the timer, and swimmers rely on the water rating of up to 200 metres. Travellers set the second time zone and the alarms. And many people simply keep a G-Shock as a second watch for the beach, the weekend and every situation where they would not risk a steel or leather watch. In a range full of delicate fashion watches, it is the one you can stop worrying about.

If you are buying it as a gift for a son or a brother at school or university, it is one of the few gifts that will survive years of daily use without losing its looks.

## Looking after a G-Shock

A G-Shock needs very little. Rinse it with fresh water after the sea, the pool or a sweaty workout, and wipe it dry. Clean dust from around the buttons with a soft brush. Avoid strong solvents and perfume on the resin, which can dull its surface. Keep it out of a parked car's dashboard in summer for long periods, as extreme heat is the one thing resin does not like. When the battery runs low, the display dims or the backlight fades; have it changed by a watch shop that can reseal the case so the water rating is kept, because a case opened carelessly is not covered by the [warranty](/warranty).

## Questions and answers

### Is a G-Shock really shockproof?

It is shock-resistant: the movement floats on cushioning points inside a thick resin case, so drops and knocks that would break most watches do not damage it. That is the design idea behind the whole brand since 1983. It does not make the watch indestructible, but it does make it the toughest everyday watch in our range.

### What can a digital G-Shock do?

Most digital models show the time, the date and the day, and add a stopwatch, a countdown timer, several daily alarms, a second time zone and a backlight. The functions of each model are listed on its product page, and the instruction booklet in the box explains the buttons. Once you learn the modes, the watch becomes a small tool you use every day.

### Is a 53 mm G-Shock too big?

On paper it is large, but in practice resin is light and the rounded case wears smaller than a steel watch of the same width. Most men are comfortable with 53 mm within a day. If your wrist is slim, a 49 mm model is the safest choice, and the buckle strap fits almost any wrist without adjustment.

### Does a G-Shock come with a warranty?

A warranty covers the movement and the module against manufacturing defects and needs the card and the invoice. Damage from misuse and water entering after the case was opened are not covered.
