Tissot is the oldest name in our watch range. The company was founded in Le Locle, high in the Swiss Jura, in 1853, and it is now one of the brands of the Swatch Group. What sets it apart from the fashion labels we carry is simple: Tissot is a watchmaker first, and every watch it sells is Swiss made. This guide explains what that means in practice, then walks through the lines on our shelves and what to consider before you buy your first Swiss watch.

## What "Swiss made" means for you

The words on the dial are regulated by Swiss law. For a watch to carry them, its movement must be Swiss, it must be cased and given its final inspection in Switzerland, and a large share of its manufacturing cost must be Swiss. For you, that translates into tighter quality control, better finishing and a watch that can be serviced for many years. It also explains the sapphire glass on nearly every Tissot we list: the hardest crystal used in watches, which does not scratch in everyday life.

## The chronographs: PRS 516 and T-Race

Most of our Tissot range are chronographs, and two families dominate. **PRS 516** carries the spirit of 1960s motor racing: a clean round case, a sporty but restrained dial and often a perforated leather strap that recalls driving gloves. **T-Race** comes from the world of motorcycle racing; it is larger, more sculpted and more aggressive, and some versions are limited editions dedicated to racing riders. Both use Swiss quartz chronograph movements, accurate and easy to own. The [Tissot page](/brand/tissot) lists both lines with each case size.

## Powermatic 80 and PRX

For mechanical watch lovers, Tissot's **Powermatic 80** movement is the main attraction. Its name refers to a power reserve of up to 80 hours: take the watch off on Friday night and it is still running on Monday morning, where most automatics stop after a day and a half. The **PRX**, with its integrated steel bracelet and 1970s profile, is the best-known watch to use it and one of the most discussed designs of recent years. If you want one Swiss automatic that does everything, this is it.

## T-Touch and solar power

**T-Touch** turned Tissot into a technology brand: touch the glass and the hands switch to a compass, an altimeter, a thermometer or a chronograph. The solar versions recharge from light, so as long as the watch sees daylight regularly, the battery almost never needs attention. They suit travellers, hikers and anyone who likes gadgets. Store a solar watch in a lit place, not in a drawer, so it stays charged.

## Sizes and straps

The men's Tissot range on our site is mostly 42 or 45 millimetres, with a few at 44 mm. If you have an average wrist and want one watch for everything, 42 mm is the safe size; the 45 mm T-Race and T-Touch are made to stand out. Leather straps are the most common, then steel, then rubber. Leather suits the PRS 516 and the office; rubber suits the T-Race, summer and sport; steel goes everywhere and needs sizing, which we do in our stores.

## Servicing and care over the years

A Swiss watch is built to be looked after, not replaced. A quartz Tissot needs a new battery every 2 to 3 years; when it is changed, the seals should be checked so the water resistance stays as stated. An automatic such as a Powermatic 80 benefits from a full service every several years, when the movement is cleaned and lubricated. Between services, the care is simple: rinse the watch with fresh water after the sea, keep it away from strong magnets such as speakers and phone cases with magnetic clasps, and never press the chronograph pushers under water. A Tissot treated this way can be worn every day for a very long time.

If you ever notice the watch running noticeably fast or slow, or mist inside the glass, bring it to us straight away, with the warranty card and the invoice, before the problem grows.

## Your first Swiss watch

If you are moving up from a fashion watch, choose by how you will wear it. For one watch to wear every day, a 42 mm PRS 516 on steel or a PRX is the most versatile. For weekends and sport, a T-Race on rubber. For travel, a T-Touch. Whatever you choose, keep the box, the warranty card and the invoice together: a Swiss watch keeps its value better with its papers, and you need them for any service.

## Questions and answers

### Why is a Tissot more expensive than a fashion watch?

Because it is made by a watchmaker rather than licensed to one. A Tissot has a Swiss movement, Swiss assembly and inspection, and materials such as sapphire glass that most fashion watches do not offer. You pay for precision, finishing and the possibility of servicing the watch for decades, not for a logo.

### How long does a Powermatic 80 run off the wrist?

Up to 80 hours, a little more than three days, when it is fully wound. Wearing it every day keeps it wound. If you leave it longer, it stops; turn the crown 20 to 30 times to wind it, then set the time. Like every automatic, it can gain or lose a few seconds a day.

### Do I need to do anything special for a T-Touch?

Mainly, let it see light. The solar models recharge from daylight or a lamp, and a watch kept in a dark drawer for months can run down. If the display starts to flash, leave it by a window for a few hours. Keep the glass dry when you use the touch functions, and read the booklet to learn each mode.

### What warranty does a Tissot have?

Each product page shows the warranty length and who provides it, and so does the card in the box. It covers the movement against manufacturing defects. Keep the card, the box and the invoice together. A broken glass, damage from a fall and water entering through an open crown are not covered. The [warranty page](/warranty) explains the details.

### Can I return a Tissot?

Yes. You have 14 days from delivery to return or exchange it, and we pay the return shipping. The watch has to be unused, in its original packaging with its tags, and the bracelet must not have been shortened. Message us your order number on WhatsApp and we arrange the pickup.
