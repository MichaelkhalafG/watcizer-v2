A good charger and the right cable make the difference between a phone that fills up in an hour and one that crawls all night. This guide explains how to read the numbers, how to match a cable to a charger, and which model suits a phone, a tablet or a laptop.

## Phone chargers

The 20 W chargers are the everyday choice for a phone. They support USB Power Delivery, the fast charging standard used by iPhone and most Android phones, and charge a recent phone to about 50 percent in 30 minutes. Some have 2 ports, a USB-A and a Type-C, so you can charge a phone and earbuds at the same time. The super slim versions fit easily in a bag.

## GaN chargers for laptops

The GaN chargers of 45, 65 and 67 W deliver enough power for most laptops, tablets and phones. GaN, gallium nitride, makes them smaller and cooler than older silicon chargers. Models with 2 Type-C ports and 1 USB-A port share their power between devices. Some have a foldable plug or a retractable cable for travel, and some are sold with a 100 W cable in the box.

## Smartwatch and multi-device charging

The 3-in-1 charger combines a magnetic puck for an Apple Watch with a Lightning and a Type-C connector on 1.2 m of cable, all on a single USB-A plug. It is a tidy solution for a bedside table or a travel bag, replacing 3 separate cables. Plug it into a charger of at least 20 W so that all 3 devices charge at a good speed.

## Type-C cables

Type-C to Type-C cables connect a Type-C charger to an Android phone, an iPhone 15 or later, a tablet or a laptop. They come rated at different powers: 3 A or 60 W for phones and tablets, 100 W for laptops. Using a 60 W cable with a 65 W laptop charger works, but a 100 W cable allows the full speed. The Crystal-Clear series has a transparent jacket that shows the braided core.

## Lightning and 8-pin cables

Older iPhones, up to the iPhone 14, use the Lightning port, also called 8-pin. The StarFlight series cables connect USB-A to 8-pin and stop charging automatically when the battery is full, which protects it over time. They come at 3 A and in a faster 66 W version, both 1.2 m long, a practical length between a socket and a bed.

## Reading the label

The watts, W, show the maximum power. Amps, A, show the current a cable can carry: 3 A is the standard for fast charging. PD means USB Power Delivery, QC means Quick Charge, used by many Android phones. A charger marked PD 20 W will fast charge an iPhone; a QC 3.0 port will fast charge many Samsung, Xiaomi and Oppo phones.

## Matching charger and cable

Speed is set by the weakest link. A 65 W charger with a 30 W cable gives at most 30 W. A 20 W phone needs only a 20 W charger and a 3 A cable. If you want 1 charger for everything, choose a 65 W GaN charger and a 100 W Type-C cable, then add a Lightning or 8-pin cable for any older iPhone in the family.

## Safety

Good chargers protect against over-voltage, over-heating and short circuits. Avoid charging on a pillow or under a blanket, where heat cannot escape. Unplug chargers during storms if your area has power surges. Replace a cable when its jacket splits or its plug becomes loose, because a damaged cable can heat up. Keep chargers away from water, and leave 1 or 2 cm of space around them so they can cool.

## Questions and answers

### Which charger do I need for an iPhone?

A 20 W USB Power Delivery charger is enough to fast charge any recent iPhone. For an iPhone 15 or later, use a Type-C to Type-C cable; for older models, a Type-C to Lightning or a USB-A to 8-pin cable. A higher-watt charger is also safe, because the phone takes only what it supports.

### Can a laptop charge from these chargers?

Yes, with the 45, 65 or 67 W GaN chargers and a Type-C cable rated for at least 60 W, ideally 100 W. Most laptops that charge through Type-C accept them. Check your laptop's original charger: if it is 65 W or less, the 65 W GaN charger will charge it at full speed.

### Why does my phone charge slowly?

The most common causes are a cable that does not support fast charging, a charger below 20 W, or a dirty port on the phone. Try a 3 A or 60 W cable with a 20 W PD charger. Also avoid using heavy apps while charging, and keep the phone out of the sun, since heat slows charging down.
