Michael Kors is best known for women's watches, which is exactly why its men's models stand out: they borrow the brand's polish and put it on a big, sporty chronograph. The range here is compact, so it is easy to compare every model side by side. This guide explains what the men's watches share, how the lines differ, what the sizes mean on the wrist and how to choose the right strap.

## What the men's watches have in common

Almost every men's Michael Kors watch on this page is a **chronograph**: a stopwatch started and stopped by the 2 pushers beside the crown, with sub-dials that count its minutes and hours. The cases are stainless steel, the movements quartz, and the finishing has the shine the brand is known for, with polished bezels, crisp markers and a small MK logo. They are watches designed to be noticed rather than to disappear under a cuff.

## The lines: Dylan, Lexington, Bayville and Saunder

**Dylan** is the sportiest of the group, a bold chronograph often on a silicone strap, sometimes with a colourful accent such as turquoise or red. **Lexington** is the classic dress chronograph of the brand, polished steel with a refined dial. **Bayville** and **Saunder** are further men's chronographs in the range, each with its own dial layout; the product photos show the differences side by side. The line name is in each title, so searching for it shows every colour version at once.

## Sizes from 42 to 48 mm

These are large watches: the most common sizes on this page are 44, 43 and 48 millimetres, with a couple at 42 mm. On a typical men's wrist of 17 to 18 centimetres, 44 mm looks modern and balanced; 48 mm is a statement size, best on wrists above about 18.5 centimetres. If your wrist is slimmer, choose a 42 or 43 mm model, or a version on rubber, which sits closer to the skin and looks smaller than steel.

## Steel bracelet or rubber strap

Steel bracelets are the most common choice here, followed by rubber and silicone. A **steel** bracelet looks the dressiest and lasts the longest; it needs sizing, which we do free of charge in our two stores in Cairo while the watch is under warranty. **Rubber** and **silicone** are lighter, cooler in summer and easy to rinse after the gym or the beach, and they fasten with a buckle that fits most wrists without sizing.

## Colours: from black to turquoise

Black and blue dials are the most common, with silver for a classic look. A few models add a bright accent — **turquoise**, **red** or orange on the hands or the bezel — which turns a serious chronograph into a fun weekend watch. Gold-tone and gunmetal cases give a dressier or more technical feel. If this is your only watch, choose black or blue on steel; if it is a second watch, the colourful versions are more rewarding.

## Reading a chronograph dial

A chronograph dial looks busy, but it follows a simple pattern. The large hands tell the time as usual. The long central seconds hand belongs to the stopwatch and stays at 12 o'clock until you start it. The small sub-dials count the stopwatch's minutes and, on some models, its hours, while a third small dial often shows the running seconds of the time itself. A date window sits between two markers on many models. Once you know which hand does what, you can read any of these watches at a glance.

## Wearing a big watch well

A large chronograph looks best when it has room. Wear it just above the wrist bone so the case does not press on the back of your hand, and leave about 1 finger's width of space under a steel bracelet in summer, when wrists swell slightly in the heat. Shirt cuffs should be a little loose, or rolled. Keep other accessories simple, and match the metal of the case to your belt buckle or ring, so the watch looks like part of the outfit.

If this is your first large watch, give yourself a few days to get used to its weight; most men stop noticing it within a week, while everyone around them does.

## Water and everyday care

Most of these watches list 50 metres of water resistance on the product page: rain, washing your hands and a short swim in a pool are fine, the sea and diving are not, and the pushers must never be pressed under water. Rinse a rubber strap or a steel bracelet with fresh water after a sweaty day and dry it with a soft cloth. Change the quartz battery as soon as the watch starts losing time, usually every 2 to 3 years.

## Questions and answers

### Are Michael Kors men's watches as good as the women's?

They share the same brand standards: stainless steel cases, quartz movements and careful finishing. The difference is style: the men's watches are larger and sportier, mostly chronographs, while the women's watches are smaller and jewellery-inspired. Both come with the warranty stated on each product page.

### Is 48 mm too big?

For many men, 48 mm is a deliberate statement. On a wrist above about 18.5 centimetres it looks balanced; on a slimmer wrist, 43 or 44 mm is usually the better choice. Compare the case size with a watch you already own, or try both sizes in our stores in Cairo.

### Does the chronograph need to run all the time?

No. It is a stopwatch: start it with the top pusher, stop it with the same pusher and reset it with the bottom one. Leaving it running all day uses the battery faster. If the chronograph hand does not return exactly to twelve after a reset, we can adjust it in our stores.
