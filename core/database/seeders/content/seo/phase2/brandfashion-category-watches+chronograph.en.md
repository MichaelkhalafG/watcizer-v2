A chronograph combines a watch with a stopwatch. The pushers on the side start, stop and reset it, and small sub-dials count the seconds, minutes and hours. With so many models here, this guide helps you understand the parts of a chronograph and pick one from the right brand, in the right size, for the way you live.

## Reading a chronograph

The large seconds hand in the centre is usually the stopwatch hand, still until you press the top pusher. The small dials count elapsed minutes, elapsed hours and the running seconds of the time of day. The bottom pusher resets everything to zero. On some fashion chronographs the sub-dials show the day, the date and 24 hours instead; the product description tells you which.

## Size and fit

Chronographs on this page range from 19 to 57.5 mm. Most men's models measure 42 to 46 mm, because sub-dials need space. Women's models usually measure 34 to 40 mm. Chronograph cases are often 12 to 14 mm thick, so a steel bracelet balances the weight. If your wrist is slim, look for 40 to 42 mm.

## Strap choice

About 3 in 5 chronographs here use a steel bracelet, durable and suited to office and evening wear. Leather straps, on about 217 models, give a classic or vintage look. Rubber straps, on about 151 models, suit sport and summer and dry quickly. Many steel chronographs accept a 22 mm rubber or leather strap if you want a change.

## Using the stopwatch

Press the top pusher to start, again to stop, and the bottom pusher to reset. Use it to time a 5 km run, a parking meter, a cooking timer or a lap of the club. Do not press the pushers under water unless the watch is rated for it. Running the chronograph all the time uses more battery, so stop it when you do not need it.

## Battery and care

A quartz chronograph's battery lasts about 2 years, a little less than a three-hand watch, because the stopwatch draws power. When the seconds hand starts jumping several seconds at once, it is time for a change. Our stores replace batteries, reset the chronograph hands to zero and test the seals at the same time.

## Questions and answers

### What is the difference between a chronograph and a multifunction watch?

A chronograph has a working stopwatch, started and stopped with the pushers, and its sub-dials count elapsed time. A multifunction watch uses similar sub-dials to show the day, the date or 24 hours, but has no stopwatch. Both look alike; the product description states which one a model is.

### Why does my chronograph hand not return to zero?

After a battery change or a knock, the stopwatch hands can drift slightly from zero. Most quartz chronographs have a reset procedure using the crown and the pushers, explained in the instruction leaflet. Our stores can reset the hands for you in a few minutes, free of charge for watches bought from us.

### Is a large chronograph comfortable?

A chronograph of 44 to 46 mm is comfortable on most men's wrists, especially on a rubber or leather strap, which is lighter than steel. Check the thickness too: under 13 mm sits more easily under a shirt cuff. If you are unsure, try different sizes in our stores in Arkadia Mall and Heliopolis.

### Can I swim with a chronograph?

Only if the product page states at least 100 m water resistance, and even then do not press the pushers under water. Most fashion chronographs are rated 30 to 50 m, which covers rain, splashes and hand washing. For the sea, choose a sport chronograph on rubber with a higher rating.
