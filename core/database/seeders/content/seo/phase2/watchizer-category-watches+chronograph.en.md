A chronograph is a watch with a stopwatch built in. Two pushers on the side of the case start, stop and reset it, and small sub-dials count the minutes and hours that pass. This guide explains how to read one, which designs suit which wrist, and what to check before you choose.

## Reading the sub-dials

Most chronographs have 3 small dials. One counts the running seconds of the time, one counts the stopwatch minutes up to 30 or 60, and one counts the stopwatch hours. The long centre hand only moves when the stopwatch runs. On some Tommy Hilfiger and Michael Kors designs a sub-dial shows the day of the week or a 24-hour scale instead, so read the small print on each dial before you assume what it counts.

## Using the pushers

The upper pusher, usually at 2 o'clock, starts and stops the timing. The lower pusher at 4 o'clock resets the hands to zero once the stopwatch is stopped. Never press either pusher under water, even on a watch rated 100 m, because the seals around them are only watertight when they are at rest. If the hands do not return exactly to 12 after a reset, a quick adjustment in our store puts them back.

## The tachymeter bezel

Many sporty chronographs carry a scale around the rim marked "tachymeter". Start the stopwatch at one marker post, stop it at the next one kilometre away, and the centre hand points to your speed in kilometres per hour. Hugo Boss, Tommy Hilfiger and Maserati use the scale as a design signature, and it is the detail that gives a chronograph its racing look.

## Size and thickness

A chronograph movement takes more space than a simple one, so these watches are often a little larger and thicker. Men's models here usually measure 42 to 46 mm across. If you have a slim wrist, look for a 40 to 42 mm case, a bracelet that tapers towards the clasp, and short lugs. The thickness is listed on many product pages; anything under 12 mm slides more easily under a shirt cuff.

## Steel, leather or rubber straps

A steel bracelet is the most common choice and suits office and weekend. Leather, often brown or black with contrast stitching, softens the sporty look and pairs well with a jacket. Rubber is the lightest and the easiest to clean, ideal in the Egyptian summer and at the beach. Watches on rubber can usually take a 22 mm leather strap later if you want a change.

## Women's chronographs

About 1 in 6 chronographs on this page is designed for women. They are smaller, often between 36 and 40 mm, and many add crystals around the bezel or as hour markers. Rose gold and gold tones dominate, with silver and two-tone options for those who prefer something cooler. A women's chronograph gives a bold, confident look that a simple dress watch cannot.

## Caring for a chronograph

Run the stopwatch now and then, even if you rarely time anything, to keep the mechanism moving. On a quartz chronograph a battery lasts around 2 years, or less if the stopwatch runs all day. Wipe the case and bracelet with a soft cloth, keep the watch away from magnets such as speakers and phone cases, and have the water resistance tested after any battery change.

## Automatic chronographs

A small number of chronographs here are automatic, wound by the motion of your wrist instead of a battery. They are thicker and heavier than quartz models, their seconds hand sweeps smoothly, and many show the movement through a clear case back. Hugo Boss and Tissot offer the most interesting examples. An automatic chronograph needs a service every 4 to 5 years, and in return it never needs a battery and can last for decades with care.

## Questions and answers

### What is the difference between a chronograph and a multifunction watch?

A chronograph has a working stopwatch controlled by the pushers, with sub-dials that count the timed minutes and hours. A multifunction watch looks similar but its small dials show the day, the date or 24-hour time, and its pushers do not time anything. The product title and photos tell you which one you are looking at.

### Does using the stopwatch drain the battery?

Yes, a little. On a quartz chronograph the stopwatch uses extra power while it runs, so leaving it running all day can shorten the battery's life noticeably. Start it when you need it and stop it afterwards, and the battery will usually last around two years before a replacement in one of our stores.

### Can I swim with a chronograph?

Only if it is rated at least 100 m, and only with the pushers left alone. The seals around the pushers are not designed to be pressed under water, so a press while swimming can let water in. For the beach and the pool, a chronograph on a rubber strap with a 100 m rating is the safest choice.

### Why does the stopwatch hand not return to 12?

After a knock or a battery change the stopwatch hands can drift a little from zero. Most quartz chronographs have a simple reset procedure through the crown and pushers, described in the instructions. If you prefer, bring the watch to one of our two stores and the team will realign the hands for you.
