A dress watch says a lot by saying little. It is thin, clean and quiet, and it finishes a formal outfit the way a good pair of shoes does. This guide explains the rules of the dress watch, the brands on this page, and how to wear one with confidence.

## The rules, briefly

A dress watch keeps the dial simple, with 2 or 3 hands and perhaps a date. The case is slim enough to slide under a shirt cuff, and the strap is leather or a fine metal bracelet. Bold numerals, rotating bezels and chunky cases belong to sports watches. The models here follow these rules with modern touches in colour and size.

## The right size

For men, 38 to 42 mm is the classic dress range, and most Hugo Boss models sit at 40 to 42 mm. For women, 25 to 32 mm looks elegant on most wrists. Thickness matters: under 10 mm slips easily under a cuff, and the product page often lists it. If you are between sizes, choose the smaller for a formal look.

## Dial colours for formal wear

Black dials are the most common here, followed by blue, white and green. White is the most traditional with a dark suit. Blue is modern and pairs well with navy and grey. Black is versatile and discreet. Green, on several Hugo Boss models, is the fashionable choice, best with brown leather and earth tones.

## Steel or leather

About 3 in 4 dress watches here come on stainless steel, practical in the heat and still elegant. Leather straps, on about 13 models, are the classic formal partner: black with black shoes and belt, brown with brown. A few models use rubber for a modern sporty-dress look. Many steel models accept a 20 mm leather strap for a second look.

## Quartz or automatic

Most dress watches on this page are quartz, which keeps them thin, accurate and always ready. About 5 are automatic, a little thicker, with the satisfaction of a mechanical movement and a sweeping seconds hand. If you wear the watch only for events, quartz is the practical choice, because an automatic stops after a day or 2 in a drawer.

## How to wear it

Wear the watch on the wrist you do not write with, about 1 cm above the wrist bone, so the cuff can cover it. The strap should be snug but loose enough for 1 finger beneath it. Match the metal of the watch to your belt buckle and cufflinks if you can. A dress watch should appear only when you check the time.

## Care between events

Keep leather straps dry and away from perfume; put the watch on after perfume has dried. Store the watch in its box, dial up, away from direct sun. Wipe a steel bracelet after each wear. A dress watch worn rarely still needs a new battery every 2 to 3 years.

## Questions and answers

### Can I wear a dress watch every day?

Yes. A dress watch is simply slimmer and quieter, and many people wear one to work daily, especially on a steel bracelet. Choose a model with at least 30 m water resistance, take it off for sport, and it will stay elegant through years of everyday wear.

### Which strap colour goes with a suit?

Match the strap to your shoes and belt: black leather with black or charcoal suits, brown leather with navy, grey or beige. A steel bracelet works with any suit colour and copes better with heat, which makes it the safest single choice if you wear different outfits through the week.

### Is a 42 mm watch too big for a suit?

On most wrists, 42 mm looks fine with a suit as long as the case is slim enough to slide under the cuff. Very thick or oversized watches catch on the sleeve. If your wrist is slim, a 38 to 40 mm model looks more balanced; our stores can let you compare.

### Which dress watch suits a woman's small wrist?

A case of 25 to 32 mm on a fine bracelet suits a small wrist best. Michael Kors and the other women's models on this page offer gold and silver options. Bracelets can be shortened in our stores in Arkadia Mall and Heliopolis, so the watch sits comfortably.
