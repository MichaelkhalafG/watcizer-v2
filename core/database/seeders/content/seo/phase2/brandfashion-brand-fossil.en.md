Fossil started in Texas in 1984 with a simple idea: well-designed, vintage-flavoured watches at prices people could afford. Four decades later it is one of the biggest fashion-watch makers in the world, and its own watches still carry that warm, slightly retro character, with brown leather, cream and navy dials and easy-to-read numerals. This guide focuses on what sets our Fossil range apart — the leather straps, the core men's lines, the automatics and the women's collection — and how to choose.

## Why leather is at the heart of Fossil

More of our Fossil watches come on leather than on steel — more than 1 in 2 — which is unusual for a fashion brand and says a lot about its style. A leather strap softens with wear, shapes itself to your wrist and ages with a visible patina, so a Fossil on leather looks more personal after a year than on the first day. Brown leather is the classic: it goes with jeans, chinos and brown shoes. Black leather is more formal and suits a dark suit. Many Fossil straps have quick-release pins, so you can change them in seconds without tools and give the same watch a different look for summer and winter.

## The men's lines: Grant, Neutra, Townsman and Commuter

**Grant** is the most familiar Fossil: a round case, Roman numerals and a chronograph, usually on brown leather, a design that has stayed in the range for years because it simply works. **Neutra** is cleaner and more modern, with slim markers and a minimalist chronograph layout. **Townsman** is the dress line, smart enough for a suit. **Commuter** is the simplest: usually a plain dial with a date, made for the man who wants a quiet everyday watch. The most common case size across these lines is 44 millimetres, with 42 and 45 mm options. The [men's Fossil watches page](/brand/fossil/men) gathers them all.

## Skeleton and automatic models

Our Fossil range includes nearly 20 automatic watches, many with a **skeleton** dial that reveals the gears and the balance wheel, or an **open-heart** window over the beating part of the movement. They need no battery: a weighted rotor winds the spring as you move. They keep running for about a day and a half off the wrist and are a few seconds a day less precise than quartz, which is normal for a mechanical watch. A skeleton Fossil makes a memorable gift for someone who is curious about how things work.

## Choosing the dial colour

Fossil dials come in a palette that matches its leather: black, brown, navy, cream and silver are the core colours, with green and blue appearing in newer lines such as **Neutra**. A cream or silver dial on brown leather is the most classic Fossil combination and the easiest to wear with everything. A navy dial adds colour without being loud and looks especially good with tan leather. Black on black is the most formal and modern, and it hides scratches well. If you are buying your first Fossil, choose the dial colour that matches the shoes you wear most, and the strap will follow naturally.

## Women's Fossil watches

The women's collection is built on small, elegant cases, mostly 34 to 38 millimetres, with some tiny 23 to 31 mm models. **Jacqueline** is the best known: a slim round case, a clean dial and a leather or steel strap, often in rose gold. **Raquel** has a bracelet that looks like jewellery, often with a mother-of-pearl dial, and **Carlie** offers delicate dials with crystal details. Steel bracelets in silver, gold and rose gold are the most common, with some on leather. The [women's Fossil watches page](/brand/fossil/women) lists every model.

## Looking after a leather strap

A leather strap lasts for years with a little care. Do not wear it in the shower, the pool or the sea; water makes leather swell, then crack as it dries. In summer, give it a day off now and then so sweat can dry out, and wipe the inside with a dry cloth. Keep perfume and creams off it. If it gets wet, let it dry flat at room temperature, never in the sun or with a hair dryer. And when a strap finally wears out, it can be replaced: note the strap width on the product page, usually 20 or 22 millimetres on men's models, and any watch shop can fit a new one.

## Questions and answers

### Should I choose Fossil on leather or on steel?

Choose leather if you want the classic Fossil look, wear the watch mostly indoors or in the evening, and like a strap that ages with character. Choose steel if you wear one watch every day in the heat, swim often or want the most durable option. A practical approach is to buy a leather model and add a steel or silicone strap for summer.

### What is the difference between Grant and Neutra?

Grant is the traditional one: Roman numerals, a classic chronograph layout and usually brown leather, with a vintage feel. Neutra is the modern one: slim markers, a cleaner dial and a more minimalist design. Both are chronographs in similar sizes, so the choice is about style. If you like heritage looks, pick Grant; if you prefer clean lines, pick Neutra.

### Can I change the strap myself?

Often yes. Many Fossil straps have quick-release pins: a small lever on the underside of the strap lets you remove and fit it without tools. Check the strap width on the product page, and buy a replacement of the same width. If your model has standard spring bars instead, any watch shop can change the strap in a few minutes.

### Does a Fossil watch come with a warranty?

Each product page states whether the watch has a warranty, how long it is and who provides it, and so does the card in the box. A warranty covers the movement against manufacturing defects; keep the card and the invoice together. Normal wear of the leather strap, a broken glass and water damage above the rating are not covered. The [warranty page](/warranty) explains the details.
