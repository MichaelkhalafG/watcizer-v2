A dress watch is chosen for the moments when you want to look your best: a wedding, an interview, a dinner or a long day in a suit. It should be elegant rather than loud, readable at a glance, and comfortable enough to forget. This guide explains what defines one and how to choose.

## What defines a dress watch

The classic rules are simple: a thin case, a plain dial with 2 or 3 hands and few complications, hour markers rather than bold numerals, and a strap in leather or a refined bracelet. A dress watch should disappear under a cuff and reappear only when you check the time. The models on this page follow those rules more or less strictly, with some modern twists in colour and size.

## Size for a formal look

Traditionally a men's dress watch measures 36 to 40 mm, but today 40 to 42 mm is common and still elegant on most wrists. Women's dress watches on this page start at 25 mm and rarely exceed 34 mm. Thickness matters as much as diameter: under 10 mm is ideal for a shirt cuff, and the product page lists it for many models.

## Dial colours for formal wear

Blue is the most popular dress dial on this page, especially on Hugo Boss models, then black, green and white. A white or silver dial is the most traditional choice with a dark suit. Blue is modern and pairs with navy and grey. Black is versatile and slimming on the wrist. Green is the bold option of recent seasons, best with a brown leather strap and earth-toned clothes.

## Leather or a fine bracelet

Leather is the classic partner of a dress watch: black with a black or grey suit, brown with navy, beige or brown. Match the strap to your belt and shoes for a polished look. A slim steel bracelet is more practical in the Egyptian heat and still elegant. About 3 in 4 models here come on steel, and many accept a 20 mm leather strap if you want both.

## Quartz or automatic for a dress watch

Most dress watches here are quartz, which keeps them thin and accurate. The few automatic models are slightly thicker but offer the pleasure of a mechanical movement and a sweeping seconds hand. If the watch will be worn only on special occasions, quartz is the more practical choice, because an automatic will have stopped after 2 or 3 days in a drawer.

## Wearing a dress watch correctly

Wear it on the wrist you do not write with, about 1 cm above the wrist bone, so the cuff can cover it. The strap should be snug enough not to spin but loose enough to slide a finger under. With a jacket, the watch should show only when you move your arm. Avoid wearing a bulky sports watch with a suit; that is exactly the moment for a dress watch.

## Dress watches for women

The women's dress watches on this page are slim and light, often 25 to 32 mm, in gold, rose gold, silver or two-tone. Many add a delicate bracelet or mother-of-pearl details. They are designed to be worn alone or stacked with a fine bracelet, and they suit both office wear and evening dresses.

## Caring for a dress watch

Keep leather straps away from water, perfume and heat; put the watch on after your perfume has dried. Store it in its box, face up, away from direct sun, which can fade a dial. Wipe a steel bracelet with a soft cloth after wearing. A dress watch worn rarely still needs a battery change every 2 to 3 years.

## Questions and answers

### Can I wear a dress watch every day?

Yes, especially if it has a steel bracelet. A dress watch is simply a slimmer, quieter watch, and many people wear one to work every day. Choose a model with at least 30 m water resistance, avoid sport with it, and it will stay elegant for years of daily wear.

### What strap colour goes with a suit?

Match the strap to your shoes and belt. Black leather goes with black or charcoal suits, brown leather with navy, grey or beige. A steel bracelet works with any colour of suit and is the safest choice if you wear different outfits through the week, and it copes better with heat and humidity.

### Is a large watch acceptable with a suit?

A watch up to about 42 mm looks fine with a suit on most wrists, as long as it is slim enough to sit under the cuff. Very large or thick watches catch on the sleeve and look sporty. If your favourite watch is large, keep it for weekends and choose a slimmer piece for formal events.

### Which dress watch suits a small wrist?

Choose a case of 36 to 40 mm for men or 25 to 32 mm for women, with short lugs and a thin profile. A leather strap that can be cut or a bracelet that can be shortened gives the best fit. Our stores can adjust the bracelet and help you compare sizes in person.
