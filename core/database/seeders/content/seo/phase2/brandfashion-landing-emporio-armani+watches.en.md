Choosing an Emporio Armani watch is easier in four steps than in one: decide how it should run, what it should do, what colour it should be and what it should sit on. This guide takes those four decisions in order, using the watches we have in stock for men and women, and then adds advice on sizes, matching pairs, care and gifts.

## Step 1: automatic or quartz

An **automatic** watch winds itself as you move your wrist and needs no battery; a quartz watch runs on a battery and keeps time even after days in a drawer. The Sea Explorer models here are automatics; most of the others are quartz. Choose automatic if you wear one watch every day and enjoy the mechanics, quartz if you rotate several.

## Step 2: what the watch should do

A three-hand watch, like **Gino**, shows the time and often the date, nothing more, which keeps the dial clean. A chronograph, like Racer and Carlo, adds a stopwatch and three small sub-dials for a sportier look. The diver-style Sea Explorer adds a rotating bezel. Decide whether you want a calm face or a busy one before you look at colours.

## Step 3: the dial colour

The range in stock covers **blue**, green, black, silver, grey, beige, yellow and mother-of-pearl dials. Blue is the easiest to wear every day; black is the most formal; green and yellow make a statement; mother-of-pearl shimmers softly and suits evenings. Choose the colour you would happily look at fifty times a day.

## Step 4: steel, leather or rubber

A stainless steel bracelet is the most durable and suits work and evenings. **Leather**, as on the Gino with a gold case or the Racer with a silver dial, feels warmer and more formal but should stay dry. Rubber and silicone straps are for sport, the beach and hot days. The strap changes the character of the same watch completely.

The **Sea Explorer** is the most technical watch here: an automatic movement, a diver-style bezel and dials in blue, green, yellow and black, including a two-tone version. ## Sizes for men and women

Men's Emporio Armani watches are mostly around **43** mm, which suits an average wrist; on a wrist below about 17 cm, look for the smaller cases on the product pages. Women's models are much smaller, around 32 to 34 mm, so they sit neatly on a slim wrist and slide under a sleeve.

## A matching pair

For an engagement or an anniversary, choose two watches that share a **metal** and a design idea rather than an identical model: a men's Gino and a women's Gino, or a men's gold-tone watch with a women's two-hand gold-tone model. Matching metal is what makes two watches look like a pair.

## Water and everyday wear

Check the rating on each product page. 50 m (**5 ATM**) allows a short pool swim; 30 m (3 ATM) means splashes and rain only; 100 m (10 ATM) or more is fine for the sea. Keep leather straps away from water and perfume, and rinse rubber straps after the beach.

## Caring for an automatic

An automatic stops if it stays off the wrist for a day or two. To restart it, set the time and give it a few gentle shakes, or wind it through the **crown** if the model allows. Keep it away from strong magnets such as speakers and phone cases with magnetic clasps, which can disturb its timekeeping.
