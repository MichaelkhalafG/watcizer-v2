## Quartz or automatic

About 9 in 10 of our watches run on quartz: a battery drives a small motor, the time is accurate to a few seconds a month, and the watch keeps running in a drawer. An automatic has no battery. A rotor inside winds the spring each time you move your wrist, the seconds hand sweeps rather than ticks, and the watch stops after a day or two off the wrist. Choose quartz for convenience and accuracy, and automatic if you enjoy the craft of a mechanical movement.

## Finding the right case size

Case sizes run from 19 mm for the smallest women's pieces to around 46 mm for oversized sports models. Most men are comfortable between 40 and 44 mm, and most women between 28 and 36 mm. A wrist under 17 cm usually suits the lower end of each range. The case size is written on every product page, and the lug width matters too: a short distance from lug to lug keeps a large dial from overhanging the wrist.

## Dial colours that last

Black and blue dials are the most common in our collection, followed by white, silver and green; a Michael Kors piece often adds gold or rose gold. Black is the safest single choice and suits every strap colour. Blue reads as smart but less formal, green is the fashionable choice of the last few seasons, and white or silver dials look crisp with a suit. If you own only one watch, a black or blue dial on a steel bracelet goes with almost everything.

## Bracelet, leather or rubber

A stainless steel bracelet, usually 20 or 22 mm wide on a man's watch, is the most versatile option and the most common in our range; it shrugs off water and suits work and weekends alike. Leather straps feel warmer and dressier, but they dislike water and sweat. Rubber and silicone straps are light, easy to clean and right for the gym or the beach. When a bracelet needs shortening, our stores can remove links for you, so the watch arrives at the right fit.

## Water resistance in plain words

A 30 m rating means splashes and hand washing only. At 50 m a watch survives a shower or a swim in calm water, and 100 m is the level for regular swimming. Divers rated 200 m or more are built for snorkelling and diving. Never press a chronograph pusher under water, and have the gaskets checked if the watch has been opened, because seals age with time.

## Questions and answers

### Which watch brand should I start with?

Start from your budget and your style. Tommy Hilfiger and Guess offer lively designs at friendly prices, Michael Kors is the favourite for women who like gold tones and crystals, and Hugo Boss, Fossil and Tissot suit buyers who want a more grown-up look. ### How do I know a watch will fit my wrist?

Measure your wrist with a strip of paper and compare it with the case size on the product page. Most men wear 40 to 44 mm and most women 28 to 36 mm. A steel bracelet can be shortened in our stores, and you can exchange the watch within 14 days if it does not feel right.

### Do automatic watches need a battery?

No. An automatic winds itself from the movement of your wrist and needs no battery at all. If you leave it off for a day or two it stops; give the crown a few turns or shake it gently, then set the time again. A watch winder keeps it running if you rotate several watches.

### Can I see a watch before I buy it?

Yes. Our two stores in Cairo, in Arkadia Mall and in Heliopolis, let you try a watch on and compare sizes. If you order online, cash on delivery lets you open the parcel and inspect the watch in front of the courier before paying, and refuse it with no delivery fee.

### How long is the warranty on a watch?

Up to 3 years depending on the watch. Each product page states the length of its warranty and whether the brand's agent or our store provides it, and the warranty page lists what is covered. Keep the invoice and the warranty card, because they are needed for any claim.
