Maserati was founded in Bologna in 1914 and became one of the great names of Italian motor racing and grand-touring cars, with its trident logo taken from the Neptune fountain in the city. The watches that carry the name are made under licence by an Italian watch company and translate the brand's design language — dashboard dials, tachymeter scales, carbon-fibre textures and the trident — into steel cases. They run on quartz movements. This guide explains the lines we carry.

## The lines in the titles

Most Maserati titles name the line, and each line borrows a different part of the car world. **Traguardo**, Italian for "finish line", is the classic racing chronograph, with sub-dials and a tachymeter on the bezel. **Successo** is another chronograph, with a more modern, layered dial. **Competizione** and **Potenza** lean the most sporty, while **Epoca** and **Royale** are the elegant ones, with slimmer profiles and cleaner faces for a suit. **Ingegno** and **Triconic** stand out for their design details, such as skeleton-style or multi-level dials.

The line name is the quickest way to find a sister model in another colour. Most come in black, blue and silver-dial versions, and the [Maserati page](/brand/maserati) shows them all with filters for strap and dial colour.

## Size: mostly 45 mm

The most common case size in our Maserati range is 45 millimetres, with others at 42, 43 and 44 mm. A 45 mm chronograph is a big, confident watch that suits an average or larger wrist and reads well at a glance, like a car's instrument cluster. If your wrist is slim, look at a 42 mm model or one of the dressier lines on leather, which sits closer to the wrist and looks smaller than a steel bracelet of the same width.

## Steel or leather

About half of our Maserati watches come on a stainless steel bracelet and most of the rest on leather, with a few on nylon, fabric or rubber. Some Maserati leather straps have contrast stitching, like the seats of a sports car, and leather suits the dressier Epoca and Royale lines. In the heat of an Egyptian summer, leather absorbs sweat and needs time to dry, so a steel version is the better choice if you wear one watch every day. A leather Maserati is at its best for evenings, the office and cooler months.

## Reading a racing chronograph

Many Maserati dials carry a **tachymeter**, the scale printed around the edge of the dial or on the bezel. It turns the stopwatch into a speed meter: start the chronograph at one marker on a road, stop it at the next kilometre post, and the seconds hand points to your average speed in kilometres per hour on the scale. Most owners never use it, but it is part of what makes a racing chronograph look the way it does. The sub-dials count the minutes and, on some models, the hours of the stopwatch, while a small seconds dial shows the running time.

## Gunmetal, gold and the trident

Beside plain steel, some Maserati models — in lines such as **Traguardo** and **Successo** — come in gunmetal grey or with gold-tone accents, and the trident logo usually appears on the dial or the crown. Gunmetal looks technical and modern; gold-tone details make the watch more formal. As with all plated finishes, the edges and the clasp show wear sooner than plain steel, so if you want one watch for every day, plain steel is the safest choice, and a plated one makes an excellent second watch.

## Care and water

Each product page states the water resistance of the model. Whatever the rating, never press the chronograph pushers under water, and make sure the crown is pushed in fully before washing your hands or going out in the rain. Wipe a steel bracelet with a soft cloth after a hot day, and keep leather away from water and perfume. A quartz battery usually lasts 2 to 3 years; when the seconds hand starts jumping in steps, it is time to change it, which we do for free in our stores while the watch is under warranty.

## Questions and answers

### Who makes Maserati watches?

Maserati watches are made under licence by an Italian watch company, which designs them with the car maker's style and uses quartz movements. That is how most car brands work with watches. It means you get the Maserati design and name with the reliability of a modern quartz movement, at a price well below the cars themselves.

### Which Maserati line is best for the office?

Epoca and Royale are the most office-friendly: slimmer cases, calm dials and often a leather strap that sits under a shirt cuff. A black or blue dial goes with most suits. The racing chronographs such as Traguardo and Competizione are bolder and better suited to casual clothes and weekends.

### What is the scale around the dial for?

On most chronographs it is a tachymeter, which measures average speed over a known distance using the stopwatch. Start the chronograph, stop it after exactly one kilometre, and read the speed in kilometres per hour where the seconds hand points. It works for anything that travels a known distance, from a car to a runner.
