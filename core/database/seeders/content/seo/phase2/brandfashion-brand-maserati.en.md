The Maserati brothers founded their company in Bologna in 1914, and the trident badge on every Maserati car comes from the city's Neptune fountain. The watches that bear the name are designed and made under licence by an Italian watch company, and their collections borrow the language of the cars: each line carries an Italian word chosen for what it evokes. This guide explains those names, then helps you choose a size, a movement and a strap.

## What the Italian line names mean

Knowing the words makes the range easier to read. **Velocità** means speed, and its watches are sporty chronographs. **Successo** means success, a confident chronograph for daily wear. **Traguardo**, the finish line, is the classic racing chronograph with a tachymeter. **Competizione**, competition, and **Potenza**, power, are the boldest. **Circuito**, the circuit, takes its cues from the race track. **Epoca**, era, and **Royale** are the elegant, classic pieces for a suit. **Ingegno**, ingenuity, is where you find skeleton dials that show the mechanism, and **Triconic** plays with layered, three-dimensional dial designs.

## Size: 44 mm and its neighbours

The most common case size in our Maserati range is 44 millimetres, followed by 45 and 42 mm. A 44 mm watch is confident without being oversized on an average man's wrist. If your wrist measures under about 17 centimetres, a 42 mm Epoca or Royale will look better balanced; above 18 centimetres, a 45 mm chronograph wears naturally. Leather straps make any size look a little smaller than a steel bracelet, because they sit closer to the wrist. The [Maserati watches page](/brand/maserati/watches) shows every model with its size.

## Quartz chronograph or automatic

Most Maserati watches we list are quartz, and many of those are chronographs: accurate, easy to own and full of motorsport detail on the dial. A smaller group of around 18 models is **automatic**, powered by a self-winding movement that needs no battery; some of them show the movement through a skeleton dial or a transparent back. An automatic runs for about a day and a half off the wrist and may gain or lose a few seconds a day, which is normal. Choose quartz for simplicity; choose automatic for the pleasure of a mechanical watch.

## How to use the tachymeter

Many Maserati chronographs, Traguardo above all, carry a **tachymeter** scale: the numbers printed around the edge of the dial or on the bezel, often running from about 60 up to 400. Start the stopwatch as you pass one kilometre marker on a road and stop it at the next; the long seconds hand then points to your average speed in kilometres per hour. If it took 30 seconds, the scale shows 120. It also works for anything that moves over a known distance, a runner on a track or a train between two posts. Most owners never time anything, but understanding the scale makes the dial much more than decoration.

## Dial colours and how to wear them

Blue, black and silver are the 3 main dial colours in the range. Blue is the signature colour of Italian motor racing and looks fresh with grey, navy and white; black is the sportiest and the easiest with dark clothes; silver is the dressiest, and pairs best with a steel bracelet and a suit. Gunmetal and two-tone models add a technical or a classic touch. Whatever you choose, match the strap to your shoes and belt, brown with brown and black with black, and the watch will look like part of the outfit rather than an afterthought.

If you want one watch for every occasion, a blue dial on a steel bracelet is the smartest compromise: lively enough for the weekend and calm enough for the office.

## Water resistance you can use

Many of our Maserati models are rated at 100 metres, which is more than most fashion watches offer. That makes them suitable for swimming and the sea, as long as the crown is pushed in fully and the chronograph pushers are never pressed under water. Other models are rated at 50 metres, enough for a short pool swim. Rinse the watch with fresh water after the sea, especially on a steel bracelet, and dry it with a soft cloth. The exact rating is on every product page.

## Steel, leather, mesh and rubber

Steel bracelets are the most common, followed closely by leather, which suits the dressier Epoca and Royale lines. A few models come on a **mesh** bracelet, a woven steel band that adjusts to any wrist by sliding the clasp, and a handful use silicone or rubber for summer and sport. Steel needs sizing, which takes a few minutes in our stores; leather should be kept away from water, sweat and perfume to last.

## Questions and answers

### Can I swim with a Maserati watch?

With the models rated at 100 metres, yes: they are fine for swimming and the sea, as long as the crown is pushed in and you never press the chronograph pushers under water. Models rated at 50 metres are suitable only for a short swim in a pool. Check the rating on the product page before you go into the water, and rinse the watch afterwards.

### What is a skeleton watch?

A skeleton watch has a dial that is partly or fully cut away, so you can see the gears, the springs and the balance wheel of the movement working inside. In the Maserati range, the Ingegno line is the place to look for them. They are usually automatic, need no battery and make a striking conversation piece.

### Which Maserati is best for a suit?

Epoca and Royale are the most formal: calmer dials, slimmer cases and often a leather strap that slides under a shirt cuff. A black or blue dial with a black or brown leather strap suits most suits. The large racing chronographs are better kept for casual wear and weekends.

### Does a Maserati watch come with a warranty?

Each product page states whether the watch has a warranty, how long it is and who provides it, and the card in the box shows the same. A warranty covers the movement against manufacturing defects and needs the card and the invoice. Damage from knocks, a broken glass and water that entered through an open crown are not covered.
