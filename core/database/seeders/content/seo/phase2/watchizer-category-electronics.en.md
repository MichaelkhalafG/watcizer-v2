## Understanding watts

A charger's power is measured in watts. A basic phone charger delivers 5 to 10 W, a fast phone charger 20 W, and a laptop charger 45 to 67 W. A higher-watt charger does not harm a phone; the phone simply takes the power it supports. That is why a 65 W charger can safely charge a phone, a tablet and a laptop, one after the other or at the same time.

## GaN chargers

Several chargers here use GaN, gallium nitride, instead of silicon. GaN parts run cooler and smaller, so a 65 W GaN charger is about the size of an old 20 W phone charger. Some have a foldable plug or a built-in retractable cable, which makes them ideal for travel. If you carry a laptop and a phone, 1 GaN charger can replace 2 bricks.

## Cables and connectors

Make sure the cable matches both the charger and the phone. Type-C to Type-C cables suit recent Android phones, iPhone 15 and later, tablets and laptops. USB-A to Lightning cables suit older iPhones. The cable must also support the power you want: a 60 W or 100 W cable for a laptop, a 3 A cable for fast phone charging. Lengths of 1.2 m are the most practical.

## Power banks

## Questions and answers

### Will a 65 W charger damage my phone?

No. A phone draws only the power it supports, so a 65 W charger delivers perhaps 20 to 30 W to a phone and more to a laptop. Modern chargers and phones negotiate the speed automatically through USB Power Delivery. Use a good cable rated for the power you need, and avoid damaged or very cheap cables.

### Which cable do I need for my iPhone?

It depends on the model. The iPhone 15 and later use a Type-C port, so a Type-C to Type-C cable works with a Type-C charger. Older iPhones use Lightning, so you need a Type-C to Lightning or USB-A to Lightning cable. The product title states both ends of every cable we sell.
