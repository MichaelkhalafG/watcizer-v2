A watch on a woman's wrist does two jobs at once: it tells the time, and it is jewellery. That is why choosing one feels personal — it has to suit the way you dress, the jewellery you already love, and the occasions you will wear it for. Here is a simple guide to the styles, the sizes, and the small details that make a watch look like yours.

## The main styles

- **The dress watch.** A small, slim case, a clean dial, a fine bracelet or a slim leather strap. It sits under a sleeve and goes with everything from a blouse to an evening dress. Our [dress watches](/subtypes/dress) are the place to start.
- **The jewellery watch.** Crystals on the bezel, a mother-of-pearl dial, a bracelet that looks like a bangle. It is made to be seen, and it often replaces a bracelet altogether.
- **The everyday casual watch.** A slightly larger case, a steel bracelet or a leather strap, easy to read. It suits work, weekends and errands alike. Browse our [casual watches](/subtypes/casual).
- **The sporty watch.** A larger, often chronograph-style case, steel or rubber. Many women choose it precisely because it is bold.

## Choosing the size

Women's watches have traditionally been smaller, but today there is no single rule — the right size is the one that suits your wrist and your style.

- **For a delicate, classic look,** a small case sits elegantly on the wrist.
- **For an everyday watch,** a medium case is easier to read at a glance.
- **For a statement,** a larger case — the "boyfriend" style — is a deliberate, confident choice.

A practical check: the lugs, where the bracelet attaches, should not extend past the edges of your wrist. If they do, the watch is too large for that wrist.

## Bracelet, leather or something in between

- **A steel bracelet** — silver-tone, gold-tone or two-tone — is the most versatile, and the easiest to live with. It is sized by removing links, and **bracelet sizing is free within the warranty**.
- **A mesh bracelet** is fine, flexible and very comfortable on the wrist.
- **A leather strap** is softer and warmer, lovely in cooler months, and best kept away from water and perfume.

## Wearing a watch with jewellery

A watch and jewellery can work beautifully together with a few simple rules:

- **Match the metal tones.** A gold-tone watch with gold jewellery, silver with silver. Two-tone watches make mixing easy.
- **Stack with intention.** A watch with one or two slim [bracelets](/subtypes/bracelets) on the same wrist looks elegant; too many can scratch the watch and crowd the wrist. Keep the bracelets on the side away from the crown.
- **Let one piece lead.** A jewellery watch is the statement — keep rings and earrings quieter around it. A simple watch lets bold jewellery shine.
- **Think about the bag too.** A watch, a bag and shoes in the same family of colours pull an outfit together. See our [bags](/subtypes/bags).

## One watch for everything, or one for each moment?

If you want one watch for every day, choose a medium size, a classic dial and a steel bracelet in the metal tone you wear most. It will go from the office to dinner without a second thought.

If you already have an everyday watch, a second one can be all about the occasion: a small, sparkling jewellery watch for evenings and weddings, or a bold sporty one for the weekend.

## Quartz or automatic?

Many women's watches run on a quartz movement, powered by a small battery: accurate, light, slim, and nothing to wind. You simply wear it, and have the battery changed every few years. An automatic watch is mechanical and winds itself as you move your wrist; it is usually a little thicker, and it stops if you leave it in a drawer for a while. If you want a watch you can forget about between occasions, quartz is the easier choice; if you love the idea of a small machine on your wrist, choose an automatic.

## Looking after it

- Take the watch off before applying perfume, cream or hair spray.
- Wipe it with a soft cloth after wearing.
- Keep it in its box or a soft pouch, apart from other jewellery that could scratch it.
- Keep a leather strap dry.
