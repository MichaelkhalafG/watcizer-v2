A watch is one of the few gifts that is worn every day and kept for years. That is exactly what makes choosing one feel like a risk: you want it to be theirs, not yours. The good news is that a few simple questions take most of the guesswork out of it.

## Start with how they live, not with the watch

Before you look at a single model, think about the person's day.

- **Do they wear a suit, or trainers?** Someone in an office most days will reach for a slim, clean dress watch. Someone always on the move will wear a sturdy sports watch that does not mind a knock.
- **What do they wear now?** If they already have a watch, look at it. Its size, its colour and whether it sits on a bracelet or a strap tell you more than any guide.
- **Gold, silver or something else?** Look at the jewellery they wear — a ring, a bracelet, glasses frames. A watch whose metal matches what they already wear will feel like it belongs.

## Pick a style that will last

Trends come and go; a gift watch should not. Three styles almost never miss:

- **The dress watch.** A clean dial, slim case, leather strap or fine bracelet. Perfect for someone who dresses up. Browse our [dress watches](/subtypes/dress).
- **The sports watch.** Steel, often a bracelet, sometimes a chronograph, made to be worn hard. See our [sports watches](/subtypes/sport).
- **The everyday casual watch.** Somewhere in between — easy with jeans, fine at a dinner. Our [casual watches](/subtypes/casual) are a safe and versatile choice.

If you are unsure, the casual watch is the safest bet: it suits more occasions than the other two.

## Get the size roughly right

The size of the case changes everything about how a watch looks on the wrist. You do not need their exact measurements — a little observation is enough:

- **Look at the watch they wear now** and note its size from its product page or box, if you can.
- **A slim wrist** usually looks best with a smaller case; a broad wrist can carry a bigger one.
- **When in doubt, go a little smaller.** A watch that is slightly small looks elegant; one that is too big looks borrowed.

The bracelet can be sized after the gift is opened — so do not worry about the exact wrist size, only the case.

## Bracelet, leather or rubber?

- **A steel bracelet** is the most versatile and the easiest to live with — water, heat and daily wear do not bother it.
- **A leather strap** is warmer and dressier, and best kept away from water.
- **A rubber or silicone strap** suits sport, the beach and the gym.

If they will wear the watch every day, a bracelet is rarely the wrong choice.

## Set a budget — and stick to it

A good watch does not have to be the most expensive one. Decide what you want to spend first, then choose the best watch within it. - **The warranty.** Every watch we sell comes with a warranty of **up to 3 years, depending on the watch**, a warranty card and a sales invoice. Keep the invoice somewhere safe and hand over the card with the gift.
- **The exchange.** If the size or the style is not quite right, you can **exchange or return it within 14 days**, as long as it is in its original, unopened state with the seals intact (return shipping is paid by the customer). So keep the packaging closed until the gift is opened — and keep the receipt.

## A quick gift checklist

1. Their style: dress, sport or casual.
2. Their metal: gold, silver or another tone.
3. The size: roughly that of the watch they wear, or a little smaller.
4. The bracelet or strap that fits their day.
5. Your budget, decided before you browse.
6. The box, the warranty card and the invoice, kept safe.
