A good watch is built to last for years, and most of what keeps it that way is simple: a little care every day, and the right service at the right moment. This guide covers the everyday habits, the battery, the bracelet, and what our service does for you — free, within the warranty, for the two things owners need most.

## Everyday habits that make a difference

Most wear on a watch comes from ordinary life, not accidents. A few habits go a long way:

- **Take it off for the rough jobs.** Heavy lifting, sports with impacts, and work with tools are hard on the crystal, the case and the movement.
- **Keep it away from strong magnets.** Speakers, laptop and tablet covers with magnetic clasps, and some phone cases can affect a mechanical movement and make it run fast. Do not store the watch on top of them.
- **Mind the crown.** Set the time with the watch off your wrist, and push the crown back in — or screw it down, if it screws — every time. A crown left out is the easiest way for dust and water to get in.
- **Clean it gently.** A soft, dry cloth for the case and crystal. A steel bracelet can be cleaned with a soft brush and a little water if the watch is water resistant enough; dry it well. Keep a leather strap away from water, perfume and creams.
- **Store it sensibly.** In its box or a soft pouch, away from direct sun and humidity, and apart from other watches and jewellery that could scratch it.

## The battery: quartz watches

A quartz watch runs on a small battery. When it begins to run down, many quartz watches give a sign — the seconds hand may start jumping several seconds at a time — and then the watch stops. When that happens, have the battery changed promptly: an exhausted battery left inside a watch for a long time can leak and damage the movement.

A battery change is more than swapping a cell. The caseback is opened, so the seal has to be seated again properly, which is what keeps the watch's water resistance intact. That is why it is worth having it done by people who know the watch.

## Automatic watches: no battery, but a little attention

An automatic watch has no battery. It is wound by a weighted rotor as you move your wrist, so it keeps running as long as you wear it. If you leave it off for a while it will stop; wind it gently through the crown, or simply wear it again, and set the time.

If you own several watches, an automatic you wear only occasionally will need setting each time — that is normal, not a fault.

## The bracelet: sizing and comfort

A bracelet that fits well sits comfortably on the wrist bone without sliding down over the hand, and leaves room for a finger underneath. Wrists change a little with the time of day and the weather, so a fit that is snug in the morning can feel tight in the summer heat.

A steel bracelet is sized by adding or removing links, and fine-tuned with the micro-adjustment holes in the clasp where the bracelet has them. Removing links needs the right tool and some care: pins and screws differ from one bracelet to another, and a link put back the wrong way can loosen.

## When to bring your watch in

- The seconds hand jumps several seconds at a time, or the watch has stopped.
- There is mist or moisture under the crystal.
- The crown feels loose, or will not screw down or push in.
- The watch gains or loses more time than usual.
- The bracelet is too loose or too tight.
