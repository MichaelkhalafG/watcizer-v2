Every watch has an engine, the movement, and the first real choice you make when you buy one is which kind of engine you want. For most buyers it comes down to two: **quartz** and **automatic**. Both tell the time well; they just do it in completely different ways, and they ask different things of you. Here is what you need to know to choose.

## How a quartz watch keeps time

A quartz watch is run by a battery and a tiny piece of quartz crystal. Quartz has a useful property: when an electric current passes through it, it vibrates at a very steady rate. In a watch, the crystal is cut so that it vibrates 32,768 times a second. A small circuit counts those vibrations and, every time it has counted 32,768 of them, sends one pulse to a motor that moves the seconds hand forward. That is why the seconds hand of most quartz watches moves in single, distinct ticks.

What this means for you:

- **Accuracy.** Because the vibration is so regular, a quartz watch is very accurate for everyday use, and stays that way with no attention from you.
- **No winding.** Put it down for a week, a month, longer — it is still running when you pick it up.
- **A battery to change.** Every few years the battery runs down and needs replacing. At Watchizer, battery replacement is free within the warranty period.
- **Slim cases and lighter weight.** A quartz movement is compact, which is why quartz watches can be thin and light.

## How an automatic watch keeps time

An automatic watch is mechanical. Instead of a battery, it stores energy in a coiled spring, the mainspring, and releases it through a train of gears at a regulated pace. What makes it "automatic" is a weighted rotor inside the case: as you move your wrist through the day, the rotor swings and winds the mainspring for you. Turn an automatic watch over, and on many models a transparent caseback lets you watch the rotor and the movement at work.

What this means for you:

- **No battery.** As long as you wear it, it winds itself.
- **It needs wearing.** Leave it in a drawer and it will eventually stop, once the energy in the mainspring runs out. Wind it by hand through the crown, or simply wear it, and it starts again; you will then need to set the time.
- **The sweep.** The seconds hand of an automatic moves in a smooth glide rather than single ticks — for many people, part of the appeal.
- **Craft.** An automatic movement is a small machine of many parts working together. Many watch lovers choose automatics for exactly that.

## So which one should you choose?

There is no wrong answer, only the one that fits how you live.

**Choose quartz if** you want a watch you can forget about, you rotate between several watches, you prefer a slimmer case, or you want the most practical everyday accuracy. A quartz chronograph — such as the Hugo Boss Grand Prix 1513477 in our [Hugo Boss collection](/brand/hugo-boss) — gives you stopwatch functions with none of the upkeep.

**Choose automatic if** you enjoy the idea of a watch that runs on your own movement, you wear one watch most days, and you value mechanical craft. Browse our [automatic watches](/subtypes/automatic), or shop by brand, for example [Tissot](/brand/tissot), and check the movement on each product page.

A few practical points help settle it:

- **How often will you wear it?** Daily wear suits an automatic perfectly. Occasional wear suits quartz.
- **Do you mind setting the time?** If an automatic sits unworn and stops, you will set it again before wearing it.
- **What matters more: the look or the mechanism?** Both kinds come in every style, from dress watches to sports watches, so you rarely have to give up a design to get the movement you want.
